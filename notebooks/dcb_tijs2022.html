DCB benchmark: Tijs et al. (2022), AS4D/PEKK-FC#

        1. Tijs, S. Abdel-Monsef, J. Renart, A. Turon and C. Bisagni, Characterization and analysis of the interlaminar behavior of thermoplastic composites considering fiber bridging and R-curve effects, Composites Part A 162 (2022) 107101, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compositesa.2022.107101.

Mode I cases of the paper (the ENF, CELS and MMB50 cases are mode II or mixed mode and are not included):

  • DCB of AS4D/PEKK-FC, 30 unidirectional plies of 0.14 mm (\(2h = 4.2\) mm, arms of 2.1 mm), width 25 mm, specimens 225 mm long;

  • Table 1: \(E_{11}\) = 138.3 GPa (tension) / 128.0 GPa (compression), \(E_{22}\) = 10.4 / 11.5 GPa, \(G_{12} = G_{13}\) = 5.19 GPa, \(\nu_{12}\) = 0.316, matrix tensile strength \(Y_T\) = 87 MPa; the analyses use the average of the tensile and compressive moduli;

  • tests at the University of Girona (ISO 15024, side-clamped hinges, \(a_0\) = 48 mm) and at TU Delft (ASTM D5528, loading blocks, \(a_0\) = 40 mm);

  • fracture toughness: initiation about 0.7 N/mm, propagation 1.12 N/mm (with fibre bridging), about 0.95 N/mm without the contribution of fibre bridging (Fig. 15);

  • ABAQUS/Standard, 2D plane strain CPE4I elements with COH2D4 cohesive elements, mesh of 0.1 mm, \(K = 10^6\) N/mm\(^3\), beam length \(L\) = 100 mm and \(a_0\) = 40/48 mm (Table 2). Fig. 25a compares the test with a bilinear law (“Numerical: linear”, \(G_{Ic}\) = 1.12 N/mm, \(\tau^0\) = 87 MPa) and a tabular multilinear law fitted to the test (“Numerical: tabular”).

This DCB, with \(a_0\) = 48 mm and the length \(L\) = 100 mm of the FE model, is the reference DCB of the convergence study of panels; the MSc thesis of D’Souza (2024) uses it with a length of 65 mm.

Conventions. The displacement of Figs. 12, 15 and 25 is the opening \(\delta\) at the load line.

Digitised data. The curves were read from Figs. 15a and 25a by eye, with an estimated uncertainty of \(\pm 0.2\) mm and \(\pm 3\) N. The test “DCB (2)” of Fig. 15a is taken with \(a_0\) = 40 mm (TU Delft): its initial compliance, about 0.030 mm/N, corresponds to \(a_0 \approx 42\) mm with the compliance calibration of the autoclave UD specimens of Tijs (2023, Table 5.4.1), while “DCB (1)” is the test of Fig. 12 with \(a_0\) = 48 mm.

panels model#

The DCB is modelled with dcb_utils.solve_dcb: CLPT panels, far end clamped, arm tips opened symmetrically to \(\pm\delta/2\) by prescribed displacements, a cohesive domain (SB_TSL, bilinear law) ahead of the initial crack front and a perfect bond (SB) over the rest of the bonded region. The load is the reaction of the prescribed displacement. When the bonded strip is narrower than the arms (bond_width), the integration points outside the strip start fully damaged.

The discretisation follows the rules of the convergence study (convergence study, dcb_utils.discretize): \(m_{tsl} = 1.25\,\lambda L_{tsl}\) terms along \(x\) of the cohesive domain (at most 25), with \(\lambda = (k_o/2D_{11})^{1/4}\), and \(n_x = 10\,L_{tsl}/l_{cz}\) Gauss points, i.e. 10 points per cohesive zone length \(l_{cz} = 0.88\,E_2 G_{Ic}/\tau_o^2\); the loads are converged to 0.1% from \(m_{tsl} = 1.0\,\lambda L_{tsl}\) and to 0.02% with 10 points per \(l_{cz}\). The cohesive domain covers the crack growth up to the last opening plus a margin of \(3 l_{cz} + 8/\lambda\). The penalty stiffness is \(k_o = 10^5\) N/mm\(^3\) instead of the \(10^6\) N/mm\(^3\) of the references: the peak load changes by 0.2% between both values in the Turon et al. (2007) case (see dcb_turon2007.ipynb).

def setup(case, k_o=1.e5, growth_max=None, n_steps=60):
    """Cohesive domain, discretisation and openings of a panels run"""
    c = dict(case)
    c['k_o'] = k_o
    lam, lcz, L_max = du.resolution(c)
    margin = du.margin(lam, lcz)
    L_avail = c['L'] - c['a0']
    growth = min(L_max, L_avail) - margin
    if growth_max is not None:
        growth = min(growth, growth_max)
    L_tsl = min(growth + margin, L_avail)
    c = du.discretize(c, L_tsl)
    # opening when the crack front of panels reaches a0 + growth, LEFM of
    # the model: crack front + process zone l_cz + 1/lambda (Kanninen 1973)
    delta_max = du.opening_at_crack_length(c, c['a0'] + growth, lam, lcz)
    openings = np.linspace(delta_max/n_steps, delta_max, n_steps)
    print(f"{case.get('label', '')}: lambda={lam:.3f} 1/mm, lcz={lcz:.2f} mm, "
          f"L_tsl={c['L_tsl']:.1f} mm, m_tsl={c['m_tsl']}, nx={c['nx']}, "
          f"delta_max={delta_max:.2f} mm")
    return c, openings


def run(key, case, openings, path):
    if ONLY is not None and key not in ONLY:
        return None
    return du.run_or_load(case, openings, path, rerun=RERUN)


def compliance_case(case):
    """Linear elastic model to evaluate the compliance"""
    c = dict(case)
    # linear analysis: m_tsl = 15 is 0.90 lambda L_tsl, enough for the
    # compliance, below the rules of the nonlinear runs (dcb_utils.discretize)
    c.update(k_o=1.e5, tau_o=87., L_tsl=20., m_tsl=15, nx=60)
    return c


def curve(res):
    return np.concatenate(([0.], res['delta'])), np.concatenate(([0.], res['P']))


def plot_compare(rows, title, xmax=None):
    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 4.5))
    style = {}
    for k in rows:
        if k.startswith('panels'):
            style[k] = dict(lw=2, color='k') if '1e6' not in k else dict(lw=1.5, color='0.5')
        elif 'LEFM' in k:
            style[k] = dict(ls='--', lw=1)
        elif 'test' in k.lower() or 'experiment' in k.lower():
            style[k] = dict(ls='none', marker='o', mfc='none', ms=4)
        else:
            style[k] = dict(marker='s', ms=3)
    du.plot_curves(ax, rows, style)
    if xmax:
        ax.set_xlim(0, xmax)
    ax.set_title(title)
    plt.show()


def report(key, case, res):
    print(f"panels {key}: m_tsl={case['m_tsl']}, nx={case['nx']}, {len(res['delta'])} openings, "
          f"{res['failed']} bisections, crack growth {res['a'][-1] - case['a0']:.1f} mm")

Comparison#

The tables give, for every curve, the initial stiffness \(K_0\), the peak load \(P_{max}\) and the opening \(\delta\) at the peak, and the relative differences of each row to the reference named in the column, \((x - x_{ref})/x_{ref}\).

def compare(key, exp_key, lefm_G):
    """FE curves of the paper, test, panels and LEFM with the toughnesses ``lefm_G``"""
    fe = fe_cases[key]
    case, res = runs[key]
    rows = dict(fe['curves'])
    rows.update(exp_tests[exp_key]['curves'])
    rows[f"panels, G_Ic = {case['G1c']} N/mm"] = curve(res)
    for G in lefm_G:
        rows[f'LEFM, corrected beam theory, {G} N/mm'] = du.lefm_curve(dict(fe, G1c=G), fe['a0'] + 60, corrected=True)
    refs = [k for k in rows if 'FE' in k][:1] + list(exp_tests[exp_key]['curves'])[:1]
    display(Markdown(f"### {case['label']}\n\n" + du.markdown_table(rows, ref=refs)))
    plot_compare(rows, case['label'], xmax=25)
    report(key, case, res)

Discussion#

  • Same bilinear law, :math:`a_0` = 48 mm. panels predicts 166.2 N at an opening of 5.66 mm. The FE model of the paper with the same bilinear law (\(G_{Ic}\) = 1.12 N/mm, \(\tau^0\) = 87 MPa) peaks at about 167 N at about 5.7 mm (Fig. 25a): the difference is 0.5% on the load and 0.7% on the opening, the closest comparison of all the validation cases, since the geometry, the material and the cohesive law are the same. The corrected beam theory gives 164.5 N, 1.1% below panels.

  • Test with R-curve. The test peaks at about 142 N: panels is 17% above it, and the FE model of the paper with the same law 18%. The paper explains the difference by the R-curve: the initiation toughness is about 0.7 N/mm and the propagation toughness of 1.12 N/mm is only reached after fibre bridging develops. The tabular multilinear law fitted to the test reproduces it (about 141 N), while a bilinear law with the propagation toughness cannot, which is also the conclusion of the paper. A multilinear law is not available in panels.

  • :math:`a_0` = 40 mm. No FE curve is given for this crack length. The test DCB (2) peaks at about 161 N and then follows the LEFM curve with 0.95 N/mm (Fig. 15a), the propagation toughness without the contribution of fibre bridging, and panels is run with this toughness: it predicts 182.1 N at 3.71 mm, 1.6% above the corrected beam theory with 0.95 N/mm (179.1 N) and 13% above the test, whose peak lies between the LEFM curves with 0.7 and 0.95 N/mm, again reflecting the R-curve.

  • Initial stiffness. panels is 9% stiffer than the FE model and 14-17% stiffer than the corrected beam theory, see dcb_lecinana2023.ipynb and dcb_tijs2023_phd.ipynb for the measured compliances. The tests are more compliant than all models, which the compliance calibration of these specimens attributes to a crack length correction of 9.18 mm (Tijs 2023, Table 5.4.1), 2.4 times the \(\chi h\) = 3.8 mm of the corrected beam theory.

  • Robustness. Both runs converged at every opening without bisection, with 10.1 mm (\(a_0\) = 48 mm) and 10.6 mm (\(a_0\) = 40 mm) of crack growth.
