Third-order shear deformation theory with a laminate offset: connections and reference surface#

With Reddy’s third-order shear deformation theory (TSDT) and a laminate offset \(d\), the mid-surface of the laminate is at \(z = d\) from the reference surface \(z = 0\) of the displacements, and the in-plane displacement is \(u(z) = u + z \phi_x - c_1 [(z - d)^3 + d^3] (\phi_x + w_{,x})\), \(c_1 = 4/(3h^2)\), so that the transverse shear strain vanishes at both faces \(z = d \pm h/2\), see Third-order shear deformation theory with a laminate offset. The rotations of the normal at the reference surface are

\[\theta_x = \phi_x - \frac{4 d^2}{h^2} (\phi_x + w_{,x}), \qquad \theta_y = \phi_y - \frac{4 d^2}{h^2} (\phi_y + w_{,y}),\]

and the connections of `MultiDomain <../multidomain.rst>`__ tie \(u, v, w, \theta_x, \theta_y\) at \(z = 0\), the surface shared by the connected panels. This notebook checks, with the models of the test modules tests/multidomain/test_tsdt_offset_connections.py and tests/tests_shell/test_tsdt_offset.py:

  1. two panels with different offsets, connected exactly: the rotations at the reference surface are continuous across the connection, those at the mid-surfaces are not;

  2. two panels with the same offset reproduce the single panel, with both connection methods;

  3. a free homogeneous plate is the same structure for any reference surface: its frequencies do not depend on \(d\).

Reference. J. N. Reddy, Mechanics of Laminated Composite Plates and Shells: Theory and Analysis, 2nd ed., CRC Press, 2003, doi:10.1201/b12409.

def table(headers, rows):
    lines = ['| ' + ' | '.join(headers) + ' |', '|' + '---|'*len(headers)]
    lines += ['| ' + ' | '.join(str(v) for v in row) + ' |' for row in rows]
    display(Markdown('\n'.join(lines)))


def frequencies(K, M, k):
    K, _ = remove_null_cols(K, silent=True)
    M, _ = remove_null_cols(M, silent=True)
    return np.sqrt(eigh(K.toarray(), M.toarray(), eigvals_only=True, subset_by_index=[0, k - 1]))

1. Continuity of the rotations at the reference surface#

Two simply supported plates, \(0.4 \times 1\) and \(0.6 \times 1\), \(h = 0.1\), cross-ply \([0/90/0/90]\) with \(E_1/E_2 = 10\), \(10 \times 10\) terms, connected along \(x = 0.4\) by 'SSxcte' with the null-space method, under a uniform pressure of \(-10^4\) Pa. The first plate has no offset, \(d_1 = 0\), the second \(d_2 = 0.3h\), \(h/2\) or \(-h/2\). The rotations are evaluated on both sides of the connection at seven points along \(y\), from the fields \(\phi_x\), \(\phi_y\) and the transverse shear strains \(\gamma_{xz} = \phi_x + w_{,x}\), \(\gamma_{yz} = \phi_y + w_{,y}\) of `Shell.strain <../shell.rst>`__. Jumps relative to the largest \(|\theta_x|\).

2. Two panels with the same offset against the single panel#

The same plates, both with \(d = 0.3h\), \(14 \times 14\) terms, against the single plate with \(18 \times 18\) terms: first six natural frequencies, with the penalty method (constants of calc_kt_kr multiplied by 1000) and with the null-space method. Relative difference \(\omega/\omega_{single} - 1\).

3. Free plate: independence of the reference surface#

A free homogeneous aluminium plate, \(0.6 \times 0.4\) m, \(h = 20\) mm, \(E = 70\) GPa, \(\nu = 0.3\), \(8 \times 7\) terms. With free edges the Ritz spaces of the different offsets coincide, the structure is the same, and so must be the natural frequencies (the six rigid-body modes are excluded).

Discussion#

The rotations of the connected panels are continuous at their common reference surface to round-off, panels with the same offset reproduce the single panel to the convergence of the approximation (within \(10^{-4}\) here, with both methods), and the frequencies of the free plate do not depend on the reference surface, to round-off.
