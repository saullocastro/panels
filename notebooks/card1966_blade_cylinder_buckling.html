Axial buckling of cylinders with integral blade stringers: Card and Jones (1966)#

    1. Card and R. M. Jones, Experimental and theoretical results for buckling of eccentrically stiffened cylinders, NASA TN D-3639, October 1966 (NTRS 19660027866).

Card and Jones tested in axial compression twelve longitudinally stiffened aluminium cylinders, in pairs with the stiffeners outside (external) or inside (internal) the skin. Cylinders 1 to 4 have integral rectangular (blade) stringers machined from 3/8 in plate, the configuration of this notebook; cylinders 5 to 12 have riveted Z stringers. For each cylinder their Table I gives the experimental buckling load and number of circumferential waves, and the buckling load of a smeared-stiffener theory for clamped and for simply supported ends (Galerkin solutions with a membrane pre-buckling state, Appendix A and Block, Card and Mikulas, NASA TN D-2960, 1965). The externally stiffened cylinders carried more than twice the load of the internally stiffened ones.

This notebook models cylinders 1 to 4 with panels, with the skin as curved panels between the stringers and each stringer as a flat plate strip, a MultiDomain assembly, and compares the linear buckling loads with the test and with the smeared theory. The same assembly is used for the vibration tests of Sewall and Naumann (1968) in sewall1968_blade_cylinder_vibration.ipynb, through the module blade_cylinder_utils.py.

Datum of the stringer height. In Fig. 2(b) (p. 6) the extension lines of the 0.33 in dimension run from the tip of the stringer to the surface of the skin opposite the stringer: 0.33 in is the total depth of the section, skin included. The stringer stands \(0.33 - t\) above the skin surface, and its plate in the model is this drawn rectangle, from the skin surface to the tip, of width \(h_b = 0.33 - t\) in. The bfsccylinder implementation of this case (bfsccylinder/notebooks/card1966_stiffened_cylinder_buckling.ipynb) reads the drawing in the same way, but its stringer plate starts at the middle surface of the skin, \(0.33 - t/2\) in, overlapping half of the skin thickness (column of the second table below).

Two checks of this reading follow. (1) The area: the skin, \(2\pi R t\), plus 60 rectangles \(0.097 \times (0.33 - t)\) in with two fillets each, \(2 r_F^2 (1 - \pi/4)\), plus the four doubler plates of the two butt joints, \(0.70 \times 0.020\) in each (p. 5), is 3 % to 6 % below the area \(A_t\) of Table I, which was determined by weighing (p. 5; the rivets are not counted; 5 % to 7 % without the doublers, as in the bfsccylinder notebook). A stringer 0.33 in high above the skin surface would match \(A_t\) within 2 %, but (2) the theory of Card and Jones decides: their simply supported \(\bar{N}_s\) is the closed-form solution of Block, Card and Mikulas (1965, NASA TN D-2960, Eq. (15)) for stringers only, with the membrane pre-buckling state and the torsional stiffness \(G_s J_s\) of the stringers (\(J\) of a rectangle, symbol list of Card and Jones, p. 4). Evaluated below with the section of Fig. 2(b), it reproduces the four values \(\bar{N}_s\) of Table I within 0.2 %, and with a stringer 0.33 in high above the skin surface it is up to 10 % higher: Card and Jones used the drawn section, not \(A_t\), and so does the model.

def torsion_J(ts, h):
    # J of a rectangle ts x h (Card and Jones 1966, symbol list, p. 4)
    return ts**3*h*(1/3 - 64/np.pi**5*ts/h*np.tanh(np.pi*h/(2*ts)))


def section(t, side, hs, z0, plate=False, width=WIDTH):
    # stringer rectangle width x hs starting at z0 from the skin middle surface:
    # area, eccentricity, moment of inertia about the skin middle surface, J of the
    # rectangle or, with plate=True, of the Kirchhoff plate strip (4 D66 hs = G J)
    A, zc = width*hs, z0 + hs/2
    J = hs*width**3/3 if plate else torsion_J(width, hs)
    return A, (1 if side == 'external' else -1)*zc, width*hs**3/12 + A*zc**2, J


def nx_ss(t, L, As, zs, Is, J, nmax=40, mmax=6, nstiff=NSTIFF):
    # Block, Card and Mikulas (1965), Eq. (15), stringers only, isotropic skin and
    # stringers, simply supported, membrane pre-buckling state; minimum over (m, n), lb/in
    E_, G_ = E_KSI*1e3, E_KSI*1e3/(2*(1 + NU))
    ds = 2*np.pi*R_IN/nstiff
    Ex, D = E_*t/(1 - NU**2), E_*t**3/(12*(1 - NU**2))
    best = (np.inf, None)
    for m in range(1, mmax + 1):
        a = m*np.pi/L
        for n in range(nmax + 1):
            b = n/R_IN
            A11 = (Ex + E_*As/ds)*a**2 + G_*t*b**2
            A22 = Ex*b**2 + G_*t*a**2
            A12 = (NU*Ex + G_*t)*a*b
            A13 = NU*Ex/R_IN*a + E_*As*zs/ds*a**3
            A23 = Ex*b/R_IN
            A33 = D*(a**2 + b**2)**2 + E_*Is/ds*a**4 + G_*J/ds*a**2*b**2 + Ex/R_IN**2
            den = A11*A22 - A12**2
            N = (A33 + A23*(A12*A13 - A11*A23)/den + A13*(A12*A23 - A22*A13)/den)/a**2
            if N < best[0]:
                best = (N, (m, n))
    return best

Model#

Discrete stringers (see bu.build_cylinder). The skin between two stringers is a curved panel of the Sanders-Koiter cylindrical shell model (cylshell_clpt_sanders) and each stringer a flat plate strip normal to the skin, of thickness \(t_b = 0.097\) in and width \(h_b = 0.33 - t\) in, from the skin surface to the stringer tip (the drawn rectangle of Fig. 2(b), see above), connected to the skin surface with the base-flange connection 'BFycte' (face='lower', the inner surface, for the internal stringers, face='upper' for the external ones; displacements and rotation about the stringer axis continuous) and the skin panels to each other with 'SSycte', both imposed exactly with conn_method='null-space' (Castro and Donadon 2017, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compstruct.2016.10.026). The blade stands inside or outside the skin according to the face of the skin to which it is connected; the eccentricity of the stringers, the distance \(\bar{z} = (t + h_b)/2\) of their centroid from the middle surface of the skin in the smeared theory, follows from this geometry. The plate strips do not represent the two fillets, 1.3 % of the area of the drawn stringer, \(0.097 (0.33 - t) + 2 r_F^2 (1 - \pi/4)\) (derived). The plate has the area, centroid and moment of inertia of the drawn rectangle and, as a Kirchhoff plate strip, the twisting stiffness \(4 D_{66} h_b = G h_b t_b^3/3\), 25 % above \(G J\) of the drawn rectangle with the \(J\) of Card and Jones (cylinder 4, first table of the section Discrete against smeared stringers). Eq. (15) for the stringer of the model, with this twisting stiffness, is 2.5 % to 4.1 % above \(\bar{N}_s\) of Table I, and for the plate from the middle surface of the skin of the bfsccylinder implementation 3.6 % to 6.8 % above (table above).

The stringers carry about half of the axial load, and the general instability of a stringer-stiffened cylinder bends them in their own plane, radially. The geometric stiffness of a plate model with the von Kármán strains contains the terms of the out-of-plane displacement only, \(N_{xx} w_{,x}^2\), and would miss the destabilizing work of the stringer load \(N_{xx} v_{,x}^2\) on the in-plane (radial) deflection of the blade; the blades are therefore modelled with plate_clpt_gl, whose Green-Lagrange membrane strains give the in-plane terms of the geometric stiffness too, with the same linear stiffness as plate_clpt_donnell.

Symmetry. The buckling mode of a cylinder with 60 identical, equally spaced stringers is close to \(w \propto \cos n\theta\) times an axial shape; it is symmetric about two planes \(\theta = 0\) and \(\theta = \pi\) through the middle of a bay, and half of the cylinder, with \(v = w_{,y} = 0\) on these planes, contains it for every \(n\) (the half model is compared with the full cylinder in the notebook of Sewall and Naumann).

Separating the differences at the spacing of the tests. For the same section, the discrete model differs from Eq. (15) in three respects, each of which bu.buckling_membrane can switch off:

  • the kinematics of the skin: Sanders-Koiter (cylshell_clpt_sanders) in the model, “the usual Donnell-type assumptions” in the smeared theory (Block, Card and Mikulas 1965, p. 5); skin_model='cylshell_clpt_donnell' gives the Donnell skin;

  • the load term of the stringers: in Eq. (9) of Block, Card and Mikulas (p. 8) the change in potential energy of the load is \(\bar{N}_x w_{,x}^2\), with \(\bar{N}_x\) “obtained by considering the shell and stringers to be loaded with a uniform normal stress”, so that the stringer load acts on the radial displacement only. The blades of plate_clpt_gl carry their load \(N_{xx}\) on the three displacements of the blade: the radial one (v of the blade), the axial one, and the out-of-plane one (w of the blade), which is the circumferential displacement of the stringer section: the circumferential displacement of the skin at the root plus the rotation of the section times the distance from the skin. blade_lateral_kG=False removes this last term (“lateral term” below) from the geometric stiffness of the blades;

  • the twisting of the stringers: Eq. (6) of Block, Card and Mikulas (p. 7) adds the energy \(G_s J_s w_{,xy}^2/d\) “as an approximation”, for a stringer that “twists in a fashion so that its angle of twist is equal to the angle of twist of the shell”, and averages it over the circumference, “hence, discreteness of the stringers is ignored” (p. 7); each blade of the model twists as a plate strip whose root follows the rotation of the skin. blade_D66_factor=0 removes the twisting stiffness of the blades; these models are compared with Eq. (15) with \(J = 0\).

The simply supported ends of the blades hold only their radial displacement; bc='SSW' also holds their circumferential displacement along the end edges, as the stringer ends of the bfsccylinder implementation do. Each model is computed with the two finest numbers of terms of the convergence study.

The twisting part grows with the spacing: the Donnell model without the lateral term is 0.2 % below Eq. (15) at a quarter of the spacing of the tests, 0.3 % at a third, 0.5 % at half, 1.0 % at two thirds, 2.6 % at their spacing, 8.2 % at 1.5 times and 20.7 % at twice the spacing, where the twisting stiffness of the thick blade (\(t_b/h_b = 0.64\)) doubles the smeared load (1184.6 against 601.8 lb/in with \(J = 0\)). The model of this notebook is therefore 5.8 % to 6.2 % below Eq. (15) for spacings up to two thirds of that of the tests, 7.5 % at the spacing of the tests, 12.3 % at 1.5 times and 23.7 % at twice. In the bfsccylinder implementation of this study (bfsccylinder/notebooks/card1966_stiffened_cylinder_buckling.ipynb, same cylinder and spacings, its stringer plate from the middle surface of the skin, compared with Eq. (15) for that plate) the model without twisting stiffness, with the Donnell skin and without the lateral term is 0.2 % to 0.4 % above Eq. (15) at every spacing (the change of its last mesh refinement is about 0.5 %), the lateral term is -2.7 % to -3.8 %, the Sanders skin -2.0 % to -3.2 %, and the twisting part -0.3 % at the spacing of the tests (its stringer ends are held circumferentially), -4.7 % at 1.5 times and -18 % at twice; its model is 6.3 % below Eq. (15) for its section at the spacing of the tests (722.7 against 771.2 lb/in) and 5.4 % to 5.6 % below at the closer spacings.

Discussion#

  • Convergence. With the last two numbers of terms the critical loads change by less than 0.15 %. With the coarsest model the clamped analysis with the linear pre-buckling state of the long cylinders 1 and 2 (\(L/R = 4\)) has too few axial terms for the boundary layers at the ends and is 12 % and 16 % low.

  • Section of the stringers. Fig. 2(b) (p. 6) dimensions the total depth of the section, 0.33 in from the stringer tip to the skin surface opposite the stringer; the plate strips of the model are the drawn rectangles, from the skin surface to the tip, \(h_b = 0.33 - t\) in, without the fillets. With this section the simply supported smeared solution of Block, Card and Mikulas (1965, Eq. (15)) reproduces the four \(\bar{N}_s\) of Card and Jones within 0.2 % (table of the section Test data): their theory used the drawn section. The area \(A_t\) of Table I, by weighing, is 3 % to 6 % larger than this section with the doublers of the butt joints; the report does not explain the difference (the rivets of the joints are not counted), which is not part of the theory and is not modelled. With a stringer 0.33 in high above the skin surface Eq. (15) would be up to 10 % above \(\bar{N}_s\), most for the external stringers, whose eccentricity raises the load most.

  • Smeared theory. With the assumptions of Card and Jones (membrane pre-buckling state, clamped or simply supported ends), panels is 1.1 % to 3.2 % below their clamped theory \(\bar{N}_c\) for the four cylinders and 0.4 % to 5.5 % below the simply supported \(\bar{N}_s\). These theoretical values are for the drawn section of Fig. 2(b) with the \(J\) of a rectangle; the plate strip of the model has the same area, eccentricity and moment of inertia, and the twisting stiffness of a Kirchhoff plate strip, 25 % above, which raises Eq. (15) by 2.5 % to 4.1 %. Against Eq. (15) for the plate strip, the same section, the simply supported load of cylinder 4 is 7.5 % lower; the section Discrete against smeared stringers splits this into the Sanders skin against the Donnell skin of the theory (-1.5 %), the work of the blade load on the circumferential displacement of the stringer section, which Eq. (9) of Block, Card and Mikulas leaves out (-3.5 %), and the twisting of the discrete blades against the term \(G_s J_s w_{,xy}^2/d\) of the theory (-2.6 %, -1.6 % with the blade ends held circumferentially). With the same section, without twisting stiffness, with the Donnell skin and without that load term, the discrete model reproduces Eq. (15) within 0.2 % for stringer spacings from a quarter to twice that of the tests (\(b/\sqrt{R t}\) = 0.48 to 3.87); only the twisting part depends on the spacing, from -0.2 % at the closest spacing to -20.7 % at twice the spacing of the tests. The torsional stiffness \(G_s J_s\) raises the loads of these integral cylinders by 10 % to 20 % (Card and Jones, p. 11).

  • Other stringers, measured the same way. The notebooks weller1977_blade_cylinder_buckling.ipynb and peterson1959_blade_cylinder_buckling.ipynb give the gap as here: the simply supported analysis with the membrane pre-buckling state (Sanders-Koiter skin, plate_clpt_gl blades with the lateral term, blade ends held radially only) against Eq. (15) of Block, Card and Mikulas for the same plate strip with the same twisting stiffness, there the \(G J\) of the stringer of the source (\(D_{66}\) of the blades scaled to it) instead of that of the Kirchhoff plate strip. The external integral rectangular stringers of the shells AS-34 and AS-35 of Weller and Singer (T. Weller and J. Singer, Experimental studies on the buckling under axial compression of integrally stringer-stiffened circular cylindrical shells, J. Appl. Mech. 44(4) (1977) 721-730, https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3424163), at \(b/\sqrt{R t} = 1.6\), give -1.6 % and -1.7 % (-2.8 % and -2.9 % with the twisting stiffness of the plate strip); the riveted Z stringers of cylinders 5 to 12 of Card and Jones, at \(b/\sqrt{R t} = 1.57\) and with two different equivalent blades, -1.6 % to -2.8 % outside and -4.1 % to -6.7 % inside the skin. These blades stand on the skin only. The same Z stringers modelled as three plates, the attached flange bonded to the skin, the web and the free flange (peterson1959_blade_cylinder_buckling.ipynb), are instead 4.4 % to 17.1 % above Eq. (15) for the section of the Z itself, simply supported (4.5 % to 17.2 % in the bfsccylinder implementation); without the attached flange they are 6.0 % to 11.2 % below it, with the sign of the blades. The attached flange doubles the skin thickness over 29 % of the pitch, a circumferential stiffening of the skin that the smeared theory, which treats the stringer as a beam, does not contain: for a stringer with a flange on the skin the discrete model is above the smeared theory, and the gaps of the blades measure the discreteness of the stringer alone. The -7.5 % of the internal cylinder 4 at \(b/\sqrt{R t} = 1.93\) (-6.6 % in the test) is slightly larger than the gaps of the internal Z stringers; the external stringers lose 1.6 % to 2.8 %. Where the skin between the stringers buckles first (shells AS-27 of Weller and Singer, Koiter parameter \(\theta\) = 0.79 to 0.81, \(b/\sqrt{R t}\) = 2.7 to 2.8) the discrete model buckles locally, one half-wave of skin per bay, 21.0 % to 27.8 % below the general instability of Eq. (15), a mode that the smeared theory does not contain. The bfsccylinder implementations of these cases give -1.2 % (AS-34, AS-35), -1.4 % to -2.7 % and -3.8 % to -6.6 % (Z, external and internal) and -18.5 % to -25.6 % (AS-27), and -6.3 % for cylinder 4 (against Eq. (15) for its plate from the middle surface of the skin).

  • The bfsccylinder implementation of this case, with the same end conditions and its stringer plate from the middle surface of the skin (\(0.33 - t/2\) in, overlapping half of the skin thickness), gives critical loads above those of panels: panels is 0.9 % to 2.9 % below for the clamped analyses and 1.6 % to 3.8 % below for the simply supported analysis (table above), while bfsccylinder reproduces \(\bar{N}_c\) within 0.6 % and \(\bar{N}_s\) within 2.7 %. Most of this difference is the section: Eq. (15) for the plate from the middle surface is 1.1 % to 2.6 % above that for the plate of this notebook, most for the internal cylinders 2 and 4, as are the differences of the discrete loads. Both use Sanders-type kinematics for the skin and plate stringers tied to the skin with the rotation about the stringer axis continuous; they differ also in the discretization (Ritz series against BFSC finite elements with cyclic symmetry), in the geometric stiffness of the blades (plate_clpt_gl includes the term \(N_{xx} u_{,x}^2\)) and, for the simply supported ends, in the stringer ends, which bfsccylinder also restrains circumferentially. Each against Eq. (15) for its own plate strip of cylinder 4, the bfsccylinder model is 6.3 % below (722.7 against 771.2 lb/in) and this one 7.5 % (695.5 against 751.9 lb/in); both split the same way (section Discrete against smeared stringers), into -2.4 %, -3.7 % and -0.3 % there (Sanders skin, lateral term, twisting) and -1.5 %, -3.5 % and -2.6 % here (-1.6 % with the blade ends held circumferentially as there).

  • Pre-buckling state. The linear static pre-buckling state, with the bending of the skin near the restrained ends, raises the critical load by less than 0.1 % (cylinder 1, clamped) to 3 % (cylinder 3, simply supported) above the membrane state, in line with the conclusion of Card and Jones that a membrane pre-buckling state is sufficient for assessing the eccentricity effect (p. 11).

  • Edge conditions (Almroth 1966). B. O. Almroth, Influence of edge conditions on the stability of axially compressed cylindrical shells, AIAA Journal 4(1) (1966) 134-140, https://doi.org/10.2514/3.3396, computed the classical buckling load of axially compressed unstiffened isotropic cylinders (Donnell equations) for eight sets of edge conditions (Eq. (9), p. 135). The paper treats no stiffeners, discrete or smeared, and gives no value for the difference between them; it bears on the comparison with the smeared theory only through the edges of the skin. With the membrane pre-buckling state, simple supports with \(v = 0\), with either \(u = 0\) or \(N_x = 0\) (his cases S1 and S2), give a load “equal to or insignificantly higher than the classical buckling load” (p. 138), and only edges free in the circumferential direction, \(N_{xy} = 0\) (S3 and S4), lower it, to about one half (Table 2, p. 137). The simply supported analysis with the membrane state and Eq. (15) both hold \(v = w = 0\) with \(N_x = 0\) at the edges of the skin, so these edge conditions do not explain their difference, which for cylinder 4 is -7.5 % against Eq. (15) for the plate strip of the model (696 against 752 lb/in) and is split in the section Discrete against smeared stringers (bullet Smeared theory). With the bending at restrained edges in the pre-buckling state (his “rigorous prebuckling analysis”) Almroth’s loads fall to 0.80 to 0.88 of the classical value for these simple supports and to 0.86 to 0.93 for clamped edges (Table 2, modes of \(n\) = 8 to 88 waves), a reduction he finds too small to explain the difference between theory and tests, which he expects initial imperfections to explain (pp. 139-140); the stiffened cylinders here, buckling with \(n\) = 5 to 8, gain up to 3 % from the linear pre-buckling state instead (previous bullet).

  • Boundary conditions. The clamped loads are 1.5 to 1.9 times the simply supported ones. The test loads lie between the two and are 1.2 to 1.6 times the simply supported analysis of panels although imperfections can only lower them, which is why Card and Jones concluded that the test cylinders were more nearly clamped than simply supported (p. 11). The axial restraint of the clamped ends, with \(u\) prescribed on the whole end section, contributes to the difference between the two conditions (Appendix B of the report).

  • Test against analysis. The test loads are 80 % to 93 % of the clamped analysis of panels, and 79 % to 92 % of the clamped theory of the report (Card and Jones give 70 % to 95 % for all twelve cylinders). Linear buckling of the perfect cylinder is an upper bound: the test loads are reduced by the initial imperfections, to which axially compressed cylinders are sensitive (the external cylinders buckled with a loud report and large diamond-shaped buckles, p. 8), by the two butt joints per cylinder with riveted doubler plates (p. 5), by the potting of the ends in a soft alloy, which is not a perfect clamp, and by the scatter of the skin thickness (\(\pm 8\) % of the average, p. 5).

  • External against internal stringers. The ratio of the external to the internal cylinder of the same length is 2.08 and 2.18 in panels, 2.04 and 2.12 in the clamped theory, and 2.35 and 2.20 in the tests: the discrete model reproduces the eccentricity effect that motivated the tests.

  • Wave numbers. The clamped analysis gives one more circumferential wave than the tests (\(n = 7\), 7, 8, 7 against 6, 6, 7, 6); the measured \(n\) is a neighbouring mode, 7 % (cylinders 1 and 3) and 4 % (cylinder 2, mode \((1, 6)\)) above the critical load, and more than 20 % for cylinder 4 (table above). The simply supported analysis gives the measured \(n\) for cylinders 1, 3 and 4. Imperfections and the actual end restraint can select a neighbouring mode. Cylinder 1 developed two tiers of buckles in the test (p. 8); in panels its lowest mode with two axial half-waves, \((m, n) = (2, 8)\), is 9 % above the critical one. The critical mode of cylinder 2 has two axial half-waves in both analyses with clamped ends, as in the bfsccylinder implementation.
