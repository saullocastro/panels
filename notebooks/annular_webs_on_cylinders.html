Annular webs of ring frames connected to cylinders#

A ring frame is modelled with an annular web, an annular-sector model of panels, connected to a cylinder with 'BFxcte'. When the cylinder is clamped at the station of the connection, the connection alone clamps the connected edge of the web (the web itself has free edges), and the lowest frequencies of the assembly must be those of the annulus clamped on that edge and free on the other, known exactly from the frequency equation of Leissa [1]. This notebook makes that comparison for:

  1. a web at the reference surface of the cylinder, at its clamped end;

  2. internal frames, the outer edge of the web on the inner surface of the cylinder (face='lower'), for cylinders with the Sanders-Koiter and with the Donnell kinematics, and for a web and a cylinder of the first-order shear deformation theory (FSDT);

  3. an external frame, the inner edge of the web on the outer surface (face='upper'), against the annulus clamped inside and free outside;

  4. webs of the third-order shear deformation theory (TSDT) on a TSDT cylinder;

  5. webs and cylinders of different shear deformation theories (FSDT and TSDT);

  6. a total-Lagrangian TSDT web on the inner surface of a total-Lagrangian TSDT cylinder;

each with the null-space and with the penalty connection. Section 7 compares an internal frame on a cylinder described by its inner surface, with an offset, and by its mid-surface, with face='lower'.

Connection#

With dict(p1=cylinder, p2=web, func='BFxcte', xcte1=x_f, xcte2=0) the connection imposes \(u_{cyl} = w_{web}\), \(v_{cyl} = v_{web}\), \(w_{cyl} = -u_{web}\) and the continuity of the rotation about the circumferential axis, \(w_{cyl,x} = w_{web,x}\), at the station \(x_f\) of the cylinder and the edge xcte2 of the web. With face='lower' or face='upper' the displacements of the cylinder are those of its inner or outer surface, \(z_f = d \mp h/2\) in the coordinate \(z\) of the cylinder (\(d\) the offset of the laminate), with the Kirchhoff-Love kinematics,

\[u(z_f) = u - z_f w_{,x}, \qquad v(z_f) = \left(1 + \frac{z_f}{r}\right) v - z_f w_{,y}\]

the term \(z_f v/r\) being that of the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, used by default whatever the model of the cylinder (and omitted with kinematics='model' for a Donnell cylinder). At a clamped end of the cylinder all of them vanish, which clamps the connected edge of the web.

With the shear deformation theories the rotation connected is that of the normal, \(\theta_x = \partial u/\partial z\), on each side, following its own model: \(\theta_x = \phi_x\) at every \(z\) of an FSDT cylinder and at the reference surface of an FSDT web; at the reference surface of a TSDT cylinder or web without offset, \(\theta_x = \phi_x\), and at a face of a TSDT cylinder \(\theta_x = -w_{,x}\), where the transverse shear strain of the TSDT vanishes. The displacements at a face are those of the model of the cylinder, \(u(z) = u + z \phi_x\) for the FSDT, with the cubic terms of the TSDT, and \(v(z)\) with the Sanders-Koiter term \(z v/r\) by default.

Model#

The cylinder is made of two half cylinders of length \(0.5 r\), closed with 'SSycte', and the web of two half annuli, also closed with 'SSycte'; each half web is connected to a half cylinder. Each field is a Ritz series of Bardell’s hierarchical functions [2]: \(m = 8\), \(n = 12\) terms for the half cylinders and \(m = 10\), \(n = 12\) for the half webs. Isotropic material, \(h = 10^{-3} r\), the ratio of the inner to the outer radius of the web \(\beta = 0.4\); the cylinder is clamped at both ends, and its lowest frequencies are well above those of the web, so that the lowest modes of the assembly are those of the web for \(n = 0, 1, 1, 2, 2\) nodal diameters.

    return FIELDS[:modelDB.db[p.model]['dofs']]


def _free(p):
    for f in _fields(p):
        for e in ('x1', 'x2', 'y1', 'y2'):
            setattr(p, e + f, 1)
            setattr(p, e + f + 'r', 1)


def cylinder(r, length, offset=0., clamped=True, h=H, m=8, n=12, model=CYL):
    # half cylinder of reference radius r, its ends x = 0, L clamped or free
    c = Shell(model=model, a=length, b=np.pi*r, r=r, m=m, n=n, plyt=h, stack=[0],
              laminaprop=(E, NU), rho=RHO, offset=offset)
    _free(c)
    if clamped:
        for e in ('x1', 'x2'):
            for f in _fields(c):
                setattr(c, e + f, 0)
            for f in 'uvw':
                setattr(c, e + f + 'r', 0)
    return c


def web(r_outer, r_inner, h=H, m=10, n=12, model=WEB):
    # half annulus, free edges
    w = Shell(model=model, a=r_outer - r_inner, b=np.pi*r_outer, r=r_outer, m=m, n=n, plyt=h,
              stack=[0], laminaprop=(E, NU), rho=RHO)
    _free(w)
    return w


def assembly(cyls, webs, conn_web, conn_method):
    # two half cylinders and two half webs closed with 'SSycte', each web connected to its
    # cylinder by conn_web(cylinder, web)
    conn = []
    for p1, p2 in (cyls, webs):
        conn += [dict(p1=p1, p2=p2, func='SSycte', ycte1=p1.b, ycte2=0),
                 dict(p1=p1, p2=p2, func='SSycte', ycte1=0, ycte2=p2.b)]
    for c, w in zip(cyls, webs):
        conn.append(conn_web(c, w))
    return MultiDomain(cyls + webs, conn, conn_method=conn_method)


def lambda2(md_, r_outer, num=8):
    # lambda^2 = omega r_o^2 sqrt(rho h/D), sorted
    K = md_.reduce(md_.calc_kC(silent=True))
    M = md_.reduce(md_.calc_kM(silent=True))
    eigvals, _ = freq(K, M, sparse_solver=False, silent=True, num_eigvalues=num)
    return np.sort(np.sqrt(-eigvals.real))*r_outer**2*np.sqrt(RHO*H/D)


def compare(rows, diffs, label, l2, ref):
    # rows of the five lowest lambda^2 against ref for n = 0, 1, 1, 2, 2
    for n, v, r in zip((0, 1, 1, 2, 2), l2[:5], np.asarray(ref)[[0, 1, 1, 2, 2]]):
        diffs.append(rel(v, r))
        rows.append(label + [n, '%.5f' % r, '%.5f' % v, '%+.5f' % diffs[-1]])

Reference roots#

With \(w = W(\rho) \cos n\theta\) the free vibration of a Kirchhoff plate has the solution \(W = A J_n(k\rho) + B Y_n(k\rho) + C I_n(k\rho) + D K_n(k\rho)\), [1], Eq. (1.17), with \(k^4 = \rho_m h \omega^2/D\). The frequency equation of the annulus with \(W = W' = 0\) at the clamped edge and the bending moment and the Kelvin-Kirchhoff edge reaction of Leissa’s Eqs. (1.11)-(1.13) zero at the free edge is the \(4 \times 4\) determinant of det_annulus, whose roots are the frequency parameters \(\lambda^2 = \omega r_o^2 \sqrt{\rho_m h/D}\), \(r_o\) the outer radius of the web. For \(\nu = 1/3\) and \(\beta = 0.4\), EXACT (clamped outside, free inside) and EXACT_INNER (clamped inside, free outside) are the reference values, to six digits:

[3]:

def det_annulus(lam2, n, beta, nu, clamped='outer'):
    # determinant of the frequency equation of the annulus of outer radius 1 and inner radius
    # beta, clamped on the edge `clamped` ('outer' or 'inner') and free on the other
    k = np.sqrt(lam2)
    rc, rf = (1., beta) if clamped == 'outer' else (beta, 1.)
    cols = []
    for f, fp in ((jv, jvp), (yv, yvp), (iv, ivp), (kv, kvp)):
        W0, W1c = f(n, k*rc), k*fp(n, k*rc, 1)
        W, W1, W2, W3 = (f(n, k*rf), k*fp(n, k*rf, 1), k**2*fp(n, k*rf, 2),
                         k**3*fp(n, k*rf, 3))
        b = rf
        M = W2 + nu*(W1/b - n**2*W/b**2)
        V = (W3 + W2/b - W1/b**2 - n**2*W1/b**2 + 2*n**2*W/b**3
             - (1 - nu)*n**2/b**2*(W1 - W/b))
        col = np.array([W0, W1c, M, V])
        cols.append(col/np.abs(col).max())
    return np.linalg.det(np.array(cols).T)


def exact_lambda2(n, beta, guess, nu, clamped='outer'):
    # root of the frequency equation between 0.9 and 1.1 times `guess`
    return brentq(det_annulus, 0.9*guess, 1.1*guess, args=(n, beta, nu, clamped))

def fsdt_internal_frame(method, h=1e-3, beta=0.4, nu=0.3):
    r_o = R - h/2
    cyls, webs = [], []
    for _ in range(2):
        c = Shell(model='cylshell_fsdt_sanders', a=0.5*R, b=np.pi*R, r=R, m=8, n=12, plyt=h,
                  stack=[0], laminaprop=(E, nu), rho=RHO)
        _free(c)
        for f in FIELDS:
            setattr(c, 'x1' + f, 0)
        cyls.append(c)
        w = Shell(model='annular_fsdt_donnell', a=r_o*(1 - beta), b=np.pi*r_o, r=r_o, m=10,
                  n=12, plyt=h, stack=[0], laminaprop=(E, nu), rho=RHO)
        _free(w)
        webs.append(w)
    md_ = assembly(cyls, webs, lambda c, w: dict(p1=c, p2=w, func='BFxcte', xcte1=0, xcte2=0,
                   face='lower'), method)
    K = md_.reduce(md_.calc_kC(silent=True))
    M = md_.reduce(md_.calc_kM(silent=True))
    eigvals, _ = freq(K, M, sparse_solver=False, silent=True, num_eigvalues=8)
    Dh = E*h**3/(12*(1 - nu**2))
    return np.sort(np.sqrt(-eigvals.real))*r_o**2*np.sqrt(RHO*h/Dh)

7. Inner surface: offset reference surface or mid-surface with 'face'#

With the reference surface of the cylinder at its inner face, offset \(d = h/2\), the inner face is \(z_f = 0\) and the face connection is the connection at the reference surface. The same cylinder described with its mid-surface, radius \(r + h/2\) and \(d = 0\), connected at its lower face, differs only by the thin-shell approximations of the laminate offset. A web of outer radius \(r\) and inner radius \(0.8 r\) (height \(0.2 r\)) is connected at mid-length of a simply supported cylinder of length \(0.5 r\) (\(v = w = 0\) at both ends, \(u = 0\) at \(x = 0\)), \(m = 10\) for the half cylinders and \(m = 8\) for the half webs, in three ways:

    1. cylinder of radius \(r\) with offset \(h/2\), no 'face';

    1. cylinder of radius \(r + h/2\) without offset, face='lower';

    1. cylinder of radius \(r + h/2\) without offset, the web attached to its mid-surface, its outer radius larger by \(h/2\).

The eight lowest frequencies of (b) and (c) relative to those of (a), for \(h/r = 0.002\) and \(0.02\) with the null-space method and for \(h/r = 0.002\) with the penalty method:

8. Discussion#

  • The null-space connection reproduces the roots of the frequency equation of the annulus to \(4 \times 10^{-4}\) % for the webs of the classical plate theory, whether the web is connected at the reference surface of the cylinder (section 1), on its inner surface (section 2) or on its outer surface (section 3), for the Sanders-Koiter and the Donnell cylinders alike: the clamped end of the cylinder clamps the connected edge of the web exactly, and \(m = 10\), \(n = 12\) terms converge the five lowest frequencies.

  • The webs of the shear deformation theories, \(h/r_o = 0.001\), give values lower than the roots of the classical theory by at most \(3.1 \times 10^{-3}\) % (sections 2, 4, 5 and 6), the effect of the transverse shear; the three annular_tsdt_* models give the same values, since the gl and tl kernels reduce to the linear ones at zero displacements, and the result does not depend on the theory of the cylinder (FSDT or TSDT) nor on the face, because at a clamped end every connected quantity of the cylinder vanishes. The total-Lagrangian web on the total-Lagrangian cylinder (section 6) gives the values of the same web on cylshell_tsdt_sanders (section 4) to the printed digits.

  • The penalty connection, with the default penalty constants, gives values 0.12 % to 0.31 % lower than the roots: the edge of the web is clamped through finite penalty springs.

  • Section 7: modelling the cylinder by its mid-surface with face='lower' agrees with the cylinder whose reference surface is its inner face to \(1.3 \times 10^{-3}\) % for \(h/r = 0.002\) with the null space; for \(h/r = 0.02\) the difference grows to 1.3 %, the thin-shell approximation of the laminate offset. Attaching the web to the mid-surface instead, its outer radius larger by \(h/2\), lowers the frequencies by 0.9 % (\(h/r = 0.002\)) and 5.1 % (\(h/r = 0.02\)). With the penalty method the two descriptions differ by 0.7 %, because the default penalty constants depend on the stiffness of the laminate about its reference surface, which the offset changes.

The test suite checks these connections with small webs (\(m = 4\), \(n = 6\)) against the same webs clamped directly on the connected edge, the total-Lagrangian web on the total-Lagrangian cylinder included, and the CLPT cases of sections 1 to 3 against the roots.
