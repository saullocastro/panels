Thick annular plates: shear deformation theories against exact and Ritz solutions#

The annular-sector models of panels with transverse shear, annular_fsdt_* (first-order shear deformation theory, FSDT) and annular_tsdt_* (Reddy’s third-order theory, TSDT), are compared here with three sources on thick isotropic annuli:

  1. Bagheri, Kiani and Eslami [1]: buckling torques of moderately thick annuli, FSDT without a shear correction factor, by the differential quadrature method;

  2. Bisadi, Es’haghi, Rokni and Ilkhani [2]: exact natural frequencies of annuli with Reddy’s TSDT, for free, clamped, hard and soft simply supported edges;

  3. Dozio [3]: Ritz natural frequencies of annuli with the FSDT and the shear correction factor \(5/6\).

Section 1 shows, for [1], the buckling torques with the shear correction factor 1, which reproduces the paper, beside those with the default of panels, fsdt_shear_correction = 'rohwer'.

Model#

A full annulus of outer radius \(a\) and inner radius \(b = R a\) is made of two half annuli whose free radial edges are connected with 'SSycte', so that modes with any number \(p\) of nodal diameters are represented. In the coordinates of the annular sector, \(x = a - \rho\) runs from the outer edge (\(x = 0\), edge x1) inwards to the inner edge (edge x2), and \(y\) is the arc length of the outer edge; the rotations \(\phi_x\), \(\phi_y\) are the rotations \(\psi_\rho\), \(\psi_\theta\) of the sources up to the sign of \(\phi_x\). Each field is a Ritz series of Bardell’s hierarchical functions, \(m\) terms along the radius and \(n\) along each half circumference [4]. The material is isotropic, one layer, \(\nu = 0.3\); the results are dimensionless and do not depend on \(E\), \(\rho\) or \(a\).

The edges, named inner-outer as in the sources, fix the following fields of panels:

edge

TSDT [2]

FSDT [1], [3]

free (F)

none

none

clamped (C)

\(u, v, w, w_{,x}, \phi_x, \phi_y\)

\(u, v, w, \phi_x, \phi_y\)

hard simply supported (\(S_h\), S)

\(v, w, \phi_y\)

\(v, w, \phi_y\)

soft simply supported (\(S_s\))

\(w\)

\(w\)

The TSDT has the slope \(w_{,x}\) as a degree of freedom of the edge ([2], Eq. (50)); the FSDT does not, and fixing \(w_{,x}\) there would over-constrain a clamped edge. For the frequencies, the in-plane conditions matter only for the in-plane modes: for one isotropic layer without offset, the in-plane displacements \(u, v\) are uncoupled from \(w, \phi_x, \phi_y\).

The frequency parameter of [2] and [3] is \(\beta = \omega a^2 \sqrt{\rho h/D}\), with \(D = E h^3/(12 (1 - \nu^2))\). The modes with \(p \ge 1\) come in pairs of equal frequencies, merged below; the count of each frequency (1 or 2) tells \(p = 0\) from \(p \ge 1\).

def annulus(model, R, delta, inner, outer, m, n, shear=None, inplane=None):
    # full annulus of two half annuli; inplane fixes u, v on the given edges
    family = 'tsdt' if 'tsdt' in model else 'fsdt'
    halves = []
    for _ in range(2):
        s = Shell(model=model, a=A*(1 - R), b=np.pi*A, r=A, m=m, n=n,
                  plyt=delta*A, stack=[0], laminaprop=(E, NU), rho=RHO)
        if shear is not None:
            s.fsdt_shear_correction = shear
        for e in ('x1', 'x2', 'y1', 'y2'):
            for f in FIELDS:
                setattr(s, e + f, 1)
                setattr(s, e + f + 'r', 1)
        for e, kind in (('x1', outer), ('x2', inner)):
            for f in EDGES[family][kind]:
                setattr(s, e + f, 0)
        halves.append(s)
    p1, p2 = halves
    conn = [dict(p1=p1, p2=p2, func='SSycte', ycte1=p1.b, ycte2=0),
            dict(p1=p1, p2=p2, func='SSycte', ycte1=0, ycte2=p2.b)]
    return MultiDomain([p1, p2], conn)


def frequencies(md, delta, family='all', num=80):
    # beta of the modes of the family 'transverse' (w, phix, phiy dominate the Ritz
    # constants), 'inplane' or 'all', rigid-body modes excluded, pairs merged
    K = md.reduce(md.calc_kC(silent=True))
    M = md.reduce(md.calc_kM(silent=True))
    eigvals, eigvecs = freq(K, M, sparse_solver=False, silent=True, num_eigvalues=num,
                            symmetric=True, max_dense_size=10000)
    h = delta*A
    D = E*h**3/(12*(1 - NU**2))
    beta = np.sqrt(np.abs(eigvals))*A**2*np.sqrt(RHO*h/D)
    phi = md.expand(eigvecs)
    transverse = np.zeros(phi.shape[0], dtype=bool)
    for p in md.panels:
        for field in (2, 3, 4):
            transverse[p.col_start + field:p.col_end:5] = True
    frac = np.linalg.norm(phi[transverse], axis=0)/np.linalg.norm(phi, axis=0)
    keep = np.isfinite(beta) & (beta > 0.1)
    if family == 'transverse':
        keep &= frac > 0.5
    elif family == 'inplane':
        keep &= frac < 0.5
    beta = np.sort(beta[keep])
    values, count = [], []
    for b in beta:
        if values and b/values[-1] - 1 < 1e-4:
            count[-1] += 1
        else:
            values.append(b)
            count.append(1)
    return np.array(values), np.array(count)

1. Buckling torque of moderately thick annuli (Bagheri, Kiani and Eslami 2019)#

A torque \(T\) on the outer edge, reacted on the inner edge, gives the membrane state \(N_{\rho\theta} = T/(2 \pi \rho^2)\), \(N_{\rho\rho} = N_{\theta\theta} = 0\) ([1], Eqs. (10) and (12)). It is applied as the tangential force per unit length \(q = T/(2 \pi a^2)\) on the outer edge, the inner edge fixed in its plane (\(u = v = 0\)); MultiDomain.linear_buckling computes the linear static state and the least buckling load. The critical torque is \(\tau = T/(2 \pi D)\) ([1], Eq. (26)). Here the simply supported edge (S) of [1] is the hard one, \(w = \phi_y = 0\) ([1], Eq. (11)).

[1] uses the transverse shear stiffness \(A_{44} = A_{55} = G h\) without a shear correction factor (its Eq. (7)): fsdt_shear_correction = 1 reproduces its Table 2. The default of panels, 'rohwer', the method of Rohwer for the transverse shear stiffness of laminates, gives the factor printed below for the homogeneous plate:

def tau(beta, inner, outer, delta, shear, m=12, n=16):
    D = E*(delta*A)**3/(12*(1 - NU**2))
    T = 2*np.pi*D
    flags = {'C': (0, 0, 0), 'S': (0, 1, 0), 'F': (1, 1, 1)}   # w, phix, phiy
    halves = []
    for _ in range(2):
        s = Shell(model='annular_fsdt_donnell', a=A*(1 - beta), b=np.pi*A, r=A, m=m, n=n,
                  plyt=delta*A, stack=[0], laminaprop=(E, NU), rho=RHO)
        s.fsdt_shear_correction = shear
        for e in ('x1', 'x2', 'y1', 'y2'):
            for f in FIELDS:
                setattr(s, e + f, 1)
                setattr(s, e + f + 'r', 1)
        s.x1w, s.x1phix, s.x1phiy = flags[outer]
        s.x2w, s.x2phix, s.x2phiy = flags[inner]
        s.x2u = s.x2v = 0
        s.add_distr_load_fixed_x(0., funcy=lambda y: T/(2*np.pi*A**2) + 0*y)
        halves.append(s)
    p1, p2 = halves
    conn = [dict(p1=p1, p2=p2, func='SSycte', ycte1=p1.b, ycte2=0),
            dict(p1=p1, p2=p2, func='SSycte', ycte1=0, ycte2=p2.b)]
    eigvals, _ = MultiDomain([p1, p2], conn).linear_buckling(num_eigvalues=6, silent=True)
    return np.abs(eigvals).min()

2. Natural frequencies with Reddy’s TSDT (Bisadi et al. 2012)#

[2] solves exactly the equations of motion of Reddy’s TSDT for the transverse vibration of an isotropic annulus, with the rotary and higher-order inertia, and tabulates \(\beta\) by \((p, s)\), \(p\) the number of nodal diameters and \(s\) the radial order, for \(R = b/a\) and \(\delta = h/a\). The model annular_tsdt_donnell has the same displacement field (Reddy’s, about the mid-surface) and its consistent mass matrix; only the transverse family of modes is compared.

4. Discussion#

Bagheri et al. [1]. With the shear correction factor 1 of [1], panels reproduces the 16 buckling torques of its Table 2 within 0.04 % (0.01 % except F-C, \(h/a = 0.05\), \(\beta = 0.3\)), converged for \(m = 8\), \(n = 12\) in the case shown. The default of panels, 'rohwer', gives the factor \(5/6\) for the homogeneous plate, and torques lower by 0.8 % (\(h/a = 0.05\), edges C-F and F-C) to 8.2 % (\(h/a = 0.10\), C-C, \(\beta = 0.5\)): the more the transverse shear lowers the torque, the thicker and the more restrained the plate, the larger the effect of the factor. The values of [1] are those of a plate without a shear correction, stiffer in transverse shear than the homogeneous plate.

Bisadi et al. [2], TSDT. The Ritz solution approaches the exact one from above. Without a clamped edge, panels reproduces the tabulated values to their last digit (within 0.0003 %) with \(m = 16\), \(n = 20\), the modes in the order of [2] and with the count expected from \(p\). A clamped edge, where the TSDT fixes \(w_{,x}\) besides the rotations, converges more slowly: up to 0.01 % for \(R = 0.2\), and 0.06 % to 0.08 % for the clamped inner edge of radius \(0.1 a\) (Table 5), still decreasing with \(m\) and \(n\). The thin annuli of the tests of annular_tsdt_* reproduce the classical plate; these tables check the thick range, \(h/a\) up to 0.3, for all four kinds of edge.

Irie et al. [5], FSDT. [2] reprints the exact FSDT values of [5] without its shear correction factor. annular_fsdt_donnell with \(\pi^2/12\) reproduces them within 0.04 % (0.09 % for F-\(S_h\), \((0, 0)\), 4.81 in [5]), whereas \(5/6\) gives up to 0.19 % more: the values of [5] are consistent with \(\pi^2/12\). The TSDT frequencies are above the FSDT ones by up to 0.8 % here, as [2] notes.

Dozio [3], FSDT. With the shear correction \(5/6\), panels reproduces the 32 tabulated values within 0.005 %, transverse and in-plane modes alike, including the in-plane torsional mode \(p = 0\) of the annuli with a clamped edge. One mode of panels is not in Table 5 of [3]: for the annulus free inside and clamped outside, a transverse pair at \(\beta = 40.294\), one nodal diameter, second radial order, between the sixth and the seventh values of [3]; annular_tsdt_donnell has the same mode at 40.720. The lists of the 3-D Ritz solutions reprinted in [3], Table 4, for the same annulus do not have it either; the reason is not known.
