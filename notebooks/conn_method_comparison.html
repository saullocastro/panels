Benchmarks with the penalty and the null-space connections#

References. M. Stamatelos and G. Labeas, see stamatelos_labeas_2023_multidomain; the DCB references, see the validation summary.

The panels models of the benchmark notebooks are compared with the two methods of imposing the multidomain connections, see Exact connections with the null-space method:

  • penalty: conn_method='penalty', the penalty stiffnesses used by the notebooks, raised 100 times for the 'SSxcte' edges of the DCB, see dcb_utils.build_dcb, and the default calc_kt_kr constant for the 'SB' bond;

  • null-space: conn_method='null-space', the 'SSxcte', 'SSycte', 'BFxcte', 'BFycte' and 'SB' connections imposed exactly.

The cohesive zone 'SB_TSL' and the prescribed displacements of the arms are penalty stiffnesses in both cases. All the models use the classical laminated plate theory (CLPT).

The null-space results are produced by conn_method_study.py, which takes the cases, discretisations and openings from the DCB notebooks themselves, and saves them in results/conn_methods/; the penalty results of the DCB cases are the results of the notebooks, results/*.npz. The cases and openings are those of the current notebooks.

The run times of both methods, the fields time and cpu_time of results/*.npz and results/conn_methods/null_space/*.npz, the profile of the cost, results/conn_methods/profile.json, and the run times of the Stamatelos notebook were measured in one job on one node of a computing cluster, under the same conditions for both methods: one run at a time, each in its own process, with one BLAS thread (the field evaluations of panels use their own threads, out_num_cores, with both methods), the penalty runs first and the null-space runs after them. The run time time is the wall time of dcb_utils.solve_dcb.

To compute the results again, after the results of the DCB notebooks (python notebooks/dcb_mode_I_validation_rerun.py run --jobs 1), delete results/conn_methods/null_space/*.npz and results/conn_methods/profile.json and run, from the root of the repository:

python notebooks/conn_method_study.py run --jobs 1 --methods null-space
python notebooks/conn_method_study.py compliance
python notebooks/conn_method_study.py profile
python notebooks/conn_method_study.py stamatelos

jobs, compliances = study.collect()
jobs = sorted(jobs, key=lambda j: (j['notebook'], j['name']))


def load(path):
    if not os.path.exists(path):
        return None
    r = du.load(path)
    return r if len(r['delta']) else None


def curve(r):
    return np.r_[0., r['delta']], np.r_[0., r['P']]


def complete(r, job):
    return r is not None and np.isclose(r['delta'][-1], job['openings'][-1])


pct = lambda a, b: 100*(a/b - 1)
res = {j['name']: dict(penalty=load(os.path.join(R, j['name'] + '.npz')),

    rp, rn = res[j['name']]['penalty'], res[j['name']]['null']

    rows.append(f"| {j['name']} | {k} of {len(rp['P'])} | {pre:.3f} | {post} |")

DCB: computational cost#

The wall times of the runs, measured with both methods under the same conditions, are compared in Measured wall times below. The cost is also measured by component, with python notebooks/conn_method_study.py profile (profile_case and profile_panels, saved in results/conn_methods/profile.json), after the runs, in the same job and with the same settings of the threads:

  • each component of a Newton-Raphson iteration of dcb_utils.solve_dcb is timed for each case, with both methods one after the other, at the linear solution at the opening of the stored peak load, where the damage grows ahead of the crack tip;

  • kT_pan is the tangent stiffness of the panels, calc_kT, with the nonlinear terms of CLPT, timed once; residual is get_kC_conn plus calc_fint; kT_TSL the damage-rate stiffness of the cohesive zone; reduce the product \(\boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{Z}\), the sum of the matrices and the products of the vectors by \(\boldsymbol{Z}\); solve the sparse solution of structsolve.solve; Z the construction of the null-space basis, once per run; the components other than kT_pan are the median of 3 repetitions.

The cost of a run follows from these timings and from the number of calls of each component, given by the iterations of each opening and the rules of solve_dcb, see conn_method_study.iteration_counts and conn_method_study.predicted_cost.

  • The tangent stiffness of the panels, kT_pan, is 53.1 to 78.1 % of the predicted cost of a run, and within it the numerical integration of the two cohesive-zone panels, 96.6 to 99.0 % of kT_pan: they have 25 x 10 terms integrated on 49 x 19 points, 30 x 10 terms integrated on 59 x 19 points, against 8 x 8 terms integrated on 15 x 15 points for the other panels. This computation is the same with both methods.

  • The null-space method removes 80 to 320 of the unknowns, but only 2.8 to 7.4 % of the nonzeros of the system, which are dominated by the full coupling of the cohesive-zone panels by 'SB_TSL', a penalty stiffness with both methods. The sparse solution is 12 to 38 % faster and get_kC_conn no longer computes the edge penalties, while \(Z^ op K Z\) adds 0.09 to 0.12 s per iteration. The basis \(Z\) is built once, in 0.008 to 0.033 s.

  • With the same iterations, the predicted cost of the null-space runs differs from that of the penalty runs by -4.22 to +0.97 %. The runs differ also by their numbers of iterations, 2788 for the penalty runs against 2826 for the null-space runs over the openings compared, giving -4.3 to +4.9 % case by case: the Newton-Raphson iterations follow slightly different paths, without a systematic trend.

Discussion#

DCB. The 17 cases of 6 notebooks were run to the last opening with both methods, without bisections of the increments:

  • the peak loads of the two methods agree within 0.07 %, and the load-opening curves within 0.12 % of the peak load before the peak and 0.15 % after it;

  • the crack growth at the last opening is the same, except for tijs2023_full_weld, 0.3 mm longer with the null-space method;

  • the null-space models are slightly stiffer, the initial stiffness by 0.07 % to 0.36 % and the elastic compliances lower by 0.13 % to 0.21 %: the connections 'SSxcte' and 'SB' are exact, while their penalty stiffnesses, 100 times the default of calc_kt_kr for the edges, leave a small compliance.

The penalty stiffnesses of dcb_utils.build_dcb are therefore adequate for these benchmarks: they change the stiffness by about 0.1 % to 0.4 % and the peak loads by less than 0.07 %, which is negligible for these benchmarks. The null-space method gives these results without a penalty factor to choose.

Stamatelos and Labeas (2023). For the strips connected by 'SSycte' or 'SSxcte', the plates of Tables 2 and 3 and the laminates bonded by 'SB', the null-space method reproduces the single-domain eigenvalues to within 0.004 %, whereas the penalty results depend on the penalty factor: -15.6 % at 0.1 times the default stiffness of the 'SSycte' strips, -2.0 % at the default and -0.02 % at 100 times the default. For the plate blades connected by 'BFycte' and 'BFxcte', the null-space results are the limit of the penalty results, equal to those at 100 times the default penalty within 0.01 %, while the default penalty differs from them by up to 0.46 %. The remaining differences to the BladeStiff1D references of Tables 4 and 5, up to 1.5 % with the null-space method and 1.9 % with the default penalty, and of Figure 7, 1.3 % to 1.6 %, are those of the models, not of the connections. The 'SB_TSL' connection is a penalty stiffness in both cases, and gives the same results.

Cost. See the summary of the cost above. Timed under the same conditions, one run at a time, the 17 DCB runs take 4.38 h with the null-space method against 4.29 h with the penalty method (+2.0 %), from -8.9 % to +9.9 % case by case, while the profile predicts -4.3 % to +4.9 %; the measured times are 0.97 to 1.19 times the predicted ones, so that the case-by-case differences are within the scatter of the timings. The tangent stiffness of the panels, the same with both methods, is 53 % to 78 % of the predicted cost of the DCB runs, almost entirely that of the cohesive-zone panels, and with the same iterations the null-space runs cost from 4.2 % less to 1.0 % more: the product \(\boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{Z}\) is offset, wholly or in part, by the smaller system and the residual without the edge penalties. The runs of the Stamatelos notebook took 77 s with the null-space method against 81 s with the penalty method, measured once under the same conditions; this notebook has many small models and was not profiled.

Conclusion. The null-space method gives the results of the penalty method in the limit of an infinite penalty stiffness, with no penalty factor to choose and no loss of conditioning, and at the same cost in the DCB benchmarks, +2 % over the 17 runs, within the scatter of the timings case by case. Where the penalty stiffnesses of the notebooks are large enough, as in the DCB benchmarks, both methods agree within 0.4 %; where they are not, the penalty results depend on the factor, while the null-space results do not.
