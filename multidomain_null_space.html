Exact connections with the null-space method#

The domains of a MultiDomain assembly can be connected with penalty stiffnesses, conn_method='penalty', see MultiDomain.get_kC_conn() and [castro2017Multidomain]. The penalty method adds, for each quantity \(q_k\) that must be continuous across a connection, the energy:

(1)#\[U_k = \frac{1}{2} k_k \int \left( q_k^{(1)} - q_k^{(2)} \right)^2\]

where \(k_k\) is the translation or rotation penalty constant, 'kt' or 'kr', and the integral is along the connected edges, or over the area of the connection 'SB'. The continuity is then only approximate. Low penalty constants make the assembly too flexible, and high ones make the stiffness matrix ill-conditioned. The default constants of calc_kt_kr() are a compromise, and they can still lead to errors of a few percent, see Comparison with the penalty method.

With conn_method='null-space', the default, the connections are imposed exactly, as with penalty constants going to infinity, without any penalty constant:

md = MultiDomain(panels, conn)  # conn_method='null-space'

and the penalty method is selected with:

md = MultiDomain(panels, conn, conn_method='penalty')

The connections that can be imposed with the null-space method are given in nullspace.NULL_SPACE_FUNCS: 'SSxcte', 'SSycte', 'BFxcte', 'BFycte', 'BFxycte' and 'SB', for all the models, see Quantities imposed. The damaged connection 'SB_TSL' represents the physical stiffness of a cohesive interface, see Cohesive zone model of the multi-domain approach, and it keeps its penalty stiffness also with conn_method='null-space'.

Theory#

The quantities \(q_k\) of Eq. (1) are linear combinations of the Ritz constants \(\boldsymbol{c}\) of the two domains. Since Bardell’s functions are polynomials, see Bardell’s hierarchical functions, the difference \(q_k^{(1)} - q_k^{(2)}\) is a polynomial along the connection, of the natural coordinate \(\xi\) or \(\eta\) shared by the two domains. It vanishes everywhere when it vanishes at as many points as the number of its coefficients, and the connections become the homogeneous linear constraints:

(2)#\[\boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

where each row of \(\boldsymbol{B}\) is \(q_k^{(1)} - q_k^{(2)}\) at one Gauss-Legendre point along the connection, or over the area of 'SB', see nullspace.constraint_matrix(). The first four functions of Bardell are cubic and the function \(i \ge 4\) has the order \(i\), so that \(\max(m_1, m_2, 4)\) points are used along \(x\) and \(\max(n_1, n_2, 4)\) along \(y\). The zero energy of Eq. (1) and the constraints of Eq. (2) are equivalent: the vectors with zero penalty energy are exactly those that satisfy the constraints.

Why not Lagrange multipliers#

The constraints could be imposed with Lagrange multipliers \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\), which add one unknown per constraint and give the system:

\[\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{K} & \boldsymbol{B}^\top \\ \boldsymbol{B} & \boldsymbol{0} \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{c} \\ \boldsymbol{\lambda} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{F} \\ \boldsymbol{0} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

This matrix is indefinite. For the linear buckling and frequency analyses the geometric stiffness matrix and the mass matrix have zero rows and columns for the multipliers, giving infinite eigenvalues that the shift-and-invert eigenvalue solvers of structsolve do not handle. Instead of adding unknowns, the null-space method removes them, giving the same solution.

Elimination of the constraints#

All the vectors that satisfy Eq. (2) are written with a basis \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) of the null space of \(\boldsymbol{B}\), \(\boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{Z} = \boldsymbol{0}\):

(3)#\[\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c}_r\]

where \(\boldsymbol{c}_r\) are the independent Ritz constants. Substituting Eq. (3) into the total potential energy gives the reduced system:

(4)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_r = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{Z}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_{G r} = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_G \boldsymbol{Z}, \qquad \boldsymbol{M}_r = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{M} \boldsymbol{Z}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_r = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{F}\]

where \(\boldsymbol{K}\), \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\), \(\boldsymbol{M}\) and \(\boldsymbol{F}\) are the matrices and vectors of the domains, which do not contain the connections imposed exactly. The reduced stiffness matrix is symmetric and positive definite whenever the connected assembly has no rigid-body motion, like the stiffness matrix of the penalty method, and the static, linear buckling, frequency and non-linear solvers of structsolve are used unchanged. The constraints are linear in \(\boldsymbol{c}\), therefore \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) does not change during a non-linear analysis:

\[\boldsymbol{F}_{int, r} = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c}_r), \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_{T r} = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_T(\boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c}_r) \boldsymbol{Z}\]

\(\boldsymbol{Z}\) is built by nullspace.null_space_basis(), one connection at a time:

  1. the rows of \(\boldsymbol{B}\) of the connection are normalized to a unit norm and written in the independent Ritz constants of the connections already processed, \(\boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{Z}\);

  2. the rank of the constraints is revealed with a QR decomposition with column pivoting, \(\boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{P} = \boldsymbol{Q} \boldsymbol{U}\), with the permutation \(\boldsymbol{P}\), the orthogonal factor \(\boldsymbol{Q}\) and the upper triangular factor \(\boldsymbol{U}\), and each pivot larger than the tolerance tol gives one independent constraint;

  3. the Ritz constants of the pivot columns are eliminated, \(\boldsymbol{c}_e = -\boldsymbol{U}_{11}^{-1} \boldsymbol{U}_{12} \boldsymbol{c}_k\), where \(\boldsymbol{c}_k\) are the remaining constants, and \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) is updated.

Redundant constraints are found in step 2 and dropped. They happen for instance at a corner shared by four domains, around a closed cylinder, or where a boundary condition flag already removes the term that a constraint would remove. Bardell’s functions of order 5 and higher vanish together with their slope at the ends of the domain, \(\xi = \pm 1\), so the constraints of an edge involve only the first four functions across it, and \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) has very few non-zero terms: the elimination is similar to the assembly of the hierarchical elements of the p-version of the finite element method. A connection along an interior line, e.g. 'BFycte' at the middle of the base, involves all the functions across the line.

The terms removed by the boundary condition flags are zero rows and columns of the matrices of the domains, as with the penalty method, and remain in \(\boldsymbol{c}_r\) with zero rows and columns in the reduced matrices.

\(\boldsymbol{Z}\) is stored in MultiDomain.Z and reused while the connections, the number of terms and the boundary condition flags do not change, see MultiDomain.get_Z().

Quantities imposed#

The quantities imposed by each connection are those of the corresponding penalty kernels, with p1 and p2 the first and second panels of the connection. For 'SB', the two panels share the axes \(x, y, z\), \(z\) being normal to their mid-surfaces, p1 is the panel on the positive side of the interface along \(z\) and p2 the one on the negative side, e.g. a stiffener base on the side of positive \(z\) of a skin is p1 and the skin p2, see MultiDomain.get_kC_conn():

\(u_1 = u_2\), \(v_1 = w_2\), \(w_1 = -v_2\), \(\Phi_{y1} = \Phi_{y2}\)

'BFxcte'

\(u_1 = w_2\), \(v_1 = v_2\), \(w_1 = -u_2\), \(w_{1,x} = w_{2,x}\), see panels.multidomain.connections.kCBFxcte

\(u_1 = w_2\), \(v_1 = v_2\), \(w_1 = -u_2\), \(\phi_{x1} = \phi_{x2}\)

'BFxycte'

the same displacements, \(\phi_{x1} = -\Phi_{y2}\)

'SB'

\(u_1(z_1) = u_2(z_2)\), \(v_1(z_1) = v_2(z_2)\), \(w_1 = w_2\) at the interface, \(z_1 = d_1 - h_1/2\) of p1 and \(z_2 = d_2 + h_2/2\) of p2, \(d_1\), \(d_2\) being the offsets, with \(u(z) = u - z w_{,x}\) and \(v(z) = (1 + z/r) v - z w_{,y}\) of each panel, see fkCSB(); the rotations are not tied and the key 'kr' is not used

where \(\Phi_y = \phi_y + v/r\) with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics and \(\Phi_y = \phi_y\) otherwise, and the terms \(v/r\) and \(z/r\) of the classical laminated plate theory are present only with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics. The quantities of 'SB' are the same for all the models, the displacements of the faces in contact, see kCsdt._sb_components(). Usage#

The matrices and vectors computed by MultiDomain have the size of all the Ritz constants and do not contain the connections imposed exactly: solving them directly would leave the domains disconnected. They are reduced with MultiDomain.reduce() before solving, and the solutions are expanded to all the Ritz constants with MultiDomain.expand(), which are used by the post-processing methods, e.g. MultiDomain.uvw(), MultiDomain.stress() and MultiDomain.plot(). With conn_method='penalty', \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) is the identity matrix, so that the same code runs with both methods.

Linear static analysis:

from structsolve import solve

md = MultiDomain(panels, conn)
kC = md.calc_kC()
fext = md.calc_fext()
c = md.expand(solve(md.reduce(kC), md.reduce(fext)))

Linear buckling analysis with the pre-buckling state of a static analysis:

from structsolve import lb, static

kC = md.reduce(md.calc_kC())
incs, cs = static(kC, md.reduce(md.calc_fext()))
c = md.expand(cs[0])
kG = md.reduce(md.calc_kG(c=c))
eigvals, eigvecs = lb(kC, kG)
eigvecs = md.expand(eigvecs)

Frequency analysis:

from structsolve import freq

eigvals, eigvecs = freq(md.reduce(md.calc_kC()), md.reduce(md.calc_kM()))
eigvecs = md.expand(eigvecs)

Geometrically non-linear analysis, with the functions of the reduced problem given by MultiDomain.get_reduced_functions():

from structsolve import Analysis

an = Analysis(*md.get_reduced_functions())
an.static(NLgeom=True)
cs = [md.expand(c_r) for c_r in an.cs]

The functions create_cylinder(), create_cylinder_blade_stiffened(), create_cylinder_z_stiffened(), the analyses of panels.multidomain.cylinder and panels.multidomain.cylinder_blade_stiffened, and tstiff2d_1stiff_freq(), tstiff2d_1stiff_compression(), tstiff2d_1stiff_flutter() and create_wingbox() take the argument conn_method, 'null-space' by default.

Comparison with the penalty method#

The penalty energy of Eq. (1) is non-negative: the penalty method is more flexible than the exact connections, its stiffness and its natural frequencies are lower, and its error decreases with the inverse of the penalty constants. The condition number of the stiffness matrix grows with the penalty constants, whereas the reduced stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{Z}\) of the null-space method does not depend on any constant. With the default constants of calc_kt_kr() the error of the penalty method is of the order of 1% for thin plates and reaches several percent for thick plates and stiffened shells, whereas the null-space method reproduces a single domain, or a single laminate with 'SB', to the convergence of the approximation.

Prescribed displacements imposed exactly#

The prescribed displacements of Shell.add_point_pd(), Shell.add_distr_pd_fixed_x() and Shell.add_distr_pd_fixed_y() are imposed with the penalty method by MultiDomain(..., pd_method='penalty'): their forces \(k u_p\) enter the external force vector and the penalty stiffnesses, e.g. of fkCpd(), fkCld_xcte() and fkCld_ycte(), for the models with 3 and 5 degrees of freedom per term, must be added to the stiffness matrix by the user, symmetrized with structsolve.sparseutils.finalize_symmetric_matrix(). For a point \((x_p, y_p)\) with the prescribed values \(q_p\) and a line \(x = x_l\) with the prescribed functions \(q_l(y)\), \(q = u, v, w\), and with \(\boldsymbol{S}^q\) the rows of the approximation functions of \(q\), the penalty energies \(\frac{1}{2} k_q (\boldsymbol{S}^q \boldsymbol{c} - q_p)^2\) and \(\frac{1}{2} \int_0^b k_q (\boldsymbol{S}^q \boldsymbol{c} - q_l)^2 dy\) give the stiffness matrices and force vectors

(5)#\[ \begin{align}\begin{aligned}\boldsymbol{K}_p = \sum_{q} k_q \left. (\boldsymbol{S}^q)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^q \right|_{(x_p, y_p)}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_p = \sum_{q} k_q q_p \left. (\boldsymbol{S}^q)^\top \right|_{(x_p, y_p)}\\\boldsymbol{K}_l = \sum_{q} k_q \int_0^b \left. (\boldsymbol{S}^q)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^q \right|_{x = x_l} dy, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_l = \sum_{q} k_q \int_0^b q_l(y) \left. (\boldsymbol{S}^q)^\top \right|_{x = x_l} dy\end{aligned}\end{align} \]

of fkCpd() and fkCld_xcte() (fkCld_ycte() along \(y = y_l\)), the forces being added by MultiDomain.calc_fext(); for a thickness coordinate z different from zero, the rows of \(u\) and \(v\) are those of \(u(z)\) and \(v(z)\). The prescribed values are reached with an error of order \(1/k\).

With pd_method='null-space', the default, they are imposed exactly, as inhomogeneous linear constraints eliminated together with the connections, without any penalty constant:

md = MultiDomain(panels, conn)

A single panel is wrapped in a MultiDomain without connections, MultiDomain([shell], conn=[]). Both settings are independent: the connections may be imposed with either conn_method, and the prescribed displacements with either pd_method.

The same methods of Shell define the prescribed displacements, the in-plane ones at the reference surface or at the thickness coordinate of the keyword z, e.g. at the faces of a face-clamped edge, see Face-clamped edges. With pd_method='null-space' the values of the penalty constants are not used: a component is prescribed when its penalty constant is neither None nor zero and its value is given, the same rule as for the penalty method, where a null penalty constant prescribes nothing. The rotations of the normal at the reference surface \(z = 0\) can also be prescribed: \(-w_{,x}\) and \(-w_{,y}\) for the classical laminated plate theory, and for the shear deformation theories \(\theta_x\) and \(\theta_y\) of Eq. (5), the rotations also tied by the connections, which are the fields \(\phi_x\) and \(\phi_y\) for the FSDT and for the TSDT without offset; the rotation about \(x\) includes \(v/r\) with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics. With an offset \(d\), the TSDT rotations \(\phi_x\) and \(\phi_y\) are those at the mid-surface, \(z = d\), and the prescribed rotations at \(z = 0\) include the transverse shear, \(\theta_x = \phi_x - (4 d^2/h^2)(\phi_x + w_{,x})\), see Prescribed rotations. The rotations are prescribed only with pd_method='null-space', through the keyword arguments kphix, phixp, kphiy and phiyp of Shell.add_point_pd(), or kphix, funcphix, kphiy and funcphiy of the distributed ones. For example, a uniform end-shortening \(\delta\) of a plate, incremented with the load factor, with the lateral contraction free:

p.add_distr_pd_fixed_x(0., ku=1., funcu=lambda y: 0.)
p.add_distr_pd_fixed_x(p.a, ku=1., funcu=lambda y: -delta, cte=False)
p.add_point_pd(0., 0., 0, None, 1., 0., 0, None)  # v = 0 at a corner

Theory#

The connections give the homogeneous constraints \(\boldsymbol{B}_h \boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{0}\) of Eq. (2), and the prescribed displacements the inhomogeneous constraints:

(6)#\[\boldsymbol{B}_d \boldsymbol{c} = \bar{\boldsymbol{d}}_0 + \lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{d}}_1\]

where \(\bar{\boldsymbol{d}}_0\) collects the constant prescribed values, cte=True, and \(\bar{\boldsymbol{d}}_1\) those scaled by the load factor \(\lambda\), cte=False. Each row of \(\boldsymbol{B}_d\) is the prescribed component at a point, or at a Gauss-Legendre point along a prescribed line, with max(n, 4) points along \(y\) for the lines x = cte and max(m, 4) along \(x\) for the lines y = cte, see nullspace.pd_constraint_matrix(). As for the connections, the component along the line is a polynomial with that many coefficients: the constraint holds along the whole line when the prescribed function is a polynomial of degree up to max(n, 4) - 1, e.g. a uniform or a linear end-shortening; otherwise it holds at the Gauss-Legendre points, the prescribed function being interpolated by the approximation functions.

With \(\boldsymbol{B} = [\boldsymbol{B}_h; \boldsymbol{B}_d]\), every admissible configuration is

(7)#\[\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c}_r + \boldsymbol{c}_{p0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{c}_{p1}\]

where \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) spans the null space of \(\boldsymbol{B}\) and \(\boldsymbol{c}_{p0}\), \(\boldsymbol{c}_{p1}\) are particular solutions, \(\boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{c}_{p0} = \{\boldsymbol{0}; \bar{\boldsymbol{d}}_0\}\) and \(\boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{c}_{p1} = \{\boldsymbol{0}; \bar{\boldsymbol{d}}_1\}\), see MultiDomain.get_cp(). They are obtained in the same elimination as \(\boldsymbol{Z}\), see nullspace.Elimination: the constraints are eliminated one block at a time, the connections first, each block written in the independent Ritz constants left by the previous ones, \(\boldsymbol{B} (\boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c}_r + \boldsymbol{c}_p) = \boldsymbol{b}\), with the right-hand side \(\boldsymbol{b}\) of the block, and decomposed with a QR decomposition with column pivoting, which gives both the new \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) and the correction of \(\boldsymbol{c}_p\) for the right-hand side \(\boldsymbol{b} - \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{c}_p\). The data of the decompositions are kept, such that \(\boldsymbol{c}_{p0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{c}_{p1}\) are computed again without any decomposition when only the prescribed values change, e.g. in a loop over the end-shortening; \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) changes only with the positions and the components prescribed.

Substituting Eq. (7) into the residual of the unconstrained system, \(\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{c}, \lambda) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), and projecting it with \(\boldsymbol{Z}^\top\) gives the reduced problem solved by structsolve:

(8)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{F}_{int, r}(\boldsymbol{c}_r, \lambda) &= \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{c}) \\ \boldsymbol{K}_{T, r} &= \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_T(\boldsymbol{c}) \boldsymbol{Z} = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}_r}{\partial \boldsymbol{c}_r} \\ -\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}_r}{\partial \lambda} &= \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \left(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext} - \boldsymbol{K}_T(\boldsymbol{c}) \boldsymbol{c}_{p1}\right)\end{split}\]

\(\boldsymbol{K}_{T, r}\) is the exact Jacobian, and \(-\partial \boldsymbol{R}_r/\partial \lambda\) depends on the configuration and on the load factor. structsolve passes the load factor to calc_fext when it declares the keyword argument lbd, and uses \(-\partial \boldsymbol{R}_r/\partial \lambda\) in the predictors and in the constraint of the arc-length methods, and \(\lambda \partial \boldsymbol{R}_r/\partial \lambda\) as a reference of the convergence criteria, Crisfield’s, the default, and the relative one, because under a prescribed displacement alone both \(\boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) and \(\boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_{int}\) vanish at equilibrium, see MultiDomain.get_reduced_functions().

The linear static analysis solves

\[\boldsymbol{K}_r \boldsymbol{c}_r = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \left(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} - \boldsymbol{K} (\boldsymbol{c}_{p0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{c}_{p1})\right)\]

see MultiDomain.linear_static(), the loads of calc_fext(inc=1.) being scaled by \(\lambda\) as in the analyses of structsolve. The linear buckling analysis about the pre-buckling state \(\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}(\lambda) = \hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_0 + \lambda \hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_1\), with \(\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_0\) due to the constant prescribed displacements and \(\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_1\) due to the load and the incremented ones, solves \((\boldsymbol{K}_r + \boldsymbol{K}_{G, r}(\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_0) + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_{G, r}(\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_1)) \boldsymbol{\phi}_r = \boldsymbol{0}\), see MultiDomain.linear_buckling(); the constant stress resultants Nxx, Nyy and Nxy of the panels are part of the constant state, in \(\boldsymbol{K}_{G, r}(\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_0)\) only. The modes satisfy the homogeneous constraints, \(\boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{\phi}_r\): under displacement control the prescribed components of the modes are zero, whereas under the equivalent load they are free. For a flat plate the modes of the linear buckling analysis are out of plane and both critical loads are equal; for a cylinder, whose modes couple the membrane and bending displacements, the critical end-shortening is higher than the end-shortening of the critical axial load with x2u=1.

Reactions#

With \(\boldsymbol{r}(\boldsymbol{c}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{c}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) the residual of the unconstrained system, the force applied by the supports on the structure, the generalized reaction work-conjugate to the set \(j\) of prescribed displacements, the constant or the incremented ones, is

(9)#\[P_j = \boldsymbol{c}_{pj}^\top \boldsymbol{r}(\boldsymbol{c}, \lambda)\]

see MultiDomain.reaction_pd(). It does not depend on the choice of the particular solution, since \(\boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{r} = \boldsymbol{0}\) at equilibrium. \(P_j \delta\) is the work of the reactions for the prescribed values multiplied by \(\delta\): for a uniform end-shortening \(\delta\) of an edge with the total axial reaction \(N\), \(P_1 = N \delta\), and \(P_1 = \partial U/\partial \lambda\) is the force conjugate to the load factor of a displacement-controlled analysis.

The resultant of the reactions along selected prescribed lines or points, in the direction \(u\), \(v\) or \(w\) of the panels, is \(F_u = \boldsymbol{c}_u^\top \boldsymbol{r}\), with \(\boldsymbol{c}_u\) a solution of the constraints for a unit translation \(u = 1\) of the selected prescriptions that prescribe \(u\), all the other prescribed values being zero, see MultiDomain.reaction_resultant(). A line whose ends are shared with a prescription that is not selected, directly or through a connection, has no resultant of its own, the force at the shared corner being undetermined, and an error is raised: for instance, the total axial force at the end of a cylinder made of several panels is the resultant of all the lines of that end. The sign is that of the force applied by the support on the structure, \(F_u = -N\) at the shortened end.

Redundant and inconsistent prescriptions#

As for the connections, a constraint that is a linear combination of the previous ones is redundant and dropped, e.g. at the corner where a prescribed edge meets a connection or another prescribed edge, or on a term already removed by a boundary condition flag. A redundant constraint is dropped only when its right-hand side is consistent, i.e. satisfied by the particular solution within rhs_tol of MultiDomain.get_Z(), \(10^{-8}\) by default, relative to the largest prescribed value. Otherwise MultiDomain.get_cp() raises a ValueError that names the prescription, the panel, the boundary condition flags that restrain it, and the connections and prescriptions imposed before it on the same or connected panels:

  • a prescribed edge meeting another one with a different value at a corner, or across a connection;

  • a non-zero value on a term removed by a boundary condition flag, e.g. \(u = \delta\) along x = a with x2u = 0: the message tells to set the flag to 1, whereas \(u = 0\) is consistent and dropped;

  • the constant and the incremented sets must each be consistent by themselves, since the constraints hold for every \(\lambda\): the same line cannot have a constant and an incremented prescription of the same component;

  • a function that is not a polynomial, prescribed along a line whose end is restrained by a flag, is consistent only within the interpolation error, which may exceed rhs_tol with few terms.

API#

  • MultiDomain(..., pd_method='null-space'), the default

  • MultiDomain.get_Z() and MultiDomain.get_cp(): \(\boldsymbol{Z}\), \(\boldsymbol{c}_{p0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{c}_{p1}\), also in the attributes Z, c_p0 and c_p1

  • MultiDomain.expand(): expand(c_r, lbd) returns the configuration of Eq. (7), expand(c_r) returns \(\boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c}_r\), e.g. for the modes

  • MultiDomain.get_reduced_functions(): the callables of structsolve.Analysis for the non-linear analyses, Newton-Raphson and the arc-length methods. The linear static analysis of Analysis.static(NLgeom=False) cannot contain the constant prescribed displacements and raises an error, see MultiDomain.linear_static()

  • MultiDomain.linear_static() and MultiDomain.linear_buckling()

  • MultiDomain.reaction_pd() and MultiDomain.reaction_resultant()

  • MultiDomain.calc_fext() excludes the forces of the prescribed displacements with pd_method='null-space'

Displacement control#

Under the uniform end-shortening \(\delta\) of Eq. (1), the membrane patch test is passed exactly, for one domain or for connected domains: the fields are \(u = -\delta x/a\) and \(v = \nu \delta y/a\) to the round-off, and the reactions are \(P_1 = N \delta\), Eq. (9), and \(F_u = -N\) at the shortened end, with \(N = E h b \delta/a\).

Beyond the critical end-shortening \(\delta_{cr}\) of the linear buckling analysis, the post-buckling path is traced under displacement control with the reduced functions of MultiDomain.get_reduced_functions(), the end-shortening being incremented, cte=False, and a small lateral load acting as an imperfection. Every converged increment of the Newton-Raphson and arc-length methods of structsolve satisfies \(\|\boldsymbol{Z}^\top \boldsymbol{r}\| \le\) relTOL \(\|\lambda \, \partial \boldsymbol{R}_r/\partial \lambda\|\). The arc length of structsolve is scaled by the linear solution, which has no out-of-plane displacement under an end-shortening: the post-buckling path is long in these units, and maxArcLength may need to be increased.

Penalty vs null-space displacement control#

The penalty method imposes a prescribed displacement \(u_p\) with the energy \(\frac{1}{2} k \int (u - u_p)^2\), i.e. a spring of stiffness \(k\) between the structure and the support, see fkCld_xcte() and fkCpd(): the structure is more flexible, the prescribed value is not reached, and the error decreases with \(1/k\), while the condition number of the stiffness matrix grows with \(k\). The null-space method has no penalty constant.

The reaction of the penalty method, \(k \int (u_p - u)\), is the product of a large constant and a small difference, and loses accuracy by cancellation for the highest constants. In a geometrically non-linear analysis the penalty forces \(k u_p\) are part of \(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) and \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\), the references of the convergence criteria of structsolve: the relative criterion measures them with the Euclidean norm over all the degrees of freedom, which grows with \(k\), Crisfield’s criterion with the norm \(\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} = (\sum_j v_j^2/|d_j|)^{1/2}\) scaled by the diagonal \(\boldsymbol{d}\) of the linear stiffness matrix, which contains the penalty stiffness, such that the scaled norm of the penalty forces grows only with \(\sqrt{k}\). Once the tolerance dominates, the error of the penalty method stops decreasing with \(1/k\), earlier with the relative criterion. The null-space method has no such trade-off, its reference being \(\lambda \partial \boldsymbol{R}_r/\partial \lambda\), see Prescribed displacements imposed exactly. Its \(\partial \boldsymbol{R}_r/\partial \lambda\) requires the product \(\boldsymbol{K}_T \boldsymbol{c}_{p1}\), computed without assembling \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\), see Tangent stiffness matrix and its product by a vector, at the cost of one evaluation of the internal force vector; the linear analyses cost the same with both methods. With \(k = 10^4 E h\) the penalty method is within \(10^{-3}\) of the null-space method for the linear and non-linear analyses of a plate under end-shortening.

When to use each method. The null-space method is exact, needs no penalty constant, keeps the conditioning of the model, and gives reactions that do not depend on any constant; it is recommended for displacement control, e.g. the end-shortening of panels and shells, and for linear buckling under prescribed displacements, and it is the default of the prescribed displacements. The penalty method, pd_method='penalty', is needed when the prescribed displacement represents a physical stiffness, e.g. a load cell or a loading fixture, or for models that rely on the penalty forms, e.g. the cohesive zone examples of Cohesive zone model of the multi-domain approach, whose reaction is given by MultiDomain.reaction_line_pd_xcte().

Limitations#

A connection imposed exactly constrains the domains exactly as the penalty method does with infinite penalty constants, which may constrain the approximation more than the penalty method with the default constants does:

  • When the two domains have a different number of terms along the connection, the terms of the trace of the richer domain that the other domain cannot represent are forced to vanish.

  • The boundary condition flags at the ends of an edge of one domain are imposed on the other domain.

  • With 'SB' and the classical laminated plate theory, \(u_1 - z_1 w_{,x} = u_2 - z_2 w_{,x}\), \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) being the faces in contact measured from the reference surface of each domain. When the reference surfaces of the two domains are apart, \(z_1 \ne z_2\), e.g. without offsets, restraining the normal in-plane displacement of an edge in both domains, e.g. \(u = 0\) at \(x = 0\), also restrains the rotation \(w_{,x}\) at that edge, which is clamped. With offsets that place both reference surfaces at the same height, \(z_1 = z_2\) and \(u_1 = u_2\). The in-plane displacements of the bonded laminates must be restrained consistently with the single laminate that they represent, e.g. restraining only the tangential displacement of the edges.

The prescribed displacements of Shell.add_point_pd(), Shell.add_distr_pd_fixed_x() and Shell.add_distr_pd_fixed_y() are imposed exactly with pd_method='null-space', the default, see Prescribed displacements imposed exactly, and with penalty stiffnesses with pd_method='penalty'. The traction-separation law of 'SB_TSL' remains a penalty stiffness.

The rank of the constraints is found with the tolerance tol of MultiDomain.get_Z(), 1e-9 by default, which applies to the constraints normalized to a unit norm. The independent constraints have pivots many orders of magnitude above the round-off errors of the redundant ones, and a warning is issued when this gap is smaller than \(10^4\).

API#

The methods of MultiDomain for the null-space method are MultiDomain.get_Z(), MultiDomain.reduce(), MultiDomain.expand() and MultiDomain.get_reduced_functions(), and for the prescribed displacements MultiDomain.get_cp(), MultiDomain.linear_static(), MultiDomain.linear_buckling(), MultiDomain.reaction_pd() and MultiDomain.reaction_resultant(), see Prescribed displacements imposed exactly.
