Multidomain connections#

Each domain of a MultiDomain assembly is a Shell with its own set of Bardell approximation functions, see Bardell’s hierarchical functions. The domains are joined by connections, given to the assembly as dictionaries with the key 'func', e.g. 'SSycte', see MultiDomain.get_kC_conn(). This page describes the connections currently supported. They are imposed exactly with the null-space method by default, see Exact connections with the null-space method and panels.multidomain.connections.nullspace, or with the penalty stiffnesses of the kernels below with conn_method='penalty'.

Penalty method#

Each penalty kernel adds, for each quantity \(q_k\) that must be continuous across the connection, the energy:

(1)#\[U_k = \frac{1}{2} k_k \int \left( q_k^{(1)} - q_k^{(2)} \right)^2\]

integrated along the connected edges, or over the connected area for 'SB', with the translation or rotation penalty constant \(k_k\), kt or kr [castro2017Multidomain]. The quantities \(q_k\) of each connection are given in the sections below.

With the null-space method the quantities penalized by the kernels become the linear constraints:

(2)#\[\boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

which are eliminated with a basis \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) of their null space, \(\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c}_r\). The results are the limit of the penalty method for penalty constants going to infinity, see Exact connections with the null-space method.

Base-flange, BF#

kCBFycte#

Connection of type:

                     _
                      |
  || --> Flange       |
  ||                  |-> Can be used to model a stiffener
======  --> Base      |
                     _|

ycte indicates the connection exists at a constant \(y_1\) for panel 1 (base) and \(y_2\) for panel 2 (flange), see panels.multidomain.connections.kCBFycte. The connection 'BFxcte', along \(y\), is the same at a constant \(x_1\) and \(x_2\), see panels.multidomain.connections.kCBFxcte. In both, p1 is the base and p2 the flange.

The rotation penalty of 'BFycte' uses the rotation of the normal about \(x\) of each panel, \(-w_{,y}\) for the classical laminated plate theory, including the term \(v/r\) for the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, and \(\phi_y\) for the FSDT and TSDT; the connection 'BFxcte' penalizes \(-w_{,x}\) or \(\phi_x\).

BFxcte at a face of the base#

Both connections tie the flange to the reference surface of the base. A flange attached to a face of the base, e.g. the web of a ring frame on the inner or the outer surface of a cylinder, is connected with the key 'face' of 'BFxcte', 'lower' for the face \(z_1 = d_1 - h_1/2\) and 'upper' for \(z_1 = d_1 + h_1/2\), \(d_1\) being the offset and \(h_1\) the thickness of the base:

           base, z1 up
====================================  upper face, z1 = d1 + h1/2
------------------------------------  reference surface, z1 = 0
====================================  lower face, z1 = d1 - h1/2
             ||  edge x2 = 0
             ||  flange, x2 = -z1
             ||

The words 'lower' and 'upper' refer to the coordinate \(z\) of the domain whose face is connected, as for 'SB' below, where the lower face of p1 meets the upper face of p2: for 'BFxcte' it is the coordinate \(z_1\) of the base p1. For a 'cylshell_*' base, whose \(z\) points outwards, 'lower' is the inner surface and 'upper' the outer surface, whatever the axes of the flange, e.g. an 'annular_*' plate.

The axis \(x_2\) of the flange is \(-z_1\), so that a flange below the lower face has its edge \(x_2 = 0\) there, xcte2=0, and a flange above the upper face its edge \(x_2 = a_2\), xcte2=p2.a. The face is a line of points of the normals of the base, which follow its reference surface with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics for a 'cylshell_*' base, whatever its model, see below, and with the kinematics of its model otherwise; for the classical laminated plate theory

\[u_1(z) = u_1 - z w_{1,x}, \qquad v_1(z) = \left(1 + \frac{z}{r_1}\right) v_1 - z w_{1,y}\]

the term \(z v_1/r_1\) being that of the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, and the connection imposes \(u_1(z_1) = w_2\), \(v_1(z_1) = v_2\), \(w_1 = -u_2\) and the continuity of the rotation about \(y\), with the penalty energy integrated along the length \(b_1\) of the base, or exactly with the null-space method. The two edges share the natural coordinate \(\eta\) and must span the same angle: an 'annular_*' flange on a 'cylshell_*' base, whose \(z\) points outwards, has its edge at the radius of the connected surface, \(r_2 - x_{2} = r_1 + z_1\), and \(b_2/r_2 = b_1/r_1\); the connection raises a ValueError otherwise. For example, the internal and the external ring frames of a cylinder cyl at the station x_f:

dict(p1=cyl, p2=web_in, func='BFxcte', xcte1=x_f, xcte2=0,
     face='lower')
dict(p1=cyl, p2=web_out, func='BFxcte', xcte1=x_f, xcte2=web_out.a,
     face='upper')

with web_in.r = cyl.r + cyl.offset - h/2 and web_out.r - web_out.a = cyl.r + cyl.offset + h/2. With the offset \(d_1 = h_1/2\) the lower face is the reference surface and face='lower' is the connection without the key.

Rigid rotation at a face of a Donnell cylinder#

A rigid rotation of the assembly by the small angle \(\Omega\) about the axis of a cylinder moves each point by \(\rho \Omega\) along the circumference, \(\rho\) being its distance to the axis. At the reference surface of the cylinder, of radius \(r_1\), the displacements are

\[u_1 = 0, \qquad v_1 = r_1 \Omega, \qquad w_1 = 0\]

for every model. The membrane strains \(u_{1,x}\), \(v_{1,y} + w_1/r_1\), \(u_{1,y} + v_{1,x}\) and the changes of curvature \(-w_{1,xx}\), \(-w_{1,yy}\), \(-2 w_{1,xy}\) of the Donnell kinematics vanish, as do those of the Sanders-Koiter kinematics and, with \(\phi_x = \phi_y = 0\), the transverse shear strains of the FSDT and TSDT: the rotation about the axis is free of strain in every model of the cylinder. The face \(z_1 = z_f\), at the radius \(r_1 + z_f\), moves by \((r_1 + z_f) \Omega\). The rotation of the normal about \(x\) of the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, \(-w_{1,y} + v_1/r_1\), gives this displacement exactly,

\[v_1(z_f) = \left(1 + \frac{z_f}{r_1}\right) v_1 - z_f w_{1,y} = (r_1 + z_f) \Omega\]

whereas the Donnell kinematics, which neglect \(v_1/r_1\) in the rotation of the normal, give \(v_1(z_f) = v_1 - z_f w_{1,y} = r_1 \Omega\). The flange, an 'annular_*' plate whose connected edge is at the radius \(r_1 + z_f\), rotates with \(v_2 = (r_1 + z_f) \Omega\) along that edge, and the condition \(v_1(z_f) = v_2\) of the connection has the residual

\[v_1(z_f) - v_2 = -z_f \Omega, \qquad \frac{\left| v_1(z_f) - v_2 \right|}{v_2} = \frac{|z_f|}{r_1 + z_f}\]

of the order of \(h_1/(2 r_1)\) for a face at \(|z_f| = h_1/2\). The other conditions of the connection have no residual. With the penalty method the rigid rotation stores the energy \(\frac{1}{2} k_t b_1 z_f^2 \Omega^2\); with the null-space method it violates the constraints, and the motion free of strain that satisfies them rotates the flange by \(r_1 \Omega/(r_1 + z_f)\): its edge follows the reference surface of the cylinder instead of the face. The translations normal to the axis and the tilts of the axis have \(v_1 \neq 0\) as well, with the same residual \(-z_f v_1/r_1\) at the face.

The term \(z_f v_1/r_1\) is of the order neglected by the Donnell kinematics, so that either choice is consistent with these kinematics, but only the Sanders-Koiter term at the face keeps the rigid rotation of the assembly free of energy and of residual. It enters the connection alone and does not change the strains of the cylinder, which remain those of its model. The key 'kinematics' of 'BFxcte' therefore defaults to 'sanders', the face following the Sanders-Koiter kinematics for every 'cylshell_*' model. With kinematics='model' the face follows the kinematics of the model of the cylinder, which differ from those of Sanders and Koiter only for 'cylshell_clpt_donnell', 'cylshell_fsdt_donnell' and 'cylshell_tsdt_donnell'; for these the residual above is present, and the connection issues a UserWarning that names this section, see kCBFxcte_face.face_rinv(). The key 'kinematics' without the key 'face' raises a ValueError: at the reference surface the kinematics of the face do not enter the connection.

BFycte at a face of the base#

The connection 'BFycte' has the same keys 'face' and 'kinematics', with the same meaning: the edge \(y_2 =\) ycte2 of the flange is tied to the line \(y_1 =\) ycte1 of the face \(z_1 = d_1 \mp h_1/2\) of the base, see panels.multidomain.connections.kCBFycte_face. On a 'cylshell_*' base this line is a generator, and the flange is a flat plate along it, e.g. a blade stringer or the web of a Z stringer, whose height then starts at the surface of the skin instead of its middle surface:

           skin, z1 up (outwards)
====================================  outer face, 'upper'
------------------------------------  reference surface, z1 = 0
====================================  inner face, 'lower'
             ||  edge y2 = 0
             ||  blade, y2 = -z1
             ||

The axis \(y_2\) of the flange is \(-z_1\): a flange below the lower face has its edge \(y_2 = 0\) there, ycte2=0, and a flange above the upper face its edge \(y_2 = b_2\), ycte2=p2.b. The connection imposes \(u_1(z_1) = u_2\), \(v_1(z_1) = w_2\), \(w_1 = -v_2\) and the equality of the rotations of the normals about \(x\), that of the base at its face being

\[\theta_{y1}(z) = \frac{\partial v_1(z)}{\partial z} = -w_{1,y} + \frac{v_1}{r_1}\]

for the classical laminated plate theory, the same at every \(z\), with the term \(v_1/r_1\) of the Sanders-Koiter kinematics whenever \(v_1(z)\) has the term \(z v_1/r_1\), and \(\partial v_1/\partial z\) of the displacement field of the FSDT and TSDT, which at a face of a TSDT laminate is \(-w_{1,y} + v_1/r_1\), the transverse shear strain vanishing there. A rigid rotation \(\Omega\) about the axis of the cylinder, \(v_1 = r_1 \Omega\), moves the face by \((r_1 + z_1) \Omega\) and rotates its normal by \(\Omega\), as the blade rotating with it, whose root is at the radius \(r_1 + z_1\): the motion is free of penalty energy and of residual. With kinematics='model' a Donnell cylinder leaves the residuals \(-z_1 \Omega\) and \(-\Omega\); the second is that of the connection at the reference surface of a Donnell cylinder, whose rotation of the normal neglects \(v/r\). The two edges share the natural coordinate \(\xi\) and have the same length, p1.a == p2.a.

The blade builder create_cylinder_blade_stiffened() and the Z builder create_cylinder_z_stiffened() connect the blades and the webs at the face on the side of the stringer, 'lower' for internal and 'upper' for external stringers, so that the given height of a blade, or of a web, is measured from the surface of the skin, or from the face of the attached flange of a Z stringer.

kCBFxycte#

Connection of type, seen along the common edge, for the default angle of 90 degrees:

               ^ z1 = y2
               |
               |  p2, e.g. a rib, edge y2 = 0
               |
===============o     o: the common edge, y1 = x2 (into the page)
p1, e.g. a spar, edge x1 = a1       ---> x1 = z2

between the edge \(x_1 =\) xcte1 of p1 and the edge \(y_2 =\) ycte2 of p2, the same straight line, with \(y_1 = x_2\) and p1.b == p2.a, the two domains at the angle 'angle', by default 90 degrees, measured from the normal of p1 to that of p2 about the common axis. It joins, for example, a spar p1, whose edge at the end of a bay is \(x_1 = a_1\), to a rib p2, whose edges \(y_2 = 0\) or \(y_2 = b_2\) run along the height of a wing-box, when the spar is connected to the skins with 'BFycte' and the rib with 'BFxcte'. The displacements of p2 are rotated to the frame of p1, and the rotations about the common axis are equal: \(-w_{1,x}\) of p1 and \(w_{2,y}\) of p2, with \(-v_2/r_2\) for the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, or \(\theta_{x1}\) and \(-\theta_{y2}\) for the FSDT and TSDT, see panels.multidomain.connections.kCBFxycte.

Skin-base, SB#

Connection of type:

======        ==> base, p1
------        --> skin, p2
                |
                v -z

The two panels share the axes \(x, y, z\), \(z\) being normal to their reference surfaces, and p1 is the panel on the positive side of the interface along \(z\), p2 the one on the negative side, whatever their roles or their order in the assembly: the lower face \(z_1 = d_1 - h_1/2\) of p1 is connected to the upper face \(z_2 = d_2 + h_2/2\) of p2, with the offsets \(d_1\), \(d_2\) of the laminates, \(u_1(z_1) = u_2(z_2)\), \(v_1(z_1) = v_2(z_2)\) and \(w_1 = w_2\), each panel with its own kinematics, for all the models, see panels.multidomain.connections.skin_base. The sketch shows a stiffener base on the side of positive \(z\) of the skin; a pad-up on the side of negative \(z\) of a skin is p2, with the skin p1. The same holds for 'SB_TSL'.

The connection 'SB' penalizes the displacements \(u(z), v(z), w\) of the lower face of p1 and of the upper face of p2, with the offsets of the laminates and the kinematics of each panel, with its slope and, with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, the term \(v/r\), see kCsdt._sb_components() and fkCSB(). The rotation penalty 'kr' of 'SB' applies only to the FSDT and TSDT, whose rotations are independent of \(u, v, w\): the rotations of the normal at the reference surfaces are then penalized with kr. The rotations of the classical laminated plate theory follow from \(w\), already tied by \(w_1 = w_2\), and tying them too would over-constrain the connection with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics.

kCSSycte#

Connection of type:

           /-> (connection at y1=ycte1)
          /
         /  /->(connection at y2=ycte2)
_________| |_________
|        | |        |

Damaged skin-base connection#

The connection 'SB_TSL' has a traction-separation law at the interface, see Cohesive zone model of the multi-domain approach, panels.multidomain.connections.kCSB_dmg and panels.multidomain.connections.tsl. Its out-of-plane stiffness is that of the cohesive interface, with the damage mapped by the traction-separation law of calc_kw_tsl(), and it keeps this penalty stiffness also with conn_method='null-space'. The damaged connections were implemented by D’Souza [nathan2024MSc].

Models based on shear deformation theories#

The kernels above, apart from fkCSB() of 'SB', which serves all the models, assume the 3 DOFs \(u, v, w\) of the models based on the classical laminated plate theory. The connections 'SSxcte' and 'SSycte' of the plate and cylindrical shell models based on the FSDT and TSDT, with the 5 DOFs \(u, v, w, \phi_x, \phi_y\), are in panels.multidomain.connections.kCsdt, with fkCSB_sdt(), the matrix of fkCSB() for these models, and the connections 'BFycte' and 'BFxcte' of these models are the functions fkCBFycte*_sdt and fkCBFxcte*_sdt of panels.multidomain.connections.kCBFycte and panels.multidomain.connections.kCBFxcte. The coupling block of 'BFxycte', fkCBFxycte12(), serves the models with 3 and with 5 DOFs, with the blocks fkCBFxycte11_sdt() and fkCBFxycte22_sdt() of each panel.

Exact connections: the null-space method#

With MultiDomain(..., conn_method='null-space'), the default, the connections 'SSxcte', 'SSycte', 'BFxcte', 'BFycte', 'BFxycte' and 'SB' are not added as penalty stiffnesses, but imposed exactly: the quantities penalized by the kernels above become the linear constraints of Eq. (2) on the Ritz constants, which are eliminated from them. No penalty constant is needed, see panels.multidomain.connections.nullspace.

Prescribed displacements with penalties#

With MultiDomain(..., pd_method='penalty') a displacement prescribed at a point or along a line, see Shell.add_point_pd(), Shell.add_distr_pd_fixed_x() and Shell.add_distr_pd_fixed_y(), is imposed with a spring between the panel and the support: the penalty stiffness is added to the stiffness matrix with the kernels of panels.multidomain.connections.kCpd, for the models with 3 and with 5 DOFs per term, and the force of the spring is in the external force vector of shell_fext(). The in-plane displacements are those at the thickness coordinate z of the kernels, the one given to the methods of Shell, by default the reference surface. With pd_method='null-space', the default, the prescribed displacements are imposed exactly, see nullspace.pd_constraint_matrix().

Penalty constants#

Function calc_kt_kr() calculates the penalty constants for translation (kt) and rotation (kr) from the laminates of the two connected panels, based on [castro2017Multidomain], with a strain compatibility criterion. The aim is to have penalty constants that are just high enough to produce the desired compatibility, but not too high such that numerical stability issues start to appear. The penalty constants are not used by the connections imposed with the null-space method, apart from the key 'kr' of 'SB', which tells whether the rotations of the FSDT and TSDT are connected. For 'SB' the function gives no kr.

The discretisation of the cohesive domain of a 'SB_TSL' connection follows from tsl_resolution(), tsl_margin() and tsl_discretization(), see panels.multidomain.connections.tsl.
