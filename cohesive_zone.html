Cohesive zone model of the multi-domain approach#

The area connection 'SB_TSL' of MultiDomain.get_kC_conn() connects two skins over an area with a traction-separation law (TSL) at the interface, such that the progressive delamination between them can be simulated. It originates from the MSc thesis of D’Souza (2024) [nathan2024MSc], Multi-Domain Semi-Analytical Cohesive Zone Approach. This page describes the model as implemented and documents where the implementation departs from the thesis. Equation numbers of the form “Eq. 5.12” refer to the thesis. Each deviation states the problem, its consequence on the load-displacement curve of the double cantilever beam (DCB), and what the implementation does now.

The implementation is found in:

  • the kernels fkCSB11_dmg(), fkCSB12_dmg() and fkCSB22_dmg() of panels/multidomain/connections/kCSB_dmg.pyx, and calc_kw_tsl();

  • the rules of the discretisation of the cohesive domain, tsl_resolution(), tsl_margin() and tsl_discretization(), see the convergence study below;

  • the methods of MultiDomain: MultiDomain.get_kC_conn(), MultiDomain.calc_k_dmg(), MultiDomain.correct_separation(), MultiDomain.calc_kT_TSL(), MultiDomain.reaction_line_pd_xcte() and MultiDomain.force_out_plane_damage();

  • the nonlinear driver tests/multidomain/test_dcb_damage.py and its counterpart for the validation notebooks, notebooks/dcb_utils.py.

The model of the thesis is the DCB of its Section 7.3: \(L = 65\) mm, \(b = 25\) mm, \(a_0 = 48\) mm, arms \([0]_{15}\) with ply thickness 0.14 mm of AS4D/PEKK-FC, \(G_{Ic} = 1.12\) N/mm, three domains per arm, of which the first one, with length \(a_1 = L - a_0 = 17\) mm, carries the cohesive zone. It is the model of the sections below up to the solution procedure and of tests/multidomain/test_dcb_damage.py. The convergence study and the validation use the model of notebooks/dcb_utils.py, in which the cohesive domain is followed by a perfect bond, on the longer reference DCB of the convergence study.

Traction-separation law and damage#

The law implemented is bilinear, with one damage variable \(d\) that degrades the three components of the traction:

(1)#\[\tau_\alpha = k_o \left(1 - d\right) \Delta_\alpha, \qquad \alpha = u, v, w\]

where \(k_o\) is the penalty stiffness of the pristine interface (key 'k_o' of the connection) and \(\Delta_\alpha\) are the separations of Eq. (5). The separation at damage onset is \(\Delta_o = \tau_o/k_o\) and the separation at complete failure is \(\Delta_f = 2 G_{Ic}/\tau_o\), with the keys 'tau_o' and 'G1c' of the connection. The damage is driven by the normal separation after the correction described in Correction of the separation field, \(\bar\Delta_w\), and it is irreversible:

(2)#\[\begin{split}d = \max\left( d^{h}, \hat d(\bar\Delta_w) \right), \qquad \hat d(\Delta) = \begin{cases} 0 & \Delta \le \Delta_o \\ \dfrac{\Delta_f (\Delta - \Delta_o)}{(\Delta_f - \Delta_o) \Delta} & \Delta_o < \Delta < \Delta_f \\ 1 & \Delta \ge \Delta_f \end{cases}\end{split}\]

where \(d^h\) is the damage of the last converged increment, stored in MultiDomain.dmg_index by MultiDomain.update_TSL_history(). The damage \(\hat d\) is computed by calc_kw_tsl() and the maximum with the history, together with the degraded stiffness \(k_o (1 - d)\) at each integration point, by MultiDomain.calc_k_dmg(). The area under the softening branch of the law is \(G_{Ic}\).

Correction of the separation field#

Section 6.3.1 of the thesis describes the correction of the separation used to compute the damage as: start at the point of maximum separation, location \(A\), move in the direction of decreasing separation up to the first non-positive separation, location \(B\), and set the separation to zero beyond \(B\). The previous implementation set to zero, for each row \(y =\) cte, everything from \(x = 0\) up to the last non-positive separation of the row. When the approximation functions give a non-positive value between \(A\) and the edge of the domain, for instance a small oscillation at \(x = a_1\), the whole row was set to zero, including the fracture process zone. MultiDomain.correct_separation() now implements the description of the thesis, for a crack front that advances along \(-x\):

(3)#\[\begin{split}\Delta^{corr}_{i,j} = \begin{cases} 0 & j \le B_i \\ \Delta_{i,j} & j > B_i \end{cases} \qquad B_i = \max \left\{ j \le A_i : \Delta_{i,j} \le 0 \right\}, \quad A_i = \arg\max_j \Delta_{i,j}\end{split}\]

with \(i\) the row and \(j\) the column of the grid of integration points, \(j\) increasing with \(x\). Rows without non-positive values between \(x = 0\) and \(A_i\) are not modified, and neither are rows without any positive separation. The negative values of the separation are not only numerical: for a beam on an elastic foundation the separation behind the crack front oscillates with a decaying amplitude, and its first negative lobe does not change when the number of terms of the cohesive domain is increased from 10 to 20.

The corrected separation is used only to compute the damage. The tractions, the internal force vector and the stiffness matrices use the full separation.

Separations at the interface#

The compatibility of the area connection in the thesis, Eqs. 4.57–4.62, assumes \(w^i_{,x} = w^j_{,x}\) and \(w^i_{,y} = w^j_{,y}\) (Eq. 4.61) to write the in-plane compatibility with the slope of the top panel only, Eq. 4.62:

(4)#\[\Delta u^{thesis} = u^t + (d^t + d^b) \, w^t_{,x} - u^b\]

This holds for a perfect bond, but not inside the fracture process zone, where the two arms rotate in opposite directions. There, Eq. (4) gives a spurious tangential separation, penalised with the same \(k^w_{CZ}\), which stiffens the interface exactly where it should open. The implementation uses the displacement jump between the two surfaces in contact, with the slope of each panel:

(5)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \Delta_u &= u^t + d^t w^t_{,x} - u^b + d^b w^b_{,x} \\ \Delta_v &= v^t + d^t w^t_{,y} - v^b + d^b w^b_{,y} \\ \Delta_w &= w^t - w^b \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

where \(d^t\) and \(d^b\) are the distances from the reference surfaces of the top and bottom panels to the interface, \(u^t, v^t, w^t\) and \(u^b, v^b, w^b\) the displacements of their reference surfaces. For cylindrical panels with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, \(v(z) = v + z (v/r - w_{,y})\), and \(w_{,y}\) is replaced by \(w_{,y} - v/r\) in \(\Delta_v\), with the radius \(r\) of the reference surface of each panel, see MultiDomain._tsl_operators() and panels.multidomain.connections.kCSB_dmg. With the thicknesses \(h^t\), \(h^b\) and the offsets \(d_t\), \(d_b\) of the laminates, Shell.offset, each laminate occupying \(d - h/2 \le z \le d + h/2\) in the coordinate \(z\) of its panel, \(d^t = h^t/2 - d_t\) and \(d^b = h^b/2 + d_b\), see kCsdt._sb_distances(); without offsets, as in the DCB examples, they are \(h^t/2\) and \(h^b/2\), the distances from the mid-planes. Top and bottom refer to the \(z\) axis, normal to the reference surfaces and common to both panels, pointing up in the DCB: the top panel is the panel on the positive side of the interface along \(z\), whose face \(z = -d^t\) is at the interface, and it is p1 of the connection dictionary; the bottom panel is the one on the negative side, whose face \(z = +d^b\) is at the interface, and it is p2, whatever their order in the assembly, see MultiDomain.get_kC_conn(). With these three separations and the degraded stiffness \(k^w_{CZ} = k_o (1 - d)\), the penalty energy of Eq. 5.1 becomes:

(6)#\[U^{tb}_{pen-AR-dmg} = \frac{1}{2} \int_x \int_y k^w_{CZ} \left( \Delta_u^2 + \Delta_v^2 + \Delta_w^2 \right) dx\,dy\]

and the stiffness matrix of the connection is:

(7)#\[\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} = \int_A k^w_{CZ} \boldsymbol{B}_\Delta^\top \boldsymbol{B}_\Delta \, dA, \qquad U^{tb}_{pen-AR-dmg} = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{c}^\top \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} \boldsymbol{c}\]

where \(\boldsymbol{B}_\Delta\) is the operator that gives the three separations of Eq. (5) from the Ritz constants \(\boldsymbol{c}\). For a frozen damage field, the internal force of the connection is \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\boldsymbol{c}\), see Eq. (8). The integral is evaluated with a Gauss-Legendre rule of nr_x_gauss \(\times\) nr_y_gauss points over the domain of the top panel, and \(k^w_{CZ}\) is given at each of these points. The terms of the kernels that are new or modified with respect to the thesis are listed in the docstrings of fkCSB11_dmg(), fkCSB12_dmg() and fkCSB22_dmg(). The undamaged connection 'SB', fkCSB(), imposes the jump of Eq. (5) at zero separation, such that the pristine 'SB_TSL', with \(k^w_{CZ} = k_o\) at every point, has the matrix of 'SB' with \(k_t = k_o\), as checked by tests/multidomain/test_sb_unified.py.

The damage kernels, kCSB_dmg.pyx, had the approximation functions in \(\eta\) of the top panel in the coupling block built with the flags of the edge \(y_1\) in place of \(y_2\). This had no effect on the DCB, where all \(y\) flags are 1.

Energy dissipated by crack formation#

The thesis adds the term \(U_{crack}\) of Eq. 5.18 to the total potential energy, Eq. 5.29, with the force vector \(\boldsymbol{F}_{crack}\) of Eq. 5.28 and the stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{crack}\) of Eq. 5.26. This counts the dissipated energy twice. The degradation of the penalty stiffness \(k^w_{CZ} = k_o(1-d)\) inside \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\), Eq. 5.5, already makes the traction follow the softening branch of the traction-separation law, and the area under that branch is \(G_{Ic}\), exactly as in a finite element with a cohesive law. The implementation has neither \(U_{crack}\), \(\boldsymbol{F}_{crack}\) nor \(\boldsymbol{K}_{crack}\), and the residual is:

(8)#\[\boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_{int} = \boldsymbol{F}^{panels}_{int} + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}(d)\boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\boldsymbol{c}\]

where \(\boldsymbol{F}^{panels}_{int}\) is the internal force vector of the panels, \(d\) is the damage at each integration point, Eq. (2), the largest value between the converged history and the value obtained from the current separation, and \(\boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\) is the penalty stiffness of the prescribed displacement. The sudden increase of the slope at damage onset reported in Section 7.1.4 of the thesis, and the need to drop \(\boldsymbol{K}^2_{crack}\), are consequences of the double counting.

Consistent tangent stiffness#

Writing the separations as \(\Delta_\alpha = \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha\boldsymbol{c}\), the internal force vector of the connection and its derivative are:

(9)#\[\boldsymbol{F}^{coh}_{int} = \int_A k_o (1 - d) \sum_\alpha \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha^\top \Delta_\alpha \, dA = \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\boldsymbol{c}\]
(10)#\[\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{F}^{coh}_{int}}{\partial \boldsymbol{c}} = \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d} = - \int_A \chi \, k_o \, \hat d'(\Delta_w) \left( \sum_\alpha \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha^\top \Delta_\alpha \right) \boldsymbol{B}_w \, dA\]

with:

(11)#\[\begin{split}\hat d'(\Delta) = \frac{\Delta_f \Delta_o}{(\Delta_f - \Delta_o) \Delta^2}, \qquad \chi = \begin{cases} 1 & \Delta_o < \bar\Delta_w < \Delta_f \text{ and } \hat d(\bar\Delta_w) > d^h \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\end{split}\]

The thesis does not derive the tangent of the cohesive zone. With the secant stiffness \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) alone, the iteration matrix misses the change of the damage with the displacements. It overestimates the stiffness of every softening point, and the Newton-Raphson iterations converge linearly, slowly, once the crack grows: in the reference DCB, the first increment after the first fully damaged point needed more than 130 iterations.

The mask \(\chi\) selects the points where the damage grows in the current state. Where it is 1, the correction of Correction of the separation field leaves the separation unchanged, \(\bar\Delta_w = \Delta_w\) and \(\partial \bar\Delta_w / \partial \boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{B}_w\). The derivative of the correction itself, a switch between 0 and \(\Delta_w\), is zero almost everywhere. Some properties of Eq. (10):

  • \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) is not symmetric, because the tangential tractions depend on \(\Delta_w\) through \(d\), while \(d\) does not depend on \(\Delta_u\), \(\Delta_v\). The term \(\boldsymbol{B}_w^\top \Delta_w \boldsymbol{B}_w\) is symmetric and negative semi-definite; it makes the normal tangent stiffness of a softening point \(k_o (1 - d - \hat d' \Delta_w) = -k_o \Delta_o / (\Delta_f - \Delta_o)\), the slope of the descending branch of the bilinear law.

  • The iteration matrix becomes \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d} + \boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\). Under displacement control the prescribed-displacement penalty keeps it regular on the softening branch of the DCB. structsolve.solve uses scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve, which accepts non-symmetric matrices.

  • Like \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\), \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) is evaluated at every iteration, while \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\) is refreshed every \(n_{NR}\) iterations, see Solution procedure.

MultiDomain.calc_kT_TSL() evaluates \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) at the Gauss-Legendre points of the connection, with \(\boldsymbol{B}_\alpha\) built once per panel from the field functions and cached. The drivers use it by default (consistent_tangent=True in test_dcb_damage.py, always in dcb_utils.solve_dcb).

Assembly of the cohesive stiffness by matrix products#

The thesis assembles \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) with loops over the terms of both panels and over the integration points, where the approximation functions are evaluated inside the loops, kCSB_dmg.pyx. Because \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) is part of the internal force vector, it is needed at every iteration (see Solution procedure), and with \(60 \times 30\) integration points and \(10 \times 10\), \(8 \times 8\) terms these loops take 6.5 s per assembly with the code of the thesis (panels 0.6.21), 67% of the run time in the elastic range. The implementation now writes the same Gauss-Legendre rule as matrix products. Let \(g = 1, \dots, n_g\) be the integration points, with weights \(w_g = w_{\xi} w_{\eta} \, ab/4\), and \(\boldsymbol{c}_{tb}\) the Ritz constants of the top panel followed by those of the bottom panel. The operators of Eq. (5) evaluated at all points are the \(n_g \times n_{dof}\) matrices:

(12)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{B}_u &= \left[ \boldsymbol{N}^t_u + d^t \boldsymbol{N}^t_{w,x} \;\; -\boldsymbol{N}^b_u + d^b \boldsymbol{N}^b_{w,x} \right] \\ \boldsymbol{B}_v &= \left[ \boldsymbol{N}^t_v + d^t \boldsymbol{N}^t_{w,y} \;\; -\boldsymbol{N}^b_v + d^b \boldsymbol{N}^b_{w,y} \right] \\ \boldsymbol{B}_w &= \left[ \boldsymbol{N}^t_w \;\; -\boldsymbol{N}^b_w \right] \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

where row \(g\) of \(\boldsymbol{N}^t_u\) holds the approximation functions of \(u\) of the top panel at point \(g\), and so on. The panel functions are the ones of the field evaluation, fuvw, obtained by evaluating the field for each unit vector of Ritz constants. With \(\text{diag}(\cdot)\) the diagonal matrix of a vector over the points, the secant stiffness is:

(13)#\[\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} = \sum_{\alpha = u, v, w} \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha^\top \text{diag}\left( w_g k_g \right) \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha, \qquad k_g = k_o (1 - d_g)\]

a product that runs on optimised BLAS routines. The integration rule is the same as in the loops, so the result is the same up to round-off. The operators depend only on the geometry and on the approximation functions, and they are computed once and cached. The consistent tangent of Eq. (10) uses the same operators, restricted to the rows where \(\chi = 1\). Only the upper triangle of Eq. (13) is added to the connection matrix, which is made symmetric afterwards together with the other connections.

Update restricted to the damaged points. Splitting \(k_g = k_o - (k_o - k_g)\) in Eq. (13):

(14)#\[\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} = \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{o} - \sum_{\alpha = u, v, w} \boldsymbol{B}_{\alpha,\mathcal{D}}^\top \, \text{diag}\left( w_g (k_o - k_g) \right)_{\mathcal{D}} \, \boldsymbol{B}_{\alpha,\mathcal{D}}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{o} = k_o \sum_{\alpha = u, v, w} \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha^\top \text{diag}\left( w_g \right) \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha\]

where \(\mathcal{D} = \{ g : k_g \neq k_o \}\) is the set of damaged points and the subscript \(\mathcal{D}\) keeps only their rows. \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{o}\), the stiffness of the pristine interface, is computed once for each \(k_o\) and cached. Before damage onset \(\mathcal{D}\) is empty and the assembly reduces to a copy of \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{o}\). After onset, \(\mathcal{D}\) holds the fracture process zone and the cracked region, and the cost of the correction grows with the number of their points, not with the size of the whole interface. The correction is exact, not an approximation: it is Eq. (13) regrouped. Because \(k_g\) is taken from the stiffness field used by the kernels, the approach does not depend on how the damage is computed.

The loops of kCSB_dmg.pyx are used only when the connection has use_kernels=True. Both assemblies integrate over the domain of the top panel and evaluate the functions of the bottom panel at the natural coordinates of the top panel, therefore the two panels must have the same dimensions \(a\), \(b\), which MultiDomain checks for the connections 'SB' and 'SB_TSL', raising a ValueError otherwise.

The cost was measured on the DCB model of the thesis (tests/multidomain/test_dcb_damage.py, \(60 \times 30\) integration points, \(10 \times 10\) and \(8 \times 8\) terms), on a laptop with 16 threads, by timing MultiDomain.get_kC_conn() in the driver of the thesis (panels 0.6.21) and in the current driver (panels 0.13.11), as the medians of three runs, between which the times varied by up to 50%. One assembly of \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) takes 6.5 s with the loops of the thesis (panels 0.6.21) at any damage state, 0.42 s with the current kernels, and 0.035 s with the matrix products for the pristine interface and 0.05–0.08 s with 20–100% of the points damaged, 85–190 times faster than the loops of the thesis. Eight displacement increments in the elastic range take 218 s with the code of the thesis and 6.4 s with panels 0.13.11, the matrix products and the update of Eq. (14) together with the changes of the driver described in Solution procedure; with the current kernels and without the predictor and the reuse of the tangent stiffness they take 29 s, with the same loads to a relative \(3.2 \times 10^{-8}\), the order of the convergence tolerance.

Load measured at the loaded edge#

The thesis evaluates the load with two approaches, Section 5.4, and scales the second one, the area integral of the traction, Eq. 5.38, with the ratio between both at the first increment, Section 7.1.4. The area integral was computed with the corrected separation of Section 6.3.1, in which the separation behind the crack front is set to zero. This removes the compressive tractions that balance the tensile ones. For a beam on an elastic foundation the separation behind the peak is physically negative, and its integral is not small.         # the integrals of the traction change by less than 6e-5 with the
        # round-off, e.g. with the ordering of the sparse solver
        assert np.isclose(res['F'], F, rtol=1e-4)
        assert np.isclose(res['F_corr'], F_corr, rtol=1e-4)

        # round-off error of up to 3e-4 relative, from the 0.015 mm

(4), before its correction to Eq. (5); restoring Eq. (4) in the current code reproduces them, and with Eq. The ratio between the corrected integral and the reaction, 15.5 to 31.8, depends on \(k_o\) and on the damage state, such that a scaling factor fixed at the first increment distorts the curve after damage onset. The line integral of \(Q_x\) with finite differences of the moments is 2.3% low. The implementation now provides:

  • MultiDomain.reaction_line_pd_xcte(), the reaction of the prescribed displacement \(w_p(y)\) imposed with the penalty \(k_w\) along \(x = x_p\):

    (15)#\[R = \int_0^b k_w \left( w_p(y) - w(x_p, y) \right) dy\]

    This is what the load cell measures, and it is used for the load-displacement curves.

  • MultiDomain.force_out_plane_damage() integrates \(\tau = k^w_{CZ} \Delta\) with the full separation \(\Delta\), the same that enters Eq. (8). It matches \(R\) by equilibrium of the loaded arm and needs no scaling. The corrected separation is used only to compute the damage.

Solution procedure#

The drivers, tests/multidomain/test_dcb_damage.py and notebooks/dcb_utils.py, solve Eq. (8) under displacement control with the modified Newton-Raphson method of Section 5.3 of the thesis. The changes described below modify the iterations but, except for the convergence criterion, not the equations being solved.

Secant stiffness of the cohesive zone at every iteration. The driver of the thesis refreshed \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) only every \(n_{NR} = 3\) iterations, together with the tangent stiffness matrix. However, \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) is the secant stiffness of the cohesive zone and it is part of the internal force vector, Eq. (8). Keeping it frozen makes the residual inconsistent with the traction-separation law, and a converged state may carry a damage field computed with the displacements of earlier iterations. The drivers now evaluate \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) at every iteration, and only the tangent stiffness of the panels, \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\), is refreshed every \(n_{NR}\) iterations. The iteration matrix of Section 5.3 of the thesis is \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} + \boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\), where the secant stiffness of the cohesive zone replaces its tangent; the drivers add the damage-rate term \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) of Eq. (10).

Predictor. Each increment started from the last converged state. Under displacement control the first residual is then dominated by the jump of the prescribed displacement, and the first iteration always ended with a ratio of Eq. (17) close to 1. The increment \(n\) now starts from the linear extrapolation of the last two converged states:

\[\boldsymbol{c}_{n}^{0} = \boldsymbol{c}_{n-1} + \frac{w_{p,n} - w_{p,n-1}}{w_{p,n-1} - w_{p,n-2}} \left( \boldsymbol{c}_{n-1} - \boldsymbol{c}_{n-2} \right)\]

with the state \(w_p = 0\), \(\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{0}\) used for the second increment (predictor=True). The damage history is only updated after convergence, so the damage of the predicted state is not stored.

Reuse of \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\) in the elastic range. While no point of the interface is damaged, the tangent stiffness of the panels is reused for up to kT_pan_reuse_steps increments, 5 by default, and within an increment it is refreshed every \(n_{NR}\) iterations, but not after the first one. The residual is always evaluated exactly, so the converged solution does not change; only the number of iterations can increase. After onset, \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\) is evaluated at the start of every increment.

Why Newton-Raphson fails after the peak. The residual is continuous but only piecewise smooth. Its derivative jumps whenever an integration point crosses \(\Delta_o\) (from \(k_o\) to the negative softening slope \(-k_o \Delta_o/(\Delta_f - \Delta_o)\)), crosses \(\Delta_f\) (from the softening slope to zero), switches between loading (\(\chi = 1\)) and unloading (\(\hat d \le d^h\)), or changes sign and enters or leaves the region zeroed by the correction of Eq. (3). The consistent tangent of Eq. (10) is exact only on one side of each kink. Before the peak, few points cross a kink in one iteration and the quadratic convergence is not affected. After the peak, the Ritz functions are global, so a Newton correction moves the whole crack front at once, and many points can cross a kink in one iteration when the integration points are sparse compared with the cohesive zone. On the reference DCB of the convergence study below (case A, \(m_{tsl} = 25\)) with \(n_x = 60\), 3.6 points per cohesive zone length, the iterations without the safeguards below converge up to the peak, but at the first opening after it, \(\delta = 5.8\) mm, they enter an exact cycle: from the eighth iteration on, the ratio of Eq. (17) repeats values between 0.76 and 0.999 with a period of 12 iterations, four times the refresh interval \(n_{NR} = 3\) of \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\), to a relative \(3 \times 10^{-10}\), which no number of iterations can break. Two safeguards are added to the drivers. Neither changes the equations or the convergence criterion, so a converged state is the same solution as before, only the path to it changes.

Backtracking line search. With \(\delta \boldsymbol{c}\) the solution of \(\boldsymbol{K}\delta \boldsymbol{c} = -\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}_i)\), where \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is the iteration matrix, the new iterate is \(\boldsymbol{c}_{i+1} = \boldsymbol{c}_i + s\delta \boldsymbol{c}\), with the step length \(s\) taken as the first of \(s = 1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4}, \dots, 2^{-n_{ls}}\) that satisfies:

(16)#\[\left\| \boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}_i + s\delta \boldsymbol{c}) \right\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} \le \left(1 - 10^{-4} s\right) \left\| \boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}_i) \right\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \qquad \left\| \boldsymbol{v} \right\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} = \sqrt{\boldsymbol{v}^\top \mathrm{diag}(|\boldsymbol{d}|)^{-1} \boldsymbol{v}}\]

where the entries \(d_j\) of the diagonal scaling vector \(\boldsymbol{d}\), not to be confused with the damage \(d\), are the diagonal of \(\boldsymbol{K}\) at the start of the increment, the scaling of the criterion of Eq. (17), which balances the rows of the prescribed-displacement penalty against the others. The merit function is the residual norm and not an energy, because the damaged interface has no potential and \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is not symmetric. When \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is the tangent stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_T = \partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \boldsymbol{c}\), the exact Jacobian of the residual, \(\delta \boldsymbol{c}\) is a descent direction of this merit function for any positive weighting:

\[\left. \frac{d}{ds} \frac{1}{2} \left\| \boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}_i + s\delta \boldsymbol{c}) \right\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}^2 \right|_{s=0} = \boldsymbol{R}^\top \mathrm{diag}(|\boldsymbol{d}|)^{-1} \boldsymbol{K}_T \delta \boldsymbol{c} = - \boldsymbol{R}^\top \mathrm{diag}(|\boldsymbol{d}|)^{-1} \boldsymbol{R} < 0\]

so a small enough \(s\) always satisfies Eq. (16). Between refreshes of \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\) the iteration matrix is not the exact Jacobian and this is not guaranteed; if no trial satisfies Eq. (16), the trial with the smallest norm is taken, a trial whose residual is not finite counting as an infinite norm. Halving \(s\) halves the jump of the separations and thus reduces the number of kinks crossed per iteration. Each trial costs one evaluation of the residual, which is cheap with the assembly by matrix products; the trial \(s = 1\) is the residual that the iteration needs anyway. The drivers use \(n_{ls} = 6\) (line_search_max).

Bisection of increments. If an increment from \(w_{p,n-1}\) to \(w_{p,n}\) does not converge within \(n_{max} = 50\) iterations (max_NR_iter), or diverges, that is, the ratio of Eq. (17) is not finite or exceeds \(10^3\) after any iteration, its iterates are discarded and the first half, from \(w_{p,n-1}\) to \((w_{p,n-1} + w_{p,n})/2\), is solved from the last converged state. This is applied recursively with a stack of pending targets: when a sub-increment converges, the driver tries again to reach the next pending target, and when it fails, the remaining interval is halved again, down to \(2^{-6}\) of the original increment (max_bisections), after which the analysis is aborted. Discarding the iterates is consistent because the damage history \(d^h\) is only updated with converged states. Every converged sub-increment updates \(d^h\) and the states used by the predictor, so a bisected increment follows a finer loading path, which matters for an irreversible law; the results are stored only at the prescribed displacements of the original list. The list of prescribed displacements itself does not change: sub-increments are only created when an increment fails.

Effect of the consistent tangent and of the safeguards. notebooks/dcb_mode_I_solver_study.py solves the reference DCB of the convergence study below, case A, with parts of the procedure switched off. On the reference discretisation (\(m_{tsl} = 25\), \(n_x = 180\)) the full procedure takes 139 iterations in 38 increments, without bisection, and the consistent tangent alone is enough: without the line search and without bisection, every opening converges in 2 to 7 iterations, 137 in total, with the same loads to \(10^{-4}\) N; the line search reduces the step in only 9 iterations after the peak. With the secant stiffness as the only tangent of the interface, also without bisection, the iterations grow as the process zone develops, from 6 per increment at damage onset to 124 at the peak, \(\delta = 5.6\) mm: 518 iterations up to the peak against 71 with the consistent tangent, and at the next opening the ratio of Eq. (17) decreases only linearly, to \(9 \times 10^{-4}\) after 200 iterations. With \(n_x = 60\), the line search breaks the cycle described above, 251 of the 332 iterations taking a reduced step: the openings of 5.8, 6.0 and 6.2 mm converge in 8, 12 and 47 iterations, but at 6.4 mm the ratio stagnates at 0.45–0.47 with steps of \(2^{-6}\) to \(2^{-2}\). With the bisection as well, the analysis completes with 3 bisected increments and 384 iterations. The bisection is therefore the safeguard of the coarse discretisations, see the convergence study. In the 17 nonlinear runs of the validation notebooks, 980 openings, every opening converged without bisection in 1 to 8 iterations, 2.84 on average.

Convergence criterion. The prescribed displacement is imposed with \(k_w = 10^6\), such that \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\boldsymbol{c}\) are of the order of \(10^6\)–\(10^7\), while the physical forces are of the order of \(10^2\) N. The denominator of Eq. 5.35 of the thesis, \(\max(F(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}), F(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}))\), is dominated by the penalty terms, and the tolerance \(10^{-4}\) is then not a relative tolerance on the physical forces. The drivers use:

(17)#\[\frac{F(\boldsymbol{R})}{\max\left( F(\boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\boldsymbol{c}), F(\boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\boldsymbol{c} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}) \right)} < \varepsilon\]

where \(F(\cdot) = \|\cdot\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\), the first term of the denominator is the internal force vector of the panels and of the connections, and the second term is the reaction of the prescribed displacement. The large \(k_w\) also degrades the accuracy of the first solution of each increment, whose ratio of Eq. (17) can be close to 1. An increment is therefore declared divergent only when the ratio is not finite or exceeds \(10^3\), instead of when it reaches 1. A divergent increment does not abort the analysis: it is bisected as described above, and the analysis is aborted only when a sub-increment of \(2^{-6}\) of the original increment (max_bisections) still does not converge.

Verification#

The implementation is verified by the tests of tests/multidomain/test_sb_tsl.py:

  • The kernels fkCSB11_dmg(), fkCSB12_dmg() and fkCSB22_dmg() were verified by comparing \(\boldsymbol{c}^\top \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{c}\) with twice the Gauss-Legendre integral of the penalty energy of Eq. (6), evaluated from the displacement field, for random \(\boldsymbol{c}\), random boundary flags, panels with different numbers of terms and thicknesses, and a random \(k^w_{CZ}\) field: the relative difference is below \(10^{-15}\).

  • The assembly by matrix products and the loops of kCSB_dmg.pyx agree to a relative \(10^{-12}\) for random boundary flags, both orders of the panels in the assembly and a damage field with pristine, softening and failed points.

  • MultiDomain.calc_kT_TSL() was verified against central finite differences of \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}(\boldsymbol{c})\boldsymbol{c}\), for random states in the softening range, with and without points where \(d^h > \hat d\): the relative error of \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) is below \(2 \times 10^{-9}\), against 9–62% for \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) alone.

  • A 'SB_TSL' connection between panels of different dimensions raises a ValueError, with the matrix products and with the kernels.

In the nonlinear analyses, the area integral of the traction, MultiDomain.force_out_plane_damage(), must match the reaction of the prescribed displacement, MultiDomain.reaction_line_pd_xcte(), which the drivers report at every increment. The results show that:

  • The Ritz basis of the cohesive domain sets the shape of the response after the peak. The cohesive domain spans \(\lambda L_{tsl} = 20.0\) (A) and 23.8 (B) decay lengths. With \(m_{tsl} = 10\), 0.50 and 0.42 terms per decay length, the basis cannot follow the process zone: the load overshoots the converged curve up to 168.2–169.4 N at \(\delta = 6.2\)–6.4 mm, 1.3–2.0% above the converged peak, and then drops by up to 14 N (A) and 19 N (B) in one increment, a sawtooth with \(CV = 2.0\)–2.7. The load differs from the converged one by 5–7%, \(e_{eq}\) reaches 18–44%, and three of the six runs stop at \(\delta = 6.2\)–7.4 mm. With \(m_{tsl} = 15\) the curve is smooth, \(CV = 0.51\)–0.70, but still 0.7–1.2% off. From \(m_{tsl} = 20\) the error is at most 0.21% for \(n_x \geq 120\), and 25 and 30 terms give the same loads to 0.02% with \(n_x = 180\). Against the terms per decay length, \(m_{tsl}/(\lambda L_{tsl})\), the errors of both cases fall on one curve: about 1% at 0.63–0.75, at most 0.14% from 0.84 and below 0.1% from 1.0, with \(n_x = 180\). The rule \(m_{tsl} = 1.25\,\lambda L_{tsl}\) keeps a margin over this threshold; with at most 25 terms it limits the cohesive domain of case A to \(25/(1.25\lambda) = 24.0\) mm. The Bardell functions go up to 30 terms, so a cohesive domain longer than \(30/(1.0\lambda)\) is not resolved.

  • The Gauss points along \(x\) set the robustness and a floor of the error. \(n_x = 60\), 120 and 180 over 24 mm correspond to 3.6, 7.1 and 10.7 points per \(l_{cz}\). With \(n_x = 60\) the error stays at 0.6–0.9% however many terms are used, 1 to 3 increments of every run with \(m_{tsl} \geq 20\) needed bisection, and the iterations increased 1.3 to 2.8 times, because the crack front crosses several integration points in one increment; the run B with \(m_{tsl} = 15\) stopped at \(\delta = 7.8\) mm. With \(n_x = 120\) the error is 0.10–0.21%, and with \(n_x = 180\) at most 0.02% for \(m_{tsl} \geq 25\), without bisections. This gives the rule \(n_x = 10\,L_{tsl}/l_{cz}\), 168 for the reference DCB, rounded up to 170. Unlike the terms, the integration points do not change the shape of the curve: with \(n_x = 60\) the curves are smooth and the peak is within 0.15%.

  • The other parameters do not matter here. \(n_{tsl} = 15\), \(n_y = 60\) and \(m = n = 12\) change the loads by at most 0.03%, since the specimen is uniform across the width. Halving the increment after \(\delta = 5\) mm changes only where the peak is sampled: the peak rises by 0.03 N (A) and 0.06 N (B) and moves to \(\delta = 5.7\) mm. The stiffer interface of case B raises the initial stiffness by 1.2%, but the peak and the loads after it differ from those of case A by less than 0.06%, in line with the insensitivity of the DCB to \(k_o > 10^4\) N/mm³ reported by Turon et al. (2007).

  • The length of the cohesive domain is detected by \(e_{eq}\), not by the load. At the last opening the crack has grown by 9.8 mm, and the margin \(3 l_{cz} + 8/\lambda\) for the process zone and the region ahead of it, where the opening decays as \(e^{-\lambda x}\), the solution of beams on an elastic foundation [kanninen1973AugmentedDCB], to below \(e^{-8}\) of its value, is 13.9 mm (A) and 12.4 mm (B), so \(L_{tsl} = 24\) mm contains both up to \(\delta = 8\) mm and \(e_{eq}\) stays below 0.09% (A) and 0.004% (B), the larger value of case A coming from its longer decay length. With \(L_{tsl} = 17\) mm the loads are within 0.06–0.25% of the converged ones, but from \(\delta = 7.0\) mm (A) and 7.4 mm (B), when the crack has grown by 6–7.5 mm, the compressive lobe enters the perfect bond and \(e_{eq}\) grows to 10–33% at the last opening.

  • The sawtooth is a discrete snap-back. With too few terms the basis cannot follow the process zone continuously, like a cohesive finite element mesh that is coarse compared with the cohesive zone [alfano2001Interface] [turon2007MeshSize]: the elastic energy builds up while the crack front is held and is released when a group of integration points fails at once, which a displacement-controlled analysis crosses as a sudden load drop. More terms refine the displacement field and remove the sawtooth, while more integration points refine the damage field, remove the bisections and lower the floor of the error, but do not change the shape of the curve.

The converged discretisation of the reference DCB is therefore \(L_{tsl} = 24\) mm, \(m_{tsl} = 25\), \(n_x = 180\), \(n_{tsl} = 10\), \(n_y = 30\), with \(8 \times 8\) terms in the other domains, which gives \(P_{max} = 166.10\) N (A) and 166.15 N (B) at \(\delta = 5.6\) mm. The rules for other geometries are:

(18)#\[m_{tsl} = 1.25\,\lambda L_{tsl}, \qquad n_x = 10\,\frac{L_{tsl}}{l_{cz}}, \qquad L_{tsl} \geq \Delta a + 3 l_{cz} + \frac{8}{\lambda}\]

with \(\Delta a\) the crack growth up to the last opening, \(m_{tsl} \leq 25\), \(n_x \geq 4 m_{tsl}\) rounded up to a multiple of 10, \(n_{tsl} = 10\), \(n_y = 30\) and \(m = n = 8\) in the other domains. They are implemented in tsl_resolution(), tsl_margin() and tsl_discretization(), and MultiDomain.calc_k_dmg() prints a warning, the first time a point is damaged, when \(m_{tsl} < 1.0\,\lambda L_{tsl}\) or \(n_x < 7\,L_{tsl}/l_{cz}\). Before version 0.13.11 the rate was \((k_o/4D_{11})^{1/4}\), that of one arm on a rigid foundation, \(2^{-1/4}\) times the rate of the symmetric DCB, and the factors of the rules were 1.5 and 1.2 instead of 1.25 and 1.0. The margin was \(2\pi/\lambda + 3 l_{cz}\), i.e. \(7.47/\lambda + 3 l_{cz}\) with the corrected rate; \(2\pi/\lambda + 3 l_{cz}\) with the corrected rate lets the tractions reach the perfect bond in the last openings of the validation runs, with \(e_{eq}\) up to 10%, against 0.25% at most with \(3 l_{cz} + 8/\lambda\). The Gauss-Legendre points and weights are computed by scipy.special.roots_legendre(), without an upper limit for \(n_x\).

Discretisation of the validation runs. The validation runs below use the rules of Eq. (18) with 25 terms and the corresponding \(L_{tsl} = 25/(1.25\lambda)\), and the range of openings is limited such that the crack growth plus the margin fits in \(L_{tsl}\). The low-strength case of Alfano and Crisfield needs a cohesive domain over the whole bonded length, \(L_{tsl} = 70\) mm, for which the first rule asks for 94 terms with \(k_o = 10^5\) N/mm³, more than the 30 Bardell functions. It is therefore run with \(k_o = 10^3\) N/mm³, for which \(m_{tsl} = 30 = 1.27\,\lambda L_{tsl}\) and \(n_x = 4 m_{tsl} = 120\) meet both rules. The damage onset, \(\Delta_o = \tau_o/k_o = 1.7 \times 10^{-3}\) mm, remains far below the separation at failure, \(\Delta_f = 2G_{Ic}/\tau_o = 0.33\) mm, so the softening branch that governs this case is hardly affected; Alfano and Crisfield also scaled the penalty stiffness with the strength. A run with \(k_o = 10^5\) N/mm³ and the same 30 terms, \(0.4\, \lambda L_{tsl}\), gives a peak of 50.81 N against 50.78 N at the same opening, and curves that differ by at most 0.7% of the peak, but \(e_{eq}\) reaches 1.5% with \(k_o = 10^5\) N/mm³, as in the runs above with too few terms, against less than \(10^{-5}\) with \(k_o = 10^3\) N/mm³.

Validation against the literature#

The model was compared with eight references that report mode I DCB results: Alfano and Crisfield (2001) [alfano2001Interface], Camanho et al. (2003) [camanho2003Delamination], Turon et al. (2007) [turon2007MeshSize], Krueger (2008) [krueger2008DCB], Tijs et al. (2022) [tijs2022Interlaminar], Leciñana et al. (2023) [lecinana2023Fatigue] and the PhD thesis of Tijs (2023) [tijs2023PhD], plus Krueger (2012) [krueger2012MMB], whose benchmarks are for the mixed-mode bending specimen and are only recorded, see below. Each reference has a notebook in notebooks/ with a dictionary of the FE cases and one of the experimental tests, a comparison table, a plot and a discussion; the panels results are stored in notebooks/results/*.npz and collected by notebooks/dcb_mode_I_validation_summary.ipynb. The results were obtained with panels 0.14.0; the run times stored with them were measured again with panels 0.14.0 and structsolve 0.9.2, one run at a time, together with the null-space runs of notebooks/conn_method_comparison.ipynb, as the key time_note of each file states.

The case of Camanho et al. (2003) is kept for reference, but it is not used to validate the model: the FE and experimental load-opening curves of the reference are 12–21% less stiff than the corrected beam theory, more than transverse shear and root rotation explain, and the reference does not give enough detail to find out why; the experimental peak, 147.1 N, is 6% above the corrected beam theory with the same \(G_{Ic}\); and the FE model has about one decohesion element per cohesive zone length, which overpredicts the peak load [turon2007MeshSize]. See the note in notebooks/dcb_camanho2003.ipynb.

Model. notebooks/dcb_utils.py builds a symmetric DCB: CLPT arms, far end clamped, arm tips opened to \(\pm\delta/2\) by prescribed displacements, the load being the reaction of the prescribed displacement. The bonded region is split into a cohesive domain ('SB_TSL', bilinear law) of length \(L_{tsl}\) ahead of the initial crack front and a perfect bond ('SB') over the rest, such that only the region reached by the crack needs the resolution of the cohesive zone. A weld narrower than the specimen (Tijs 2023) is represented by starting the integration points outside the bonded strip fully damaged. The solution procedure is the one described in Solution procedure. The discretisation follows the rules of Eq. (18) (dcb_utils.discretize): \(m_{tsl} = \lceil 1.25\,\lambda L_{tsl} \rceil\) with at most 25 terms, and \(n_x = 10\,L_{tsl}/l_{cz}\), but not less than \(4\,m_{tsl}\) to integrate the products of the approximation functions, rounded up to a multiple of 10, and \(L_{tsl}\) covers the crack growth plus a margin of \(3\,l_{cz} + 8/\lambda\) for the process zone and the decaying opening ahead of it; the range of openings is limited so that \(L_{tsl}\) stays resolved, which allows 4–11 mm of crack growth. The penalty stiffness is \(k_o = 10^5\) N/mm³, except for the low-strength case of Alfano and Crisfield (see the convergence study): a longer decay length \(1/\lambda\) needs fewer terms, and for the DCB of Turon et al. (2007) the peak load with the \(k_o = 10^6\) N/mm³ of their model differs by 0.2% (64.13 against 64.02 N), in line with their Fig. 9. The reference curves of linear elastic fracture mechanics (LEFM) are the simple and the corrected beam theories, the latter with the crack length \(a + \chi h\) of Williams, \(\chi = \sqrt{E_{11}/(11 G_{13})\,[3 - 2(\Gamma/(1+\Gamma))^2]}\), \(\Gamma = 1.18\sqrt{E_{11}E_{22}}/G_{13}\), and the bending stiffness \(D_{11} b\) of the arm.

The results show that:

  • Same cohesive law as the reference. For the DCB of Tijs et al. (2022), which is also the reference case of the thesis, the FE model of the paper uses the same geometry, material and bilinear law (\(G_{Ic}\) = 1.12 N/mm, \(\tau^0\) = 87 MPa). panels predicts 166.2 N at 5.66 mm against 167 N at 5.7 mm, -0.5% on the load and -0.7% on the opening.

  • Cohesive zone beyond LEFM. With the low strength \(t_o\) = 1.7 MPa of Alfano and Crisfield (2001) the process zone is of the order of the specimen length and the peak of their interface model is 22% below the corrected beam theory; panels reproduces it within 2.6%, at the same opening and with the same initial stiffness (+2.3%), with \(k_o = 10^3\) N/mm³ and the cohesive domain over the whole bonded length (see the convergence study).

  • Nominal strengths. For the unidirectional specimens with nominal strengths, the panels peak is within 2.1% of the corrected beam theory in every case, and after the peak it follows the LEFM propagation branch (50.7 N, 0.04% below it, at \(\delta\) = 3.00 mm for Alfano and Crisfield, Table I). The FE results of the references scatter by about \(\pm 10\%\) around the corrected beam theory: 1 mm decohesion elements for a cohesive zone of 1.35 mm in Camanho et al. (2003), whose FE peak is above both the test and the beam theories, and about 57 N in Turon et al. (2007), below both.

  • Tests. The test of Turon et al. (2007), with a bilinear-like response, is predicted within 3.3% (and that of Camanho et al., kept for reference, within -4.8%). The AS4D/PEKK-FC tests show a strong R-curve (initiation about 0.7 N/mm, propagation 1.12 N/mm, and 0.95 N/mm without fibre bridging for the test with \(a_0\) = 40 mm, the toughness of its panels run) that a bilinear law with the propagation toughness cannot represent: panels, the bilinear FE model of Tijs et al. and the corrected beam theory are all 11–18% above these tests, which the tabular law of the paper matches. Fibre bridging also makes the experimental curves decrease more slowly than every model after the peak.

  • Welded joints. For the welded DCB of Tijs (2023) panels is 4–9% below the FE results of the thesis, 2–5% below the corrected beam theory and 6.5–7.6% below the tests with a single toughness value. The cohesive zone is long compared with the arm thickness (\(l_{cz}\) = 2.5–2.7 mm, \(h\) = 2.24 mm), which lowers the peak below the LEFM limit; the FE model of the thesis is not described in enough detail to separate this effect from its modelling assumptions.

  • Initial compliance. The CLPT arms of panels are too stiff for the unidirectional specimens: the compliance is 15–19% below the tests and 15–21% below the corrected beam theory, which matches the tests of Leciñana et al. within 4%. The arms have no transverse shear deformation, which is large for these materials (\(E_{11}/G_{13}\) = 22–27), and the rotation at the crack front comes only from the elastic foundation of the cohesive interface. The same appears in the load-opening curves as an initial stiffness 12–17% above the corrected beam theory, while the peak loads, controlled by the energy release rate, are not affected. For the quasi-isotropic welded specimens, with a bending modulus closer to the shear modulus, the compliance of panels is within 15% of the tests.

  • Robustness. All 17 nonlinear runs converged at every opening without bisection, in 1 to 8 iterations, and the load balances the integral of the cohesive tractions within 0.25% (\(e_{eq}\)), except for the full weld (7.6%), whose perfect bond spans the whole width, and the low-strength case of Alfano and Crisfield with \(k_o = 10^5\) N/mm³ (1.5%), whose 30 terms are only \(0.4\,\lambda L_{tsl}\).

Krueger (2008). NASA/TM-2008-215123 [krueger2008DCB] gives two mode I DCB benchmarks, a unidirectional \([0]_{24}\) specimen of T300/1076 and a multidirectional D±30 specimen of C12K/R6376, built with 3D solid elements and the virtual crack closure technique, with no cohesive law, no interfacial strength and no experiment. Their critical load is the one at which \(G_T\) reaches \(G_c\) at mid-width, while panels and beam theory follow the width average \(\bar{G} = P_{crit}^2 a_e^2/(b\,EI)\); the two criteria differ by \(\sqrt{G_c/\bar{G}}\), 1.026 and 1.123, which is the whole offset between the benchmark and the corrected beam theory. panels is 2.0% above the corrected beam theory for the unidirectional specimen and 5.2% below it for the multidirectional one, whose cohesive zone (\(l_{cz}\) = 1.99 mm with \(\tau_o\) = 40 MPa) is not small compared with its 2.0 mm arms. Raising \(\tau_o\) from 40 to 60 MPa moves the peaks by 1.1 and 1.2% only, so the comparison does not rest on that choice. See notebooks/dcb_krueger2008.ipynb.

Krueger (2012). NASA/CR-2012-217562 [krueger2012MMB] gives benchmarks for the mixed-mode bending specimen of IM7/8552 at \(G_{II}/G_T\) = 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8, with the critical points (1.64 mm, 128.5 N), (1.34 mm, 385 N) and (1.65 mm, 751 N). They are recorded in notebooks/krueger2012_mmb_benchmark.ipynb but not simulated: the damage of 'SB_TSL' is driven by the normal separation only, and the loading lever is not modelled.

Differences that remain#

  • The interpenetration stiffness \(k_{ipen}\) of Eq. 5.12 of the thesis is not implemented: MultiDomain.calc_k_dmg() returns \(k_o(1-d)\) also for negative separations, such that a fully damaged point offers no resistance to interpenetration. This does not affect a monotonically loaded DCB.

  • The damage history MultiDomain.dmg_index is stored once per assembly, which restricts the model to one 'SB_TSL' connection.

  • The finite element model of Section 5.6 of the thesis constrains \(U_x\) along the loaded edge, while the Ritz model leaves \(u\) free there.

  • The arms follow CLPT: without transverse shear deformation, the compliance of unidirectional DCB specimens is 15–21% below the corrected beam theory (see the validation above). A shear deformable kinematics would remove this difference.

  • The cohesive law is bilinear and the damage is driven by the normal separation only: R-curve effects (multilinear laws) and mixed-mode delamination are not represented.
