Cohesive zone model of the multi-domain approach#

The area connection 'SB_TSL' of MultiDomain.get_kC_conn() connects two skins over an area with a traction-separation law (TSL) at the interface, such that the progressive delamination between them can be simulated. It originates from the MSc thesis of D’Souza (2024) [nathan2024MSc], Multi-Domain Semi-Analytical Cohesive Zone Approach. This page describes the model as implemented and documents where the implementation departs from the thesis. Equation numbers of the form “Eq. 5.12” refer to the thesis. Each deviation states the problem, its consequence on the load-displacement curve of the double cantilever beam (DCB), and what the implementation does instead.

The implementation is found in:

  • the kernels fkCSB11_dmg(), fkCSB12_dmg() and fkCSB22_dmg() of panels/multidomain/connections/kCSB_dmg.pyx, and calc_kw_tsl();

  • the rules of the discretisation of the cohesive domain, tsl_resolution(), tsl_margin() and tsl_discretization(), see Discretisation of the cohesive domain;

  • the methods of MultiDomain: MultiDomain.get_kC_conn(), MultiDomain.calc_k_dmg(), MultiDomain.correct_separation(), MultiDomain.calc_kT_TSL(), MultiDomain.reaction_line_pd_xcte() and MultiDomain.force_out_plane_damage().

The model of the thesis is the DCB of its Section 7.3: \(L = 65\) mm, \(b = 25\) mm, \(a_0 = 48\) mm, arms \([0]_{15}\) with ply thickness 0.14 mm of AS4D/PEKK-FC, \(G_{Ic} = 1.12\) N/mm, three domains per arm, of which the first one, with length \(a_1 = L - a_0 = 17\) mm, carries the cohesive zone. It is the model of the sections below up to the solution procedure. The rules of the discretisation of Discretisation of the cohesive domain are stated for a model in which the cohesive domain is followed by a perfect bond.

Traction-separation law and damage#

The law implemented is bilinear, with one damage variable \(d\) that degrades the three components of the traction:

(1)#\[\tau_\alpha = k_o \left(1 - d\right) \Delta_\alpha, \qquad \alpha = u, v, w\]

where \(k_o\) is the penalty stiffness of the pristine interface (key 'k_o' of the connection) and \(\Delta_\alpha\) are the separations of Eq. (5). The separation at damage onset is \(\Delta_o = \tau_o/k_o\) and the separation at complete failure is \(\Delta_f = 2 G_{Ic}/\tau_o\), with the keys 'tau_o' and 'G1c' of the connection. The damage is driven by the normal separation after the correction described in Correction of the separation field, \(\bar\Delta_w\), and it is irreversible:

(2)#\[\begin{split}d = \max\left( d^{h}, \hat d(\bar\Delta_w) \right), \qquad \hat d(\Delta) = \begin{cases} 0 & \Delta \le \Delta_o \\ \dfrac{\Delta_f (\Delta - \Delta_o)}{(\Delta_f - \Delta_o) \Delta} & \Delta_o < \Delta < \Delta_f \\ 1 & \Delta \ge \Delta_f \end{cases}\end{split}\]

where \(d^h\) is the damage of the last converged increment, stored in MultiDomain.dmg_index by MultiDomain.update_TSL_history(). The damage \(\hat d\) is computed by calc_kw_tsl() and the maximum with the history, together with the degraded stiffness \(k_o (1 - d)\) at each integration point, by MultiDomain.calc_k_dmg(). The area under the softening branch of the law is \(G_{Ic}\).

Correction of the separation field#

Section 6.3.1 of the thesis describes the correction of the separation used to compute the damage as: start at the point of maximum separation, location \(A\), move in the direction of decreasing separation up to the first non-positive separation, location \(B\), and set the separation to zero beyond \(B\). Setting to zero, for each row \(y =\) cte, everything from \(x = 0\) up to the last non-positive separation of the row would instead zero the whole row, including the fracture process zone, whenever the approximation functions give a non-positive value between \(A\) and the edge of the domain, for instance a small oscillation at \(x = a_1\). MultiDomain.correct_separation() implements the description of the thesis, for a crack front that advances along \(-x\):

(3)#\[\begin{split}\Delta^{corr}_{i,j} = \begin{cases} 0 & j \le B_i \\ \Delta_{i,j} & j > B_i \end{cases} \qquad B_i = \max \left\{ j \le A_i : \Delta_{i,j} \le 0 \right\}, \quad A_i = \arg\max_j \Delta_{i,j}\end{split}\]

with \(i\) the row and \(j\) the column of the grid of integration points, \(j\) increasing with \(x\). Rows without non-positive values between \(x = 0\) and \(A_i\) are not modified, and neither are rows without any positive separation. The negative values of the separation are not only numerical: for a beam on an elastic foundation the separation behind the crack front oscillates with a decaying amplitude, and its first negative lobe does not change when the number of terms of the cohesive domain is increased from 10 to 20.

The corrected separation is used only to compute the damage. The tractions, the internal force vector and the stiffness matrices use the full separation.

Separations at the interface#

The compatibility of the area connection in the thesis, Eqs. 4.57–4.62, assumes \(w^i_{,x} = w^j_{,x}\) and \(w^i_{,y} = w^j_{,y}\) (Eq. 4.61) to write the in-plane compatibility with the slope of the top panel only, Eq. 4.62:

(4)#\[\Delta u^{thesis} = u^t + (d^t + d^b) \, w^t_{,x} - u^b\]

This holds for a perfect bond, but not inside the fracture process zone, where the two arms rotate in opposite directions. There, Eq. (4) gives a spurious tangential separation, penalised with the same \(k^w_{CZ}\), which stiffens the interface exactly where it should open. The implementation uses the displacement jump between the two surfaces in contact, with the slope of each panel:

(5)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \Delta_u &= u^t + d^t w^t_{,x} - u^b + d^b w^b_{,x} \\ \Delta_v &= v^t + d^t w^t_{,y} - v^b + d^b w^b_{,y} \\ \Delta_w &= w^t - w^b \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

where \(d^t\) and \(d^b\) are the distances from the reference surfaces of the top and bottom panels to the interface, \(u^t, v^t, w^t\) and \(u^b, v^b, w^b\) the displacements of their reference surfaces. For cylindrical panels with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, \(v(z) = v + z (v/r - w_{,y})\), and \(w_{,y}\) is replaced by \(w_{,y} - v/r\) in \(\Delta_v\), with the radius \(r\) of the reference surface of each panel, see MultiDomain._tsl_operators() and panels.multidomain.connections.kCSB_dmg. With the thicknesses \(h^t\), \(h^b\) and the offsets \(d_t\), \(d_b\) of the laminates, Shell.offset, each laminate occupying \(d - h/2 \le z \le d + h/2\) in the coordinate \(z\) of its panel, \(d^t = h^t/2 - d_t\) and \(d^b = h^b/2 + d_b\), see kCsdt._sb_distances(); without offsets, as in the DCB examples, they are \(h^t/2\) and \(h^b/2\), the distances from the mid-planes. Top and bottom refer to the \(z\) axis, normal to the reference surfaces and common to both panels, pointing up in the DCB: the top panel is the panel on the positive side of the interface along \(z\), whose face \(z = -d^t\) is at the interface, and it is p1 of the connection dictionary; the bottom panel is the one on the negative side, whose face \(z = +d^b\) is at the interface, and it is p2, whatever their order in the assembly, see MultiDomain.get_kC_conn(). With these three separations and the degraded stiffness \(k^w_{CZ} = k_o (1 - d)\), the penalty energy of Eq. 5.1 becomes:

(6)#\[U^{tb}_{pen-AR-dmg} = \frac{1}{2} \int_x \int_y k^w_{CZ} \left( \Delta_u^2 + \Delta_v^2 + \Delta_w^2 \right) dx\,dy\]

and the stiffness matrix of the connection is:

(7)#\[\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} = \int_A k^w_{CZ} \boldsymbol{B}_\Delta^\top \boldsymbol{B}_\Delta \, dA, \qquad U^{tb}_{pen-AR-dmg} = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{c}^\top \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} \boldsymbol{c}\]

where \(\boldsymbol{B}_\Delta\) is the operator that gives the three separations of Eq. (5) from the Ritz constants \(\boldsymbol{c}\). For a frozen damage field, the internal force of the connection is \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\boldsymbol{c}\), see Eq. (8). The integral is evaluated with a Gauss-Legendre rule of nr_x_gauss \(\times\) nr_y_gauss points over the domain of the top panel, and \(k^w_{CZ}\) is given at each of these points. The terms of the kernels that are new or modified with respect to the thesis are listed in the docstrings of fkCSB11_dmg(), fkCSB12_dmg() and fkCSB22_dmg(). The undamaged connection 'SB', fkCSB(), imposes the jump of Eq. (5) at zero separation, such that the pristine 'SB_TSL', with \(k^w_{CZ} = k_o\) at every point, has the matrix of 'SB' with \(k_t = k_o\).

Energy dissipated by crack formation#

The thesis adds the term \(U_{crack}\) of Eq. 5.18 to the total potential energy, Eq. 5.29, with the force vector \(\boldsymbol{F}_{crack}\) of Eq. 5.28 and the stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{crack}\) of Eq. 5.26. This counts the dissipated energy twice. The degradation of the penalty stiffness \(k^w_{CZ} = k_o(1-d)\) inside \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\), Eq. 5.5, already makes the traction follow the softening branch of the traction-separation law, and the area under that branch is \(G_{Ic}\), exactly as in a finite element with a cohesive law. The implementation has neither \(U_{crack}\), \(\boldsymbol{F}_{crack}\) nor \(\boldsymbol{K}_{crack}\), and the residual is:

(8)#\[\boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_{int} = \boldsymbol{F}^{panels}_{int} + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}(d)\boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\boldsymbol{c}\]

where \(\boldsymbol{F}^{panels}_{int}\) is the internal force vector of the panels, \(d\) is the damage at each integration point, Eq. (2), the largest value between the converged history and the value obtained from the current separation, and \(\boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\) is the penalty stiffness of the prescribed displacement. The sudden increase of the slope at damage onset reported in Section 7.1.4 of the thesis, and the need to drop \(\boldsymbol{K}^2_{crack}\), are consequences of the double counting.

Consistent tangent stiffness#

Writing the separations as \(\Delta_\alpha = \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha\boldsymbol{c}\), the internal force vector of the connection and its derivative are:

(9)#\[\boldsymbol{F}^{coh}_{int} = \int_A k_o (1 - d) \sum_\alpha \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha^\top \Delta_\alpha \, dA = \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\boldsymbol{c}\]
(10)#\[\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{F}^{coh}_{int}}{\partial \boldsymbol{c}} = \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d} = - \int_A \chi \, k_o \, \hat d'(\Delta_w) \left( \sum_\alpha \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha^\top \Delta_\alpha \right) \boldsymbol{B}_w \, dA\]

with:

(11)#\[\begin{split}\hat d'(\Delta) = \frac{\Delta_f \Delta_o}{(\Delta_f - \Delta_o) \Delta^2}, \qquad \chi = \begin{cases} 1 & \Delta_o < \bar\Delta_w < \Delta_f \text{ and } \hat d(\bar\Delta_w) > d^h \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\end{split}\]

The thesis does not derive the tangent of the cohesive zone. With the secant stiffness \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) alone, the iteration matrix misses the change of the damage with the displacements. It overestimates the stiffness of every softening point, and the Newton-Raphson iterations converge linearly, slowly, once the crack grows: in the reference DCB, the first increment after the first fully damaged point needed more than 130 iterations.

The mask \(\chi\) selects the points where the damage grows in the current state. Where it is 1, the correction of Correction of the separation field leaves the separation unchanged, \(\bar\Delta_w = \Delta_w\) and \(\partial \bar\Delta_w / \partial \boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{B}_w\). The derivative of the correction itself, a switch between 0 and \(\Delta_w\), is zero almost everywhere. Some properties of Eq. (10):

  • \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) is not symmetric, because the tangential tractions depend on \(\Delta_w\) through \(d\), while \(d\) does not depend on \(\Delta_u\), \(\Delta_v\). The term \(\boldsymbol{B}_w^\top \Delta_w \boldsymbol{B}_w\) is symmetric and negative semi-definite; it makes the normal tangent stiffness of a softening point \(k_o (1 - d - \hat d' \Delta_w) = -k_o \Delta_o / (\Delta_f - \Delta_o)\), the slope of the descending branch of the bilinear law.

  • The iteration matrix becomes \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d} + \boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\). Under displacement control the prescribed-displacement penalty keeps it regular on the softening branch of the DCB. structsolve.solve uses scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve, which accepts non-symmetric matrices.

  • Like \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\), \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) is evaluated at every iteration, while \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\) is refreshed every \(n_{NR}\) iterations, see Solution procedure.

MultiDomain.calc_kT_TSL() evaluates \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) at the Gauss-Legendre points of the connection, with \(\boldsymbol{B}_\alpha\) built once per panel from the field functions and cached. The nonlinear drivers use it by default (consistent_tangent=True).

Assembly of the cohesive stiffness by matrix products#

The thesis assembles \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) with loops over the terms of both panels and over the integration points, where the approximation functions are evaluated inside the loops, kCSB_dmg.pyx. Because \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) is part of the internal force vector, it is needed at every iteration (see Solution procedure), and with \(60 \times 30\) integration points and \(10 \times 10\), \(8 \times 8\) terms these loops dominate the run time in the elastic range. The implementation writes the same Gauss-Legendre rule as matrix products. Let \(g = 1, \dots, n_g\) be the integration points, with weights \(w_g = w_{\xi} w_{\eta} \, ab/4\), and \(\boldsymbol{c}_{tb}\) the Ritz constants of the top panel followed by those of the bottom panel. The operators of Eq. (5) evaluated at all points are the \(n_g \times n_{dof}\) matrices:

(12)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{B}_u &= \left[ \boldsymbol{N}^t_u + d^t \boldsymbol{N}^t_{w,x} \;\; -\boldsymbol{N}^b_u + d^b \boldsymbol{N}^b_{w,x} \right] \\ \boldsymbol{B}_v &= \left[ \boldsymbol{N}^t_v + d^t \boldsymbol{N}^t_{w,y} \;\; -\boldsymbol{N}^b_v + d^b \boldsymbol{N}^b_{w,y} \right] \\ \boldsymbol{B}_w &= \left[ \boldsymbol{N}^t_w \;\; -\boldsymbol{N}^b_w \right] \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

where row \(g\) of \(\boldsymbol{N}^t_u\) holds the approximation functions of \(u\) of the top panel at point \(g\), and so on. The panel functions are the ones of the field evaluation, fuvw, obtained by evaluating the field for each unit vector of Ritz constants. With \(\text{diag}(\cdot)\) the diagonal matrix of a vector over the points, the secant stiffness is:

(13)#\[\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} = \sum_{\alpha = u, v, w} \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha^\top \text{diag}\left( w_g k_g \right) \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha, \qquad k_g = k_o (1 - d_g)\]

a product that runs on optimised BLAS routines. The integration rule is the same as in the loops, so the result is the same up to round-off. The operators depend only on the geometry and on the approximation functions, and they are computed once and cached. The consistent tangent of Eq. (10) uses the same operators, restricted to the rows where \(\chi = 1\). Only the upper triangle of Eq. (13) is added to the connection matrix, which is made symmetric afterwards together with the other connections.

Update restricted to the damaged points. Splitting \(k_g = k_o - (k_o - k_g)\) in Eq. (13):

(14)#\[\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} = \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{o} - \sum_{\alpha = u, v, w} \boldsymbol{B}_{\alpha,\mathcal{D}}^\top \, \text{diag}\left( w_g (k_o - k_g) \right)_{\mathcal{D}} \, \boldsymbol{B}_{\alpha,\mathcal{D}}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{o} = k_o \sum_{\alpha = u, v, w} \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha^\top \text{diag}\left( w_g \right) \boldsymbol{B}_\alpha\]

where \(\mathcal{D} = \{ g : k_g \neq k_o \}\) is the set of damaged points and the subscript \(\mathcal{D}\) keeps only their rows. \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{o}\), the stiffness of the pristine interface, is computed once for each \(k_o\) and cached. Before damage onset \(\mathcal{D}\) is empty and the assembly reduces to a copy of \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{o}\). After onset, \(\mathcal{D}\) holds the fracture process zone and the cracked region, and the cost of the correction grows with the number of their points, not with the size of the whole interface. The correction is exact, not an approximation: it is Eq. (13) regrouped. Because \(k_g\) is taken from the stiffness field used by the kernels, the approach does not depend on how the damage is computed.

The loops of kCSB_dmg.pyx are used only when the connection has use_kernels=True. Both assemblies integrate over the domain of the top panel and evaluate the functions of the bottom panel at the natural coordinates of the top panel, therefore the two panels must have the same dimensions \(a\), \(b\), which MultiDomain checks for the connections 'SB' and 'SB_TSL', raising a ValueError otherwise.

Written as matrix products, one assembly of \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) on the DCB model of the thesis is one to two orders of magnitude faster than with the loops over the terms and the integration points.

Load measured at the loaded edge#

The thesis evaluates the load with two approaches, Section 5.4, and scales the second one, the area integral of the traction, Eq. 5.38, with the ratio between both at the first increment, Section 7.1.4. The area integral was computed with the corrected separation of Section 6.3.1, in which the separation behind the crack front is set to zero. This removes the compressive tractions that balance the tensile ones. For a beam on an elastic foundation the separation behind the peak is physically negative, and its integral is not small: in a linear solution of the DCB of the thesis the integral of the traction computed with the corrected separation is more than an order of magnitude larger than the reaction at the loaded edge. Their ratio depends on \(k_o\) and on the damage state, such that a scaling factor fixed at the first increment distorts the curve after damage onset. A line integral of the transverse shear force \(Q_x\) of the loaded arm, evaluated from the derivatives of the moments of the Ritz field, is a third measure, less accurate than the reaction. The implementation provides:

  • MultiDomain.reaction_line_pd_xcte(), the reaction of the prescribed displacement \(w_p(y)\) imposed with the penalty \(k_w\) along \(x = x_p\):

    (15)#\[R = \int_0^b k_w \left( w_p(y) - w(x_p, y) \right) dy\]

    This is what the load cell measures, and it is used for the load-displacement curves.

  • MultiDomain.force_out_plane_damage() integrates \(\tau = k^w_{CZ} \Delta\) with the full separation \(\Delta\), the same that enters Eq. (8). It matches \(R\) by equilibrium of the loaded arm and needs no scaling. The corrected separation is used only to compute the damage.

When the cohesive domain carries the whole load, the difference between both measures is a check of the equilibrium of the solution, and the drivers report it at every increment. It grows when the basis does not represent the tractions, or when the tractions ahead of the process zone, its compressive lobe and the decaying opening beyond it, reach a perfect bond beyond the cohesive domain, whose tractions are not part of the integral.

Penalty stiffness of the edge connections#

The default penalties of calc_kt_kr() for an 'SSxcte' connection between two equal laminates are \(k_t = A_{11}/h\) and \(k_r = D_{11}/h\), from Castro and Donadon (2017) [castro2017Multidomain]. With the penalty energy \(\frac{1}{2} k_r (w^i_{,x} - w^j_{,x})^2\) per unit width, see panels.multidomain.connections, the connection behaves as a rotational spring of stiffness \(k_r\) per unit width, which adds to a cantilever loaded at the tip a compliance equal to \(3 D_{11}/(k_r a) = 3h/a\) of its bending compliance, about 13% for each connection of the DCB arm (\(h = 2.1\) mm, \(a = 48\) mm). The arms of the reference model have two connections each, at the crack tip moment, so that the elastic stiffness of the DCB with the default penalties is lower than that of the beam on an elastic foundation of Kanninen [kanninen1973AugmentedDCB], which the model approaches when the penalties grow, and the separation at the crack front, and therefore the damage onset, lags the imposed displacement. The drivers multiply the default 'SSxcte' penalties by edge_penalty_factor, 100 by default. The library defaults are unchanged.

Solution procedure#

The nonlinear drivers solve Eq. (8) under displacement control with the modified Newton-Raphson method of Section 5.3 of the thesis. The changes described below modify the iterations but, except for the convergence criterion, not the equations being solved.

Secant stiffness of the cohesive zone at every iteration. The driver of the thesis refreshed \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) only every \(n_{NR} = 3\) iterations, together with the tangent stiffness matrix. However, \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) is the secant stiffness of the cohesive zone and it is part of the internal force vector, Eq. (8). Keeping it frozen makes the residual inconsistent with the traction-separation law, and a converged state may carry a damage field computed with the displacements of earlier iterations. The drivers evaluate \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg}\) at every iteration, and only the tangent stiffness of the panels, \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\), is refreshed every \(n_{NR}\) iterations. The iteration matrix of Section 5.3 of the thesis is \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{dmg} + \boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\), where the secant stiffness of the cohesive zone replaces its tangent; the drivers add the damage-rate term \(\boldsymbol{K}^{conn}_{\dot d}\) of Eq. (10).

Predictor. Each increment started from the last converged state. Under displacement control the first residual is then dominated by the jump of the prescribed displacement, and the first iteration always ended with a ratio of Eq. (17) close to 1. The increment \(n\) starts from the linear extrapolation of the last two converged states:

\[\boldsymbol{c}_{n}^{0} = \boldsymbol{c}_{n-1} + \frac{w_{p,n} - w_{p,n-1}}{w_{p,n-1} - w_{p,n-2}} \left( \boldsymbol{c}_{n-1} - \boldsymbol{c}_{n-2} \right)\]

with the state \(w_p = 0\), \(\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{0}\) used for the second increment (predictor=True). The damage history is only updated after convergence, so the damage of the predicted state is not stored.

Reuse of \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\) in the elastic range. While no point of the interface is damaged, the tangent stiffness of the panels is reused for up to kT_pan_reuse_steps increments, 5 by default, and within an increment it is refreshed every \(n_{NR}\) iterations, but not after the first one. The residual is always evaluated exactly, so the converged solution does not change; only the number of iterations can increase. After onset, \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\) is evaluated at the start of every increment.

Why Newton-Raphson fails after the peak. The residual is continuous but only piecewise smooth. Its derivative jumps whenever an integration point crosses \(\Delta_o\) (from \(k_o\) to the negative softening slope \(-k_o \Delta_o/(\Delta_f - \Delta_o)\)), crosses \(\Delta_f\) (from the softening slope to zero), switches between loading (\(\chi = 1\)) and unloading (\(\hat d \le d^h\)), or changes sign and enters or leaves the region zeroed by the correction of Eq. (3). The consistent tangent of Eq. (10) is exact only on one side of each kink. Before the peak, few points cross a kink in one iteration and the quadratic convergence is not affected. After the peak, the Ritz functions are global, so a Newton correction moves the whole crack front at once, and many points can cross a kink in one iteration when the integration points are sparse compared with the cohesive zone. The iterations without the safeguards below may then enter an exact cycle at the first openings after the peak, with a period that is a multiple of the refresh interval \(n_{NR}\) of \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\), which no number of iterations can break. Two safeguards are added to the drivers. Neither changes the equations or the convergence criterion, so a converged state is the same solution as before, only the path to it changes.

Backtracking line search. With \(\delta \boldsymbol{c}\) the solution of \(\boldsymbol{K}\delta \boldsymbol{c} = -\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}_i)\), where \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is the iteration matrix, the new iterate is \(\boldsymbol{c}_{i+1} = \boldsymbol{c}_i + s\delta \boldsymbol{c}\), with the step length \(s\) taken as the first of \(s = 1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4}, \dots, 2^{-n_{ls}}\) that satisfies:

(16)#\[\left\| \boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}_i + s\delta \boldsymbol{c}) \right\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} \le \left(1 - 10^{-4} s\right) \left\| \boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}_i) \right\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \qquad \left\| \boldsymbol{v} \right\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} = \sqrt{\boldsymbol{v}^\top \mathrm{diag}(|\boldsymbol{d}|)^{-1} \boldsymbol{v}}\]

where the entries \(d_j\) of the diagonal scaling vector \(\boldsymbol{d}\), not to be confused with the damage \(d\), are the diagonal of \(\boldsymbol{K}\) at the start of the increment, the scaling of the criterion of Eq. (17), which balances the rows of the prescribed-displacement penalty against the others. The merit function is the residual norm and not an energy, because the damaged interface has no potential and \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is not symmetric. When \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is the tangent stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_T = \partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \boldsymbol{c}\), the exact Jacobian of the residual, \(\delta \boldsymbol{c}\) is a descent direction of this merit function for any positive weighting:

\[\left. \frac{d}{ds} \frac{1}{2} \left\| \boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}_i + s\delta \boldsymbol{c}) \right\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}^2 \right|_{s=0} = \boldsymbol{R}^\top \mathrm{diag}(|\boldsymbol{d}|)^{-1} \boldsymbol{K}_T \delta \boldsymbol{c} = - \boldsymbol{R}^\top \mathrm{diag}(|\boldsymbol{d}|)^{-1} \boldsymbol{R} < 0\]

so a small enough \(s\) always satisfies Eq. (16). Between refreshes of \(\boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G\) the iteration matrix is not the exact Jacobian and this is not guaranteed; if no trial satisfies Eq. (16), the trial with the smallest norm is taken, a trial whose residual is not finite counting as an infinite norm. Halving \(s\) halves the jump of the separations and thus reduces the number of kinks crossed per iteration. Each trial costs one evaluation of the residual, which is cheap with the assembly by matrix products; the trial \(s = 1\) is the residual that the iteration needs anyway. The drivers use \(n_{ls} = 6\) (line_search_max).

Bisection of increments. If an increment from \(w_{p,n-1}\) to \(w_{p,n}\) does not converge within \(n_{max} = 50\) iterations (max_NR_iter), or diverges, that is, the ratio of Eq. (17) is not finite or exceeds \(10^3\) after any iteration, its iterates are discarded and the first half, from \(w_{p,n-1}\) to \((w_{p,n-1} + w_{p,n})/2\), is solved from the last converged state. This is applied recursively with a stack of pending targets: when a sub-increment converges, the driver tries again to reach the next pending target, and when it fails, the remaining interval is halved again, down to \(2^{-6}\) of the original increment (max_bisections), after which the analysis is aborted. Discarding the iterates is consistent because the damage history \(d^h\) is only updated with converged states. Every converged sub-increment updates \(d^h\) and the states used by the predictor, so a bisected increment follows a finer loading path, which matters for an irreversible law; the results are stored only at the prescribed displacements of the original list. The list of prescribed displacements itself does not change: sub-increments are only created when an increment fails.

Effect of the consistent tangent and of the safeguards. With the consistent tangent, every opening of a DCB discretised by the rules of Discretisation of the cohesive domain converges in a few iterations, without line search or bisection. With the secant stiffness as the only tangent of the interface, the number of iterations grows as the process zone develops and the iterations after the peak converge only linearly. The line search breaks the cycles of the coarse integration rules, and the bisection is the safeguard that completes their analyses.

Convergence criterion. The prescribed displacement is imposed with \(k_w = 10^6\), such that \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\boldsymbol{c}\) are of the order of \(10^6\)–\(10^7\), while the physical forces are of the order of \(10^2\) N. The denominator of Eq. 5.35 of the thesis, \(\max(F(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}), F(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}))\), is dominated by the penalty terms, and the tolerance \(10^{-4}\) is then not a relative tolerance on the physical forces. The drivers use:

(17)#\[\frac{F(\boldsymbol{R})}{\max\left( F(\boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\boldsymbol{c}), F(\boldsymbol{K}^{pen}_{PD}\boldsymbol{c} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}) \right)} < \varepsilon\]

where \(F(\cdot) = \|\cdot\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\), the first term of the denominator is the internal force vector of the panels and of the connections, and the second term is the reaction of the prescribed displacement. The large \(k_w\) also degrades the accuracy of the first solution of each increment, whose ratio of Eq. (17) can be close to 1. An increment is therefore declared divergent only when the ratio is not finite or exceeds \(10^3\), instead of when it reaches 1. A divergent increment does not abort the analysis: it is bisected as described above, and the analysis is aborted only when a sub-increment of \(2^{-6}\) of the original increment (max_bisections) still does not converge.

Discretisation of the cohesive domain#

The cohesive domain needs a Ritz basis and an integration rule that resolve two lengths. Ahead of the process zone the opening \(w^t - w^b\) of two arms connected by an elastic foundation of stiffness \(k_o\) decays as \(e^{-\lambda x}\), the solution of beams on an elastic foundation [kanninen1973AugmentedDCB], with the rate:

(18)#\[\lambda = \left( \frac{k_o}{2 D_{11}} \right)^{1/4}\]

and the cohesive zone, where the traction softens, has the length estimated with Rice’s model (Turon et al. 2007 [turon2007MeshSize]):

(19)#\[l_{cz} = 0.88\,\frac{E_2 G_{Ic}}{\tau_o^2}\]

The Ritz basis of the cohesive domain, \(m_{tsl}\) terms along \(x\), sets the shape of the response after the peak. With too few terms per decay length the basis cannot follow the process zone continuously, like a cohesive finite element mesh that is coarse compared with the cohesive zone [alfano2001Interface] [turon2007MeshSize]: the elastic energy builds up while the crack front is held and is released when a group of integration points fails at once, a discrete snap-back that a displacement-controlled analysis crosses as a sudden load drop, and the load-displacement curve becomes a sawtooth. More terms refine the displacement field and remove the sawtooth. The Gauss-Legendre points along \(x\), \(n_x\), set the robustness and a floor of the error: when the crack front crosses several integration points in one increment, the iterations increase and increments need bisection, but the integration points refine the damage field without changing the shape of the curve. The terms along \(y\), the Gauss points along \(y\) and the terms of the other domains matter little for a specimen that is uniform across the width. The cohesive domain must contain the crack growth, the process zone and the region ahead of it where the opening decays: when the compressive lobe ahead of the process zone reaches the perfect bond beyond the cohesive domain, the load hardly changes, but the integral of the traction no longer balances the reaction, see Load measured at the loaded edge.

A convergence study on a DCB of AS4D/PEKK-FC (Tijs et al. 2022 [tijs2022Interlaminar]) gives the rules:

(20)#\[m_{tsl} = 1.25\,\lambda L_{tsl}, \qquad n_x = 10\,\frac{L_{tsl}}{l_{cz}}, \qquad L_{tsl} \geq \Delta a + 3 l_{cz} + \frac{8}{\lambda}\]

with \(\Delta a\) the crack growth up to the last opening, \(m_{tsl} \leq 25\), \(n_x \geq 4 m_{tsl}\) rounded up to a multiple of 10, \(n_{tsl} = 10\), \(n_y = 30\) and \(m = n = 8\) in the other domains. The margin \(8/\lambda\) lets the opening decay to below \(e^{-8}\) of its value at the end of the process zone. The Bardell functions go up to 30 terms, so a cohesive domain longer than about \(30/\lambda\) is not resolved; a lower \(k_o\) gives a longer decay length \(1/\lambda\) and fewer terms. The rules are implemented in tsl_resolution(), tsl_margin() and tsl_discretization(), and MultiDomain.calc_k_dmg() prints a warning, the first time a point is damaged, when \(m_{tsl} < 1.0\,\lambda L_{tsl}\) or \(n_x < 7\,L_{tsl}/l_{cz}\). The Gauss-Legendre points and weights are computed by scipy.special.roots_legendre(), without an upper limit for \(n_x\).

The model has been compared with the mode I DCB results of Alfano and Crisfield (2001) [alfano2001Interface], Camanho et al. (2003) [camanho2003Delamination], Turon et al. (2007) [turon2007MeshSize], Krueger (2008) [krueger2008DCB], Tijs et al. (2022) [tijs2022Interlaminar], Leciñana et al. (2023) [lecinana2023Fatigue] and Tijs (2023) [tijs2023PhD], and with the mixed-mode benchmarks of Krueger (2012) [krueger2012MMB], which 'SB_TSL' cannot represent, since its damage is driven by the normal separation only. For the unidirectional specimens the peak loads are within 2.6% of the corrected beam theory or of a cohesive finite element model with the same law, while the initial compliance is 15-21% lower than that of the corrected beam theory, because the CLPT arms have no transverse shear deformation.

Limits of validity#

The following limits come from the model itself, not from its discretisation:

  • Bilinear law driven by the normal separation. The law is bilinear, calc_kw_tsl() accepts no other tsl_type, and the damage of Eq. (2) is computed from the corrected normal separation \(\bar\Delta_w\) only; the tangential separations are degraded by the same damage but do not drive it. Mixed-mode delamination and laws with more than one softening branch, such as those that represent an R-curve from fibre bridging, are not represented.

  • No interpenetration stiffness. The stiffness is \(k_o (1 - d)\) for separations of either sign. The interpenetration stiffness of Eq. 5.12 of the thesis is not implemented, so a fully damaged point offers no resistance when the faces of the crack close. Under an increasing opening the damaged points of a DCB have a positive separation and this does not matter; it does when the faces of a damaged region come into contact.

  • One cohesive interface per assembly. The damage history \(d^h\) is stored once per assembly, in MultiDomain.dmg_index, and is read by every 'SB_TSL' connection, which restricts an assembly to one 'SB_TSL' connection.

  • One crack front along \(-x\). The correction of the separation, Eq. (3), assumes in each row \(y =\) cte a single crack front that advances along \(-x\).

  • Classical laminated plate theory. 'SB_TSL' is available only for the models of the classical laminated plate theory; the models based on shear deformation theories raise a NotImplementedError. The arms have no transverse shear deformation, so the compliance of arms with a low transverse shear stiffness is underestimated, while the peak loads, controlled by the fracture toughness, are affected much less.

  • Matching domains. The two panels of the connection must have the same dimensions \(a\), \(b\), since the separations are evaluated at the integration points of the domain of the top panel.
