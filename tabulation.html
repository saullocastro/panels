Approximation functions tabulated at the integration points#

The numerically integrated matrices and vectors of panels are sums over the integration points of products of the approximation functions of the Ritz method. This page describes how the kernels evaluate these functions: before version 0.13.0 they were evaluated again in every loop over the terms of the matrix, the innermost loop included; they are now evaluated once per integration point and term, in tables. The results are the same, bit for bit, and the assembly is several times faster.

Integrands#

Each field of a panel is approximated with the products of the hierarchical functions of Bardell, see Bardell’s Hierarchical Functions, along \(\xi\) and \(\eta\):

\[u(\xi, \eta) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} c_{u, ij}\, f_i(\xi)\, g_j(\eta)\]

where \(f_i\) and \(g_j\) depend on the boundary conditions of the field, i.e. the flags x1u, x1ur, x2u, x2ur along \(x\) and y1u, y1ur, y2u, y2ur along \(y\). The entry of a stiffness matrix between the terms \((i, j)\) and \((k, l)\) is the sum over the \(n_x \times n_y\) Gauss-Legendre points

\[K_{(ij)(kl)} = \sum_{p=1}^{n_x} \sum_{q=1}^{n_y} w_p w_q\, \mathcal{F}\left(f_i(\xi_p), f_k(\xi_p), g_j(\eta_q), g_l(\eta_q), \ldots\right)\]

where \(\mathcal{F}\) combines the functions and their derivatives \(f'\), \(f''\), \(g'\), \(g''\) of each field with the constitutive matrix and, for the non-linear matrices, with the displacement gradients at the point.

Previous implementation: functions in the innermost loop#

The kernels had the structure below, e.g. fkC_num of plate_clpt_donnell_num.pyx, with seven functions per side:

for ptx in range(nx):
    for pty in range(ny):
        xi, eta, weight = ...
        for i in range(m):
            fAu = f(i, xi, x1u, x1ur, x2u, x2ur)
            ...                                   # 7 functions of i
            for k in range(m):
                fBu = f(k, xi, x1u, x1ur, x2u, x2ur)
                ...                               # 7 functions of k
                for j in range(n):
                    gAu = f(j, eta, y1u, y1ur, y2u, y2ur)
                    ...                           # 7 functions of j
                    for l in range(n):
                        gBu = f(l, eta, y1u, y1ur, y2u, y2ur)
                        ...                       # 7 functions of l
                        kCv[c] += weight*(...)    # the entries of (ij)(kl)

The value of \(g_l(\eta_q)\) does not depend on \(i\), \(k\) or \(j\), but it was computed again for each of them: per integration point, \(7 m^2 n^2\) evaluations in the innermost loop, \(7 m^2 n\) in the loop over \(j\), and so on, for only \(7 (m + n)\) distinct values. Each evaluation is a polynomial of degree up to 29 in \(\xi\), selected by the index of the term, see panels/core/src/bardell_functions.cpp, which costs much more than the arithmetic of the entry itself. For \(m = n = 12\) this is about 10^5 evaluations per integration point, against 168 distinct values.

The internal force vector, calc_fint, has three loops over the \(m n\) terms per integration point (the rotations, the strains, the forces), with the functions evaluated again in each; the connections between panels and the stiffeners have the same nested structure, along a line or over an area.

Current implementation: tables per integration point#

The functions are evaluated once per integration point and term, at the start of the loop over the integration points, in a table along \(x\) and another along \(y\), and the loops over the terms read them:

ftab = np.zeros((7, m), dtype=DOUBLE)
gtab = np.zeros((7, n), dtype=DOUBLE)
with nogil:
    for ptx in range(nx):
        for pty in range(ny):
            xi, eta, weight = ...
            # approximation functions evaluated once
            for i in range(m):
                ftab[0, i] = f(i, xi, x1u, x1ur, x2u, x2ur)
                ...                               # one row per function
            for j in range(n):
                gtab[0, j] = f(j, eta, y1u, y1ur, y2u, y2ur)
                ...
            for i in range(m):
                fAu = ftab[0, i]
                ...
                for k in range(m):
                    fBu = ftab[0, k]
                    ...
                    for j in range(n):
                        gAu = gtab[0, j]
                        ...
                        for l in range(n):
                            gBu = gtab[0, l]
                            ...
                            kCv[c] += weight*(...)

Per integration point the number of evaluations falls from \(O(m^2 n^2)\) to \(O(m + n)\), with tables of a few kilobytes; the loops over the pairs of terms keep only the arithmetic of the entries, which is unchanged. The functions read from the tables are the same numbers, combined in the same order, such that the matrices and vectors are identical, bit for bit, to those of the previous implementation.

The kernels that integrate along a line, e.g. the connections at a constant \(\xi\) or the prescribed displacements, evaluate the functions across the line at a single coordinate: their tables are filled once, before the outermost loop over the terms.

Kernels#

All the kernels that evaluate the approximation functions in their loops over the terms are tabulated:

  • the 9 geometrically non-linear models, panels/models/<model>_num.pyx: fkC_num, fkG_num, calc_fint, fkM_num, fkAx_num, fkAy_num, calc_fext_follower and fkCfollower_num;

  • the connections, panels/multidomain/connections: kCSSxcte, kCSSycte, kCBFxcte, kCBFycte (including the shear deformation theories), kCpd (prescribed displacements) and kCSB_dmg (skin-base connection with damage, the cohesive zone of the double cantilever beam, see Cohesive zone model of the multi-domain approach);

  • the blade stiffeners, panels/stiffener/models.

The analytical kernels, which use closed-form integrals of the products of the functions, e.g. fk0 or kCSB, are not affected. The kernel calc_kTv of the product of the tangent stiffness matrix by a vector, see Product of the tangent stiffness matrix by a vector, is written with the tables from the start.

Implementation#

The rewriting is done by panels.dev.tabulate, applied by the generators of the modules, theory/shells/fsdt_tsdt/write_pyx.py, theory/shells/cylshell_clpt_sanders/write_pyx.py and theory/shells/follower_pressure/write_pyx.py, and with python -m panels.dev.tabulate <file.pyx> ... to the modules written by hand. For each kernel it

  1. finds the calls NAME = f(IDX, COORD, x1F, x1Fr, x2F, x2Fr), with f, fp or fpp, the flags of the field F along \(x\) (or \(y\)) and an optional suffix of the panel, e.g. x1u1 for the panel 1 of a connection, and fails on a call in another form;

  2. groups them in one table per direction, panel and coordinate, with one row per function and as many columns as the loop over IDX;

  3. fills the tables right before the loops over the terms of the loop over the integration points, after checking that the coordinates are not modified afterwards, and replaces each call by the read of the table;

  4. declares and allocates the tables outside the nogil block.

A kernel already tabulated is left unchanged, such that applying it twice does nothing. The helper _terms_sdt of the connections of the shear deformation theories, whose flags come from an array, was tabulated by hand in the same way.

Verification#

  • The matrices and vectors of every tabulated kernel were compared, bit for bit, with those of the previous implementation, recording the output of each call while running 20 test files of the models, of the follower pressures, of the partial integration domains, of the aerodynamic matrices, of the connections, of the cohesive zone and of the stiffened panels: the 73946 calls of 92 kernels gave identical outputs.

  • tests/test_tabulate.py checks the rewriting and that no kernel of the package evaluates the approximation functions in its loops any more.

  • The tests of the package, among which the consistency of the tangent stiffness matrix with the internal force vector, tests/tests_shell/test_tangent_consistency.py, are unchanged and pass.
