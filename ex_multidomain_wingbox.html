Wing-box modeled as a multi-domain assembly#

create_wingbox() builds the rectangular, untapered wing-box of Elalfy, De Breuker and Castro, case High-AR-t5 (their Tables 1 and 2, p. 7), with skins, spars, ribs and optionally blade stringers, all of them plate domains, 'plate_clpt_donnell', connected with penalty stiffnesses, see panels.multidomain.wingbox. The wing-box is clamped at the root and loaded by a vertical force at the tip. The ribs are connected to the skins with 'BFxcte' and to the spars with 'BFxycte' (rib_spar=True, the default); the stringers pass through the ribs without a connection.

wingbox_tip_force_lb() runs the linear buckling analysis: the pre-buckling state is the linear static solution, solved with structsolve.solve, which removes the null rows and columns of the clamped root, and gives the geometric stiffness matrix of MultiDomain.calc_kG(). Without stringers, with 45 domains, the first buckling mode is a local buckling of the upper skin of the root bay. The first buckling load of their Abaqus model is 173.11 N (Table 3, p. 8). With the connections imposed exactly, conn_method='null-space', the load converges from above to 176.4 N, 1.9% above Abaqus, whereas with the default penalty connections it converges to 161.9 N, 6.5% below: the default penalty constants of calc_kt_kr() leave some relative motion at the joints. The 172.78 N of the default model, penalty connections with 6264 Ritz constants, close to Abaqus, is therefore a coincidence of the number of terms and the penalty constants.

The defaults of create_wingbox() are the first row. The test uses the third one, with the null-space method:

@pytest.mark.slow
def test_wingbox_tip_force_lb():
    # case High-AR-t5 without stringers, 9 bays, 45 domains, connections
    # imposed with the null-space method, skins of 12 x 12 terms, spars of
    # 12 x 7 and ribs of 6 x 8, 13608 Ritz constants, 1 N at the tip
    terms = dict(skin=(12, 12), spar=(12, 7), rib=(6, 8))
    md, c, eigvals, eigvecs = wingbox_tip_force_lb(num_eigvalues=4,
            conn_method='null-space', terms=terms)
    Pcr = eigvals[0]
    print('first buckling tip force: %.2f N, Abaqus %.2f N, error %.2f%%'
          % (Pcr, ABAQUS_HIGH_AR_T5, 100*(Pcr/ABAQUS_HIGH_AR_T5 - 1)))
    # 176.44 N, converged to 0.03% (176.47 N with skins of 16 x 16 terms),
    # 1.9% above Abaqus
    assert np.isclose(Pcr, 176.44, rtol=1e-3)
    assert abs(Pcr/ABAQUS_HIGH_AR_T5 - 1) < 0.03
    root_top = md.panels[0]
    v = abs(eigvecs[:, 0])
    assert v.argmax() in range(root_top.col_start, root_top.col_end)

The blade stringers are added with nstr, the number of stringers per skin, which also splits the skins into nstr + 1 strips:

from panels.multidomain import wingbox_tip_force_lb

md, c, eigvals, eigvecs = wingbox_tip_force_lb(nstr=4, hs=3.e-3,
                                               ts=1.5e-3)

With 4 stringers per skin, hs = 3 mm and ts = 1.5 mm, 225 domains and 19872 Ritz constants, the default terms and penalty connections, the first buckling load is 708.9 N (43 s with one BLAS thread), a buckling of the upper skin and of its stringers in the two bays next to the root, with a global bending part; no reference value is available for this configuration, which is not one of the paper.
