Plates and cylindrical shells#

A Shell is one rectangular domain of a plate, \(0 \le x \le a\), \(0 \le y \le b\), or of a cylindrical shell of radius \(r\), with \(y\) the arc length along the circumference. It holds the inputs of the model and calculates the structural matrices and force vectors, which are solved with structsolve. The same class is the building block of the assemblies MultiDomain and StiffPanelBay. The package panels gathers these classes; this page states the conventions shared by all of them, the kinematics of the models and how to choose one.

Conventions#

Coordinates. \(x\) runs along the edge of length \(a\) and, for a cylinder, along its axis; \(y\) along the edge of length \(b\), the arc length \(y = r \theta\) for a cylinder; \(z\) is normal to the reference surface, \(z = 0\), and \(w\) is the displacement along \(z\), positive outwards for a cylinder. The shallow shell assumption \(r + z \approx r\) is adopted, and \(1/r = 0\) for the plates. The laminate spans \(d - h/2 \le z \le d + h/2\), \(d\) being the offset offset, \(d = 0\) when the reference surface is the mid-surface, the convention of composites.laminated_plate(). The natural coordinates of the approximation functions are \(\xi = 2x/a - 1\) and \(\eta = 2y/b - 1\).

Ritz constants. Each field \(F\) of \(u, v, w\) (and \(\phi_x, \phi_y\) for the shear deformation theories), of index \(k\), is the series

(1)#\[F(x, y) = \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} \sum_{i = 0}^{m - 1} c_{N_d (j m + i) + k} \, P_i^F(\xi) \, P_j^F(\eta)\]

with the \(N_d = 3\) or 5 degrees of freedom per term in the order \(u, v, w, \phi_x, \phi_y\), and Bardell’s functions \(P_i^F\), \(P_j^F\) with the boundary flags of \(F\) along \(x\) and \(y\), see Bardell’s hierarchical functions. The vector \(\boldsymbol{c}\) has the size \(N_d \, m \, n\), Shell.get_size(), and every matrix and vector of a Shell has its rows and columns in this order. In an assembly, the constants of each domain start at its row_start, see Multi-domain assemblies.

Generalized strains and stresses. The constitutive matrix Shell.ABD relates the generalized stresses Nxx, Nyy, Nxy, Mxx, Myy, Mxy (and Qy, Qx) to the generalized strains exx, eyy, gxy, kxx, kyy, kxy (and gyz, gxz), in this order, with the higher-order terms of the TSDT appended, see fsdt_tsdt_field.strain_names(). The changes of curvature carry the sign \(\kappa_{xx} = -w_{,xx}\), and the engineering shear strains are used.

Units. No unit system is assumed: lengths, forces and densities must be given in one consistent set of units, in which the results are returned.

Kinematics of the models#

The models of panels.models, selected with Shell.model, differ in the displacement field through the thickness and in the non-linear terms of the strains. The integrands of their matrices are derived symbolically from these kinematics and coded in the modules of panels.models, see Kernels of the structural matrices.

Classical laminated plate theory (CLPT), 3 degrees of freedom per term, \(u(z) = u - z w_{,x}\), \(v(z) = v - z w_{,y}\) for the Donnell kinematics. With \(s = 0\) for the Donnell kinematics (the von Kármán strains for the plates) and \(s = 1\) for the Sanders-Koiter kinematics [sanders1963Nonlinear], and the rotations of the normal \(\beta_x = -w_{,x}\) and \(\beta_y = -w_{,y} + s v/r\), the strains are

(2)#\[\begin{split}\varepsilon_{xx} &= u_{,x} + \frac{1}{2} \beta_x^2, \qquad \varepsilon_{yy} = v_{,y} + \frac{w}{r} + \frac{1}{2} \beta_y^2, \qquad \gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x} + \beta_x \beta_y \\ \kappa_{xx} &= -w_{,xx}, \qquad \kappa_{yy} = -w_{,yy} + s \frac{v_{,y}}{r}, \qquad \kappa_{xy} = -2 w_{,xy} + \frac{s}{r} \left( \frac{3}{2} v_{,x} - \frac{1}{2} u_{,y} \right)\end{split}\]

the changes of curvature of the Sanders-Koiter theory being those written by Amabili [amabili2008Nonlinear], Sec. 1.3.8. With \(s = 1\) all the strains vanish for any small rigid-body motion, which matters for long shells and for deformations of long wavelength, e.g. low circumferential wave numbers. The non-linear terms omit the rotation about the normal, \(\frac{1}{2}(v_{,x} - u_{,y})\), a simplification of the Sanders-Koiter theory for small strains and moderately small rotations, which keeps the geometric stiffness of an axial load equal to that of the Donnell kinematics, see panels.models. The plate models 'plate_clpt_gl' and 'plate_clpt_tl' replace the membrane strains by the Green-Lagrange strains, and the latter also the changes of curvature by those of the deformed normal, see Large rotations: plates with Green-Lagrange membrane strains and Large rotations: total-Lagrangian Kirchhoff-Love plates.

The cylinder 'cylshell_clpt_gl' keeps the changes of curvature of the Sanders-Koiter kinematics and replaces the membrane strains by the complete Green-Lagrange strains \(\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{F}^\top \boldsymbol{F} - \boldsymbol{I})\) of the mid-surface, with the deformation gradient in the local basis

(3)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{F} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 + u_{,x} & u_{,y} \\ v_{,x} & 1 + v_{,y} + w/r \\ w_{,x} & w_{,y} - v/r \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

the strains of the Flügge-Lur’e-Byrne non-linear theory as given by Amabili [amabili2008Nonlinear], Sec. 1.3.6. They vanish for any rigid motion and contain the rotation about the normal: an axial load \(N_{xx}\) has the geometric stiffness \(N_{xx} (u_{,x}^2 + v_{,x}^2 + w_{,x}^2)\), instead of \(N_{xx} w_{,x}^2\) with Eq. (2). The difference matters when \(v_{,x}\) is of the order of \(w_{,x}\): when a long cylinder bends as a beam, \(w = W(x) \cos\theta\), \(v = -W(x) \sin\theta\), the work of the axial load on \(v_{,x}^2\) equals that on \(w_{,x}^2\), and the Sanders-Koiter kinematics give about twice the Euler load of a pinned tube. For shell-like modes the difference decreases as the circumferential waves shorten: for axially compressed panels with \(R/h = 100\) and \(L/R = 2\) supported by shear diaphragms, the Green-Lagrange strains give a buckling load 4.2 % below that of the Sanders-Koiter kinematics for a panel of 180 degrees and 1.0 % below for a panel of 40 degrees. Linear analyses, static and natural frequencies, are those of 'cylshell_clpt_sanders'.

The cylinder 'cylshell_clpt_tl' is the total-Lagrangian Kirchhoff-Love counterpart of 'plate_clpt_tl': the membrane strains of 'cylshell_clpt_gl' and changes of curvature measured with the normal of the deformed mid-surface \(\boldsymbol{X}\). With its area vector \(\boldsymbol{a} = \boldsymbol{X}_{,x} \times \boldsymbol{X}_{,y}\), whose length is the area stretch \(1 + \varepsilon_{xx} + \varepsilon_{yy} + \ldots\):

(4)#\[\kappa_{xx} = -\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{X}_{,xx} \qquad \kappa_{yy} = -\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{X}_{,yy} - \frac{1}{r} - \frac{\varepsilon_{xx} + 2 \varepsilon_{yy}}{r} \qquad \kappa_{xy} = -2 \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{X}_{,xy} - \frac{\gamma_{xy}}{2 r}\]

where the second derivatives of the position include those of the local basis, e.g. the component of \(\boldsymbol{X}_{,yy}\) along the normal is \(w_{,yy} - 2 v_{,y}/r - w/r^2 - 1/r\). Linearized, \(-\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{X}_{,\alpha\beta} + (1 + \varepsilon_{xx} + \varepsilon_{yy}) b^0_{\alpha\beta}\), with \(b^0_{yy} = -1/r\) the only non-zero component of the second fundamental form \(b_{\alpha\beta} = \boldsymbol{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{X}_{,\alpha\beta}\) of the undeformed cylinder, is the change of the second fundamental form \(-(b_{\alpha\beta} - b^0_{\alpha\beta})\) [vanderheijden2008Koiter], Sec. 3.16, whose linear part differs from Sanders’ by \(\varepsilon_{yy}/r\) in \(\kappa_{yy}\) and \(\gamma_{xy}/(2r)\) in \(\kappa_{xy}\). The terms \(-\varepsilon_{yy}/r\) and \(-\gamma_{xy}/(2r)\) remove them, so that the linear part is exactly that of the Sanders-Koiter kinematics of Eq. (2), Koiter’s linear changes of curvature [vanderheijden2008Koiter], Eq. (3.16.8), p. 183, and Eq. (3.17.3), p. 202; a change of the order of the membrane strain divided by the radius leaves the accuracy of the classical theory of thin shells unchanged [vanderheijden2008Koiter], p. 202. The derivation is given step by step in Large rotations: total-Lagrangian Kirchhoff-Love cylindrical shells. All six strains are invariant under any rigid rotation of any deformed state, whereas the linear changes of curvature of 'cylshell_clpt_gl' are exact for small rotations only: the difference matters where a part of the shell rotates through a large angle while it bends, e.g. a shallow panel under a point load that snaps through and inverts its curvature. The linear analyses and the linear buckling analyses with a membrane prebuckling state are those of 'cylshell_clpt_gl'. The curvatures are cubic in the displacements, and the exact integration takes \(3p + 1\) Gauss-Legendre points along each direction, against \(2p + 1\) for the other models.

First-order (FSDT) and third-order (TSDT) shear deformation theories, 5 degrees of freedom per term, the rotations \(\phi_x\) and \(\phi_y\) being independent fields:

(5)#\[\begin{split}u(z) &= u + z \phi_x - c_1 \left[(z - d)^3 + d^3\right] (\phi_x + w_{,x}) \\ v(z) &= v + z \left(\phi_y + s \frac{v}{r}\right) - c_1 \left[(z - d)^3 + d^3\right] (\phi_y + w_{,y}) \\ w(z) &= w\end{split}\]

with \(c_1 = 0\) for the FSDT and \(c_1 = 4/(3 h^2)\) for the TSDT of Reddy [reddy1984HigherOrder], whose transverse shear strains \((\phi_x + w_{,x})(1 - 4 (z - d)^2/h^2)\) and \((\phi_y + w_{,y})(1 - 4 (z - d)^2/h^2)\) vanish at both faces, such that the TSDT needs no shear correction. The FSDT uses a shear corrected transverse shear stiffness, by default that of Rohwer, see fsdt_shear_correction of Shell. The terms in \(s\) correct the cylinders with the Sanders-Koiter kinematics, as in the shell theory of Reddy and Liu [reddy1985Shells] with \(r + z \approx r\); the strains are those of panels.models.fsdt_tsdt_field, and the TSDT with an offset is described in Third-order shear deformation theory with a laminate offset.

Plates bent in their own plane. A blade stringer modelled as a plate strip normal to the skin, or the web of a stiffener, carries its share of the axial load as a membrane stress resultant \(N_{xx}\) of the plate, and in a general instability of the stiffened structure it bends in its own plane: the out-of-plane displacement of the skin at the joint is the in-plane displacement \(v\) of the blade. The geometric stiffness is the second variation of the work of the pre-buckling stresses on the quadratic part of the membrane strains,

\[\delta^2 U_G = \int N_{xx} \left( \delta u_{,x}^2 + \delta v_{,x}^2 + \delta w_{,x}^2 \right) dA\]

for a pre-buckling state \(N_{xx}\) alone,

with the Green-Lagrange strains of 'plate_clpt_gl', whereas the von Kármán strains of 'plate_clpt_donnell' keep only \(\delta w_{,x}^2\). A blade of the von Kármán plate model is therefore loaded without destabilizing its in-plane (for a stringer, radial) bending: the stringer behaves as a column whose load does no work on its lateral deflection, and the general instability load is overestimated, by a factor of two or more when the stringers carry about half of the load. In the smeared stiffener theories the total load of the skin and of the stringers enters the term \(\bar{N}_x w_{,x}^2\) of the skin [block1965Stiffened], which is what 'plate_clpt_gl' recovers for discrete blades. The two models have the same linear stiffness, mass and aerodynamic matrices, so that linear static and frequency analyses do not change, and the analytical geometric stiffness of a constant \(N_{xx}\) costs the same. Skins and plates that buckle out of their plane only, e.g. the skin between the stringers, are not affected.

The thin-plate limit of the FSDT and the TSDT is the CLPT, see Boundary conditions, at the cost of 5 instead of 3 degrees of freedom per term. The models with 5 degrees of freedom per term are supported by MultiDomain, but not by StiffPanelBay, whose stiffeners assume the 3 degrees of freedom of the CLPT, nor by the cohesive connection 'SB_TSL', see Cohesive zone model of the multi-domain approach.

Inputs#

Geometry and model. The dimensions a, b and the radius r (None for a plate). The attribute model selects the kinematics and the non-linear equations, see panels.models; when it is not given, a plate gets 'plate_clpt_donnell' and a cylinder 'cylshell_clpt_sanders'; plates with large rotations, e.g. the domains of a wing box, need 'plate_clpt_gl', see Large rotations: plates with Green-Lagrange membrane strains, or 'plate_clpt_tl' when they also bend through large rotations, see Large rotations: total-Lagrangian Kirchhoff-Love plates, whose exact integration by default costs about twice that of 'plate_clpt_gl', and half with nx = gauss_points(m), ny = gauss_points(n), not exact but enough in practice. The models based on the classical laminated plate theory (CLPT) have the 3 degrees of freedom \(u, v, w\) per term, those based on the shear deformation theories (FSDT, TSDT) the 5 degrees of freedom \(u, v, w, \phi_x, \phi_y\).

Laminate. The stacking sequence stack, the ply thickness plyt and the lamina properties laminaprop, or one value per ply with plyts and laminaprops, the density rho (or rhos) and the offset of the laminate offset build the laminate Shell.lam with composites.laminated_plate(), whose constitutive matrix Shell.ABD enters every kernel. A stiffness that varies over the domain is given instead by ABDnxny, one matrix per integration point. The transverse shear stiffness of the FSDT is controlled by fsdt_shear_correction, and the TSDT with an offset is described in Third-order shear deformation theory with a laminate offset.

Approximation. The numbers of terms m and n of the approximation functions along \(x\) and \(y\), at most 30, see Bardell’s hierarchical functions, and the numbers of integration points nx and ny of the numerically integrated matrices, by default max(2*m - 1, 7) and max(2*n - 1, 7) for the current m and n, the smallest numbers that integrate exactly the products of up to four approximation functions, see gauss_points(). The boundary conditions are the flags of the edges, see Boundary conditions.

Loads. Point, line and pressure loads and the constant stress resultants of a prestress, see Loads.

The constructor sets the defaults, then the given attributes, and builds the laminate when a and b are given; the calculation methods rebuild the laminate and the size from the current attributes, see Shell.

Matrices and vectors#

The linear matrices of a constant constitutive matrix over the whole domain are calculated in closed form; the others, and all of them when c, ABDnxny or a partial integration domain is given, are integrated numerically, see Bardell’s hierarchical functions. The non-linear quantities follow the Donnell or Sanders-Koiter kinematics of the model, with a tangent stiffness matrix that is the exact derivative of the internal force vector, see Tangent stiffness matrix and its product by a vector. The integration can be limited to a part of the domain with x1, x2, y1, y2, see Shell.integration_limits(), which is how the panels of a StiffPanelBay share one set of approximation functions.

Analyses#

The methods above have the signatures expected by the solvers of structsolve:

  • linear static: solve(kC, fext);

  • linear buckling: lb(kC, kG), with \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) of the constant stress resultants Nxx, Nyy, Nxy or of a static solution c;

  • frequency: freq(kC, kM), with an optional prestress \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\);

  • panel flutter: freq(kC + kG + kA, kM), see Panel flutter with the piston theory;

  • non-linear static: structsolve.Analysis with calc_fext, calc_fint, calc_kC and calc_kG, with the Newton-Raphson or the arc-length methods;

  • follower pressures, in all the analyses above, see Follower pressure loads: theory.

Results#

The fields at points or over a grid are calculated from the Ritz constants with Shell.uvw(), Shell.strain() and Shell.stress(), plotted with plot_shell(), and the shell with its results is saved with Shell.save(), see Field outputs, plots and files.
